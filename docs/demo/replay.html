Replay of a landmark market day

Anytime-valid volatility monitor, experiment E18 (pre-registered case studies), primary design [REAL]

This page replays, minute by minute, what the streaming volatility monitor (the e-detector) and the competing change-point methods saw on a day of a large, well-remembered market shock. The numbers come from a rerun of E18's primary design on market data [REAL]; the alarm lists were checked against E18's saved results when the page was built. This is one pre-registered case study, not evidence that the method is better: E18 has four events, no pooled test, and does not rank the methods.

What happened on this day [REAL]

    How to read the panels

    1. Clipped statistic
    Each bar is one minute (one bar of the Nifty session): Yt = min(ut2, τ2) / m0, where ut is the one-minute log return divided by a predictable intraday volatility profile, τ is 3 robust standard deviations of the calibration window, and m0 is the mean of min(u2, τ2) on that window. If volatility has not changed, Yt averages about 1. No bar can exceed B = τ2 / m0 (dotted line), so one jump adds only a bounded amount of evidence. Grey bars fall in a recalibration: the monitor is not betting, and they show the same quantity against the previous baseline.
    2. e-process
    The wealth of the increase side and of the decrease side, on a log scale. Each side is a Shiryaev-Roberts e-process: a sum, over every possible start time, of the wealth of a bettor who started then. The increase side bets that Yt averages above 1.25, the decrease side that it averages below 0.75 (the null band, shaded in panel 1). An alarm is raised when either side reaches 2A. Hatched: the monitor is recalibrating after an alarm; amber: the recalibration that covered the reference time T, when the monitor was blind.
    3. Alarms
    One row per method, with exactly the alarms that E18 recorded for this asset from 7 days before to 24 hours after T (results/e18_alarms.csv; the build stops if any differs). Filled triangles are up alarms (volatility rose), open triangles down alarms. A tinted span means the method was paused after its own alarm (750 bars or 1,440 minutes, as in E13 and E18), a hatched span on the e-detector's row that it was recalibrating.
    Caption
    The clock time at the cursor (IST for Nifty, UTC for crypto), the monitor's state, and each side's wealth against the threshold 2A (the bars show it on a log scale from 1 to 2A). Everything after the cursor is drawn faintly. Keys: space plays or pauses, the arrow keys step.

    Case studies, not evidence: with four events, and five coins that move together, E18 has no pooled statistic, no p-value, and does not rank the methods. The thresholds of the competitors were matched to the e-detector's alarm counts using the whole half-year (or two years) outside the event windows, which a user could not have done in real time; that favours the competitors.

    The monitor in brief

    Calibration
    On n returns (750 bars, two Nifty sessions; 1,440 minutes for crypto) set the robust scale s = 1.4826 × median |u|, the clip τ = 3s, and m0 = mean of min(u2, τ2).
    Increase side
    Mtup = (1/6) Σλ Σj≤t Πi=j..t (1 + λ (Yi/1.25 − 1)), with bets λ in {0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.35, 0.5}. If the conditional mean of Yi is at most 1.25, each product (the wealth of a bettor who starts at j with 1) is a nonnegative supermartingale, so a large Mup is evidence that the mean of Y has risen above 1.25.
    Decrease side
    The same with factors 1 + λ (1 − Yi/0.75), λ = f / (B/0.75 − 1), f in {0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.95}. Because Y is bounded by B, a fall in volatility can be detected at all: without a bound, no nonparametric e-test can detect a fall in the variance (docs/math.md, section 5.3).
    Alarm and guarantee
    Alarm when either side reaches 2A, with A = (steps per day) / 0.05: 7,500 per 375-bar Nifty session, 28,800 per 1,440-minute crypto day. If the clipped squared returns keep the baseline's conditional mean and the baseline is the true one, the chance of any false alarm within the first day after a calibration is at most m/A = 5% (Theorem B in docs/math.md). Here the baseline is estimated on the calibration window (plug-in), for which the bound is not proved, and real returns do not meet the conditions exactly: on real data the false alarm rate is measured, not guaranteed.
    After an alarm
    The monitor recalibrates on the next n returns and cannot alarm meanwhile. This blind period decided the crypto cases of E18.
    Competitors
    BOCPD (Bayesian online change point detection, Adams and MacKay 2007) for the variance of Gaussian returns, whose alarm statistic is the posterior log odds that the current run is shorter than w steps, signed by whether the short runs have the higher variance; run on the returns and on returns clipped at 3 robust standard deviations of the previous day; and a two-window variance-ratio scan that compares the realised variance of the last k returns with that of the k before (inspired by Bibinger, Jirak and Vetter 2017; not their test). Each pauses for n steps after its own alarm, and its up and down thresholds were set so that it raises as many up and as many down alarms as the e-detector on the same stream, counting only alarms outside the windows from 8 days before to 2 days after each event.
