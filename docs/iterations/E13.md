## Iteration 1. Getting the competitors right without the BJV paper text

**OBJECTIVE.** Implement the volatility jump test of Bibinger, Jirak and Vetter (2017, BJV) exactly as published, with equation numbers in the docstrings, and Bayesian online change-point detection (BOCPD) as in Adams and MacKay (2007, Algorithm 1).

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The paper text could not be read. `arxiv.org` (abstract and PDF), its `ar5iv` mirror, the Humboldt University repository (`edoc.hu-berlin.de`) and a talk PDF on `epfl.ch` were all blocked by the session's network policy (direct download returned "CONNECT tunnel failed, 403"; the fetch tool could not resolve the hosts). Four web searches returned only metadata and second-hand descriptions. A broader probe of other scholarly hosts was refused by the session's permission system and was not retried.

**HYPOTHESIS.** The structure of the test can be pinned down from verified secondary information, and its critical values can be checked by simulation, so a faithful implementation of the idea is possible even if the exact equations cannot be quoted.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** What web search verified: the test uses rescaled differences of local spot volatility estimates on adjacent windows of `k_n` observations; the main statistic uses non-overlapping blocks and a second one overlapping windows (reported as clearly more powerful); the null limit is of Gumbel type; the paper's simulations use `k_n = 125`; the paper is Annals of Statistics 45(4), 1542 to 1578 (2017), arXiv:1502.00043. On that basis `competitors.py` implements: standardised differences of (truncated) realised variance on adjacent windows, the maximum over non-overlapping blocks with the classical Gumbel constants for maxima of normal variables, the maximum over overlapping windows with the moving-sum (MOSUM) Gumbel constants, a truncated (jump-robust) version, and the argmax as change-point estimate. The MOSUM constants were copied from the source code of the CRAN package `mosum` (fetched from raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/mosum), which implements Eichinger and Kirch (2018). The docstrings say plainly that the published equation numbers were NOT checked. BOCPD follows Algorithm 1 of Adams and MacKay (the arXiv listing and title were verified by search) with a conjugate inverse gamma prior on the variance; a unit test checks it against an uncapped textbook recursion to 1e-12.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Unit tests (`tests/test_competitors.py`): brute-force checks of both BJV statistics with truncation and missing values, the standard-normal behaviour of a single comparison (mean, sd, correlation -1/2 between neighbours), the Student t form of the BOCPD predictive, the exact BOCPD recursion, and the alarm scan and count matching.

**RESULTS.** All checks pass. On 2000 Gaussian windows of 2000 steps, the nominal 5% Gumbel critical values give 2.2% (blocks, k = 50) and 0% (overlap, k = 50) rejections: the extreme value limits are conservative at these sizes, as is usual for maxima of Gaussian processes.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** Both competitors are implemented, tested and documented, with the gap in verification stated in the code.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The exact BJV statistic (its normalisation, whether the published version is a ratio or a difference standardised by a quarticity estimate, and the constants of its limit theorem) must be checked against the paper once `arxiv.org` is reachable. Every comparison in E13 also uses simulation-calibrated critical values, so the conclusions do not depend on the asymptotic constants.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Choose the tuning constants of the online competitors.

## Iteration 2. Pilot: tuning the competitors to give them their best shot

**OBJECTIVE.** Choose the BOCPD short-run window w and the BJV online window k before any reported run.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The alarm rule "posterior mass of runs shorter than w" has a blind spot: once the delay exceeds w, the posterior spreads over all run lengths since the change and the mass below w can stay under the threshold. A first sanity check with w = 60 detected only 77% of a 50% sd increase within 500 steps.

**HYPOTHESIS.** A larger w (with a larger run-length cap) removes most of the blind spot for small changes at little cost for large ones. For BJV online, a longer window k helps small changes and slows large ones.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Pilot script (scratchpad, seed 999, never reused): Gaussian paths, 2000-step calibration, thresholds set to 2% false alarms per 390-step day on 2000 null paths, then 300 paths per change size.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** BOCPD w in {60, 120, 250} (cap 256, 256, 512) and BJV online k in {30, 60, 120}; sd ratios 1.25, 1.5, 2, 0.8, 0.5 at step 500.

**RESULTS [SYNTHETIC, pilot].** Detection share within 2000 steps after the change (median delay in steps):

| | 1.25 | 1.5 | 2 | 0.8 | 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BOCPD w = 60 | 0.48 (243) | 0.80 (31) | 0.97 (12) | 0.56 (348) | 0.98 (22) |
| BOCPD w = 120 | 0.74 (97) | 0.97 (35) | 0.97 (12) | 0.85 (116) | 0.98 (23) |
| BOCPD w = 250 | 0.91 (112) | 0.98 (36) | 0.97 (13) | 0.92 (119) | 0.98 (24) |
| BJV k = 30 | 0.16 (638) | 0.21 (37) | 0.61 (23) | 0.12 (1050) | 0.68 (28) |
| BJV k = 60 | 0.19 (254) | 0.52 (52) | 0.96 (32) | 0.18 (184) | 0.97 (48) |
| BJV k = 120 | 0.37 (113) | 0.90 (79) | 0.96 (38) | 0.36 (113) | 0.98 (74) |

**IMPROVEMENTS.** Fixed for all reported runs: BOCPD w = 250 with cap 512 (best detection), BJV online k = 120 (best detection; the rolling BJV is a Shewhart-type chart and needs long windows for small changes).

**REMAINING ISSUES.** On clean Gaussian data BOCPD is already faster than the e-detector's E4 delays for large changes and decreases (for example 24 against 71 steps for a halving of the sd). The question is what happens off the Gaussian model.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Offline size under contamination.

## Iteration 3. First offline size run: two failures and one saturation bug

**OBJECTIVE.** Measure the size of every offline method under seven null scenarios, with critical values matched to 5% on clean Gaussian data.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (a) A smoke test showed the BOCPD statistic (the maximum posterior short-run probability) rounding to exactly 1.0 on many paths, so quantiles and "greater than" comparisons break. (b) In the first full run (kept as `e13a_offline_size_iter2.csv` in the scratchpad, not in results), the BJV statistics with the Gaussian normalisation rejected 37% to 53% of Student t5 windows, and the e-detector with its proven threshold (A = 1500 / 0.05) rejected 14% of clean Gaussian windows.

**HYPOTHESIS.** (a) Working with the log-odds of a short run removes the saturation. (b1) The BJV standardisation assumes Var(r²) = 2 σ⁴, true for locally Gaussian returns only; Student t5 returns have Var(r²) = 8 σ⁴, so the statistic is inflated by a factor of 2. Studentising with the sample variance of the squared returns should restore the size. (b2) The e-detector's excess comes from estimating its baseline on only 500 steps (the plug-in effect of E4d and review point M4), not from the betting; the confidence-sequence baseline of E8 should restore the guarantee.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `BOCPDVariance.short_log_odds` and `bocpd_paths(return_log_odds=True)`; all BOCPD thresholds are now on the log-odds scale. Added "BJV overlap, k=50, truncated, studentised" (our modification, `norm="empirical"`, not in the paper) and "e-detector, CS baseline" (nominal level from P(false alarm) ≤ δ + m / A with δ = 0.01) to the offline methods, and the studentised rolling BJV to the online methods.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Rerun of `offline-null` with the extended method list (same seeds).

**RESULTS.** See iteration 4.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** No saturation; two extra methods that test the two hypotheses directly.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** Writing the size table, pandas read the label "null" back as a missing value, so the first saved table looked empty. The label is now "no change" in the script, and the saved file was relabelled once with the same mapping (no number changed).

**NEXT ITERATION.** Report the rerun and move to online use.

## Iteration 4. Offline size and power at matched size

**OBJECTIVE.** Answer Q13a with the extended method list.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** None new; this iteration records the outcome of iteration 3's hypotheses.

**HYPOTHESIS.** As in iteration 3.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** None to the methods. `offline-null` (2000 + 7000 windows) then `offline-change` (18 cells of 300 windows).

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Seeds 13100 to 13117 (null) and 13200 to 13225 (changes).

**RESULTS [SYNTHETIC].** (b1) confirmed: the studentised BJV has size 1.7% under Student t5 (the Gaussian-normalised versions 37% to 53%) and 3.7% under jumps. (b2) confirmed: with the CS baseline the e-detector's nominal size is 0.0% in every i.i.d. scenario, against 11.4% to 18.6% for the plug-in. At matched size the e-detector (plug-in) has power 96% to 100% in every cell, BOCPD 79% to 100% on Gaussian data (but 94% to 98% size under jumps and t5), BJV with k = 50 only 6% to 8% for a 25% increase and 30% with k = 125. The CS baseline costs power for small changes under t5 (76% against 99% for sd × 1.25). Under ticks the CS version over-rejects at the matched threshold (11.3%), although its nominal size is 0%.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** A clean answer: in this design no competitor matches the e-detector on both size and power.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The offline design favours methods that know a clean calibration stretch (BOCPD and the e-detector) over BJV, which tests for a jump anywhere without a reference; stated in the report. The plug-in e-detector's nominal guarantee fails with 500 calibration steps by a factor of about 3, which confirms review point M4 in a new setting.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Online use.

## Iteration 5. Online false alarms and delays; a truncated BOCPD

**OBJECTIVE.** Answer Q13b and Q13c.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** First online run (kept in the scratchpad as `e13b_online_null_run1.csv`, `e13c_online_change_part*_run1.csv`): BOCPD, with its threshold matched to the e-detector on Gaussian data (1.0% false alarms per day), raised false alarms on 73% of days with jumps and 53% with Student t5, but on clean data it detected decreases three times faster than the e-detector (26 against 81 steps for a halving of the sd). The question a referee will ask: is the e-detector's advantage the betting, or just the truncation?

**HYPOTHESIS.** Clipping the returns at the e-detector's truncation level before BOCPD removes most of the jump problem and keeps most of the speed.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** "BOCPD on truncated returns" (clip at 3 × scaled MAD of the calibration window, our modification). Online null and change runs repeated with the same seeds; the numbers of all other methods were checked to be identical to the first run.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** 4000 Gaussian paths for threshold matching (seed 13300 and 13301), 1000 paths per null scenario, 200 paths per change cell (30 cells).

**RESULTS [SYNTHETIC].** Truncated BOCPD: false alarms per day 0.9% (Gaussian), 1.6% (jumps), 7.5% (Student t5), 2.3% (price noise), 1.0% (ticks); the e-detector 1.2% to 1.9% in the same columns. Under jumps it detects a halving of the sd in 25 steps (e-detector 81) and a 25% increase in 132 steps with 81% detected (e-detector 104 steps, 99%). Under t5 it raises a false alarm before the change on 8% to 12% of paths (e-detector 1% to 4%). The rolling BJV is robust once truncated and studentised but detects only 9% to 35% of 20% to 25% changes.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The comparison now separates the two ingredients: truncation fixes most of BOCPD's jump problem; betting adds the false alarm control under fat tails and better detection of small changes; BOCPD keeps a clear speed advantage for decreases.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** No robust likelihood BOCPD (for example Student t observations) was tried. The online "per day" match is estimated from about 60 false alarms, so competitor thresholds carry Monte Carlo error.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Real data.

## Iteration 6. Real data, rate-matched

**OBJECTIVE.** Answer Q13d: alarm rates and a validation that is matched on alarm counts (review point M3).

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** Real returns are not Gaussian, so a competitor threshold matched on Gaussian data gives a different alarm rate on real data, and validation scores are not comparable across alarm rates.

**HYPOTHESIS.** Matching each competitor's numbers of up and down alarms to the e-detector's (as E6b did for the ratio rule) makes the validation scores comparable.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Gaussian-matched thresholds for 1440-step days (A = 28,800, cal 1440) and 375-step sessions (A = 7500, cal 750) from 2000 simulated paths each. On real data: BOCPD with a predictable prior (robust variance of the previous day) and, for the truncated version, clipping at 3 times that robust sd; rolling BJV (k = 120, truncated, Gaussian-normalised and studentised); the same pause after each alarm as the e-detector's recalibration (1440 or 750 steps). Validation as in E6e: next-day robust volatility against an EWMA forecast that uses only past data. Thresholds matched by bisection to the e-detector's counts per coin.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Five Binance coins, 1 Jul to 31 Dec 2025 (184 days each); Nifty 50, 2024 to 2025 (494 regular sessions).

**RESULTS [REAL].** Crypto, matched to band 0.25 (627 e-detector alarms): after up alarms the next day was above the EWMA forecast in 78.5% of cases for the e-detector against 65.6% to 68.0% for the four competitors (p ≤ 0.001 each); after down alarms 80.5% against 58.7% to 71.7% (p ≤ 0.02). The e-detector scored highest in all ten coin and direction cells. Matched to band 0 the gap is similar (74.9% and 68.3% against 57.7% to 65.3% and 53.2% to 58.4%). Nifty: no significant difference (e-detector 81.0% and 82.6%, competitors 77.2% to 78.5% and 69.1% to 76.8%, p ≥ 0.065).

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The reviewer's point M3 now has a rate-matched answer against strong competitors, not only against a naive rule.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The e-detector's settings were chosen in E5 on Bitstamp BTC over the same half-year, so the Jul to Dec 2025 crypto result is not fully out of sample for it. Next iteration.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Repeat on an earlier half-year that no setting has seen.

## Iteration 7. Held-out half-year

**OBJECTIVE.** Check that the crypto result of iteration 6 is not an artefact of the period on which the e-detector's settings were chosen.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The e-detector's real-data settings (A, cal, c, band) were frozen in E5 on Bitstamp BTC in Jul to Dec 2025, the same half-year as the iteration 6 crypto run.

**HYPOTHESIS.** If the advantage comes from the method and not from tuning, it holds on an earlier half-year with every setting of every method left as it was.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** New command `real-crypto-h1-2025` in `experiments/e13_benchmarks.py`: the same five Binance coins, 1 Jan to 30 Jun 2025 (181 days), the same intraday profile code, the same Gaussian-matched thresholds and the same rate matching by bisection. No setting was changed after looking at these data. Pooled table now has three markets.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** One run with 2 processes. Outputs `results/e13d_real_crypto_h1_2025_{summary,random,matched_thresholds,events}.csv`.

**RESULTS [REAL].** Matched to band 0.25 (612 e-detector alarms): after up alarms 82.0% for the e-detector against 67.8% to 71.5% for the four competitors (p ≤ 0.002 each); after down alarms 69.7% against 56.7% to 67.0%, significant for three competitors (p ≤ 0.011) but not for BOCPD on truncated returns (67.0%, p = 0.47). Per coin the e-detector is highest in nine of ten cells and ties the truncated BOCPD in the tenth (DOGE down, 41 of 58 each). Matched to band 0: 74.8% and 66.3% against 61.8% to 65.0% (p ≤ 0.007) and 54.9% to 63.4% (truncated BOCPD p = 0.37). Random-time base rates 46.5% to 50.1%.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The up-alarm advantage replicates out of sample with a similar size (about 11 to 14 points). The down-alarm advantage over the truncated BOCPD does not: on held-out data the two are statistically indistinguishable, which is consistent with the synthetic finding that truncated BOCPD is the strongest competitor for decreases. Recorded as a competitor win in the report.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** Alarms are clustered in time, so the z-test p-values are optimistic. Nifty shows no significant difference in either direction.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Final audit: regression of all E13 numbers in the report against the CSVs, tests, dash check.
