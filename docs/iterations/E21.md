## Iteration 1. Literature check and design, fixed before any reported run

**OBJECTIVE.** Answer the referee's M5 (`docs/review/referee_report_paper.md`, section 4 items 2 and 14, major comment M5): the Gaussian likelihood-ratio, CUSUM and BOCPD competitors of E4 and E13 were calibrated on Gaussian data, BOCPD had a Gaussian observation model, the "BJV-type" test was reconstructed without the paper, and there was no classical robust detector on the same statistic and no econometric monitoring procedure.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** A Gaussian-calibrated competitor that fails under jumps or fat tails says little about what a distribution-free threshold buys. The fair questions are: (i) at the same false alarm rate on clean data, what does Ville's threshold cost in delay compared with a threshold calibrated for the true law; (ii) when the law is not Gaussian, does a threshold calibrated by simulation or by a bootstrap of the calibration window keep the rate, and what does the e-detector's threshold buy there; (iii) how do a robust BOCPD and an econometric monitoring procedure with a training sample compare.

**Literature check (web searches on 3 November 2026; arxiv.org, proceedings.neurips.cc, papers.neurips.cc and publisher full texts could not be opened from this machine, and no attempt was made to get around the block).**
* Knoblauch, Jewson and Damoulas (2018), "Doubly robust Bayesian inference for non-stationary streaming data with β-divergences", NeurIPS 31, pages 64 to 75 (proceedings listing, Monash and Turing listings, arXiv 1806.02261 in search results). From the listings: robust BOCPD by general Bayesian inference with β-divergences, robust for both the parameter and the change-point posterior, structural variational approximations, β chosen by minimising expected predictive loss online. The equations could not be read, so their exact construction (two β values, the variational family) could not be checked. Decision: implement the Student t version that the task allows, not a β-divergence version "of the type" whose form we could not check.
* Altamirano, Briol and Knoblauch (2023), "Robust and scalable Bayesian online changepoint detection", ICML (arXiv 2302.04759 in search results): a later robust BOCPD by diffusion score matching. Not implemented; cited only as context.
* Horváth, Kokoszka and Zhang (2006), "Monitoring constancy of variance in conditionally heteroskedastic time series", Econometric Theory 22(3), 373 to 402 (Cambridge Core and RePEc listings): sequential monitoring of a change in the unconditional variance of a conditionally heteroskedastic series, theory through a strong invariance principle for the squared observations, procedures adapted from monitoring in linear regression (Chu, Stinchcombe and White 1996; Horváth, Hušková, Kokoszka and Steinebach 2004). The detector and boundary could not be read in the paper itself.
* Horváth, Hušková, Kokoszka and Steinebach (2004), "Monitoring changes in linear models", Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference 126(1), 225 to 251 (search listings). Boundary verified through search excerpts of two later papers (Aue, Horváth, Kokoszka and Steinebach 2008, Test 17, 515 to 530; arXiv 1308.1237): g(m, k) = c m^{1/2} (1 + k/m) (k/(m + k))^γ, 0 ≤ γ < 1/2, with P(sup_k |Q(m, k)| / (σ g(m, k)) ≤ c) → P(sup_{0<t<1} |W(t)| / t^γ ≤ c).
* Chu, Stinchcombe and White (1996), "Monitoring structural change", Econometrica 64(5), 1045 to 1065 (Econometric Society and RePEc listings): fluctuation and CUSUM monitoring with asymptotically controlled size. Its boundary could not be read in the paper. The R package strucchange (version 1.6-0, `R/monitoring.R`, read from the CRAN mirror on GitHub) implements OLS-CUSUM monitoring with the boundary sqrt(x (x - 1) (c² + log(x / (x - 1)))), x = (m + k)/m, and c solving 2 (1 - Φ(c) + c φ(c)) = α; its documentation cites Leisch, Hornik and Kuan (2000) and Zeileis et al. (2005), not CSW directly, so we call it "the strucchange OLS-CUSUM boundary" and treat it as a sensitivity check.
* Bibinger, Jirak and Vetter (2017): still not readable (arxiv.org blocked). Item 4 renames the E13 test accordingly.

**HYPOTHESIS.** (1) At the e-detector's own false alarm rate on clean Gaussian data, its statistic with a calibrated threshold is the e-detector (same alarms up to the split between sides), so "the price of a distribution-free threshold" appears only against a threshold that spends the full 5% budget. (2) Thresholds calibrated on Gaussian data hold for the robust statistics X_t under jumps and ticks (the truncation does the work) but not under Student t3, where the truncated statistic has a different shape. (3) The bootstrap of the calibration window holds the rate for i.i.d. laws and fails under GARCH (it destroys the clustering). (4) A Student t BOCPD removes most of plain BOCPD's false alarms under jumps and fat tails. (5) The econometric CUSUM on raw squared returns fails under fat tails (its normalisation needs a fourth moment), and its boundary makes it slow for changes that come long after the training sample.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED (design, fixed now).**
* Statistic: X_t = min(r_t², τ²), τ = 3 × scaled MAD, plug-in m0 from the calibration window (as the e-detector), Y = X / m0.
* Classical rules on Y: Page's two-sided CUSUM with reference values from the Gaussian likelihood ratio of a variance change θ (k = θ log θ / (θ - 1) - 1 above 1, and its mirror below), "small" for sd × 1.25 and × 0.8 (k = 0.240 and 0.207) and "medium" for × 1.5 and × 0.67 (k = 0.460 and 0.348); and the e-detector's own Shiryaev-Roberts betting statistic (same 6 + 5 bets, decrease bets from each path's B = τ² / m0) with a calibrated threshold. Each side gets half of the target rate.
* Calibration (a): 20,000 clean Gaussian paths (seeds 21000 to 21009), calibration 2000, one 390-step day. Targets: the e-detector's own first-day rate on these paths ("matched"), and 5% (the e-detector's bound). Calibration (b): for each path, 1000 bootstrap replicates of its own calibration window: resample 2000 X values for m0*, then 390 X values as the stream, Y* = X* / m0*; per-side threshold at the 1 - α/2 quantile of the replicate maxima. τ is kept (X is already truncated). Bootstrap rules: SR betting and CUSUM small.
* Robust BOCPD: Student t with ν = 3 fixed (the heaviest Student law with a finite variance, so that the heaviest law of the design is inside the model), discrete prior on 65 variances from 0.04 to 25 (log step 0.1), the inverse gamma prior of E13 (α0 = 2, mean = calibration mean square) discretised on that grid, hazard 1 / A, warm start on the calibration window, w = 250, runs of at least w lumped (exact). Calibrated as E13 calibrates BOCPD: threshold on the short-run log-odds matched to the e-detector's rate on E13's 4000 Gaussian calibration paths (seeds 13300, 13301; 1.55%), and the rule p ≥ 0.5.
* HKZ-type monitoring: training sample = the calibration window (m = 2000), Z = r² (primary, the published statistic) and Z = X (robust variant), Bartlett long-run variance with bandwidth floor(m^{1/3}) = 12, boundary (1 + k/m)(k/(m + k))^γ with γ = 0.25 primary, γ = 0 and 0.45 and the strucchange boundary as sensitivity. Critical values: asymptotic (simulated sup_{0<t<1} |W(t)|/t^γ at 95%, 20,000 paths; strucchange's closed form) and Gaussian-matched (one critical value for |D|).
* Designs. Q21a: 8 laws (Gaussian, Student t3, t5, jumps, price noise, ticks, slow SV, GARCH 0.99), 4000 fresh paths each (seeds 21100 to 21107), first-day and two-day rates; bootstrap rules on the first 2000 (seeds 21150 to 21157). Q21b: E4 design (calibration 2000, change at step 500 of 3500), 6 laws × 6 sd ratios, 1000 paths per cell (seeds 21200 to 21235), bootstrap on the first 500 (seeds 21250 to 21285). Q21c: BOCPD-t on the exact E13 paths (Q13b and Q13c seeds) and 1000 fresh Student t3 paths (seed 21400); the cheap rules also on the E13 change paths. Q21d: E13's real-data design on the five coins in both half-years: restart rules (calibrate 1440, monitor, recalibrate after an alarm) rate-matched to the e-detector's up and down counts per series (bands 0 and 0.25), also at Gaussian-matched thresholds for 1440-minute days (E13's seeds 13500, 13501); BOCPD-t run continuously with E13's predictable prior and pause, w = 250, 720 and 1440; validation and tests as in E13 (next day against the EWMA forecast; four-hour outcome; z-test and week-clustered test against the e-detector).
* The e-detector reference in E21 uses per-path decrease bets, as the streaming monitor does (`restart_events` reproduces `VolatilityMonitor` alarm for alarm, unit test); on the E13 paths E13's own version (decrease bets common to a batch) is also run, to check that E13's numbers are reproduced.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Smoke runs on 4000 calibration paths and 300 null paths per law (scratch outputs, not reported). Unit tests (`tests/test_benchmarks_robust.py`): vectorised statistics against brute-force recursions, the restart loop against `VolatilityMonitor`, the lumped grid BOCPD against the unlumped one, the Gaussian grid BOCPD against the conjugate filter of `competitors.py` (log-odds within 1e-4), the Wiener simulation against the exact law of sup |W| (95% point 2.2414), the strucchange critical value (2.795).

**RESULTS.** Smoke runs only (not reported): the pipeline runs end to end. The first version of the Wiener simulation used a geometric grid only and gave 2.19 for the 95% point of sup |W| (exact 2.2414); a mixed grid (geometric below 0.01, 20,000 equal steps above) gives 2.250 on 20,000 paths, within Monte Carlo error.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** Design fixed and recorded before the reported runs.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** See the next iterations.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Reported runs.

## Iteration 2. Calibration runs, and a failure of the count matching under restarts

**OBJECTIVE.** Run the calibration steps and check, before the real-data runs, that the restart rules can be rate-matched to the e-detector.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** A timing run on BTC (Jul to Dec 2025, band 0.25; e-detector 72 up and 55 down alarms) showed that the coordinate bisection of `match_restart_counts` (iteration 1) can stop far from the targets: CUSUM small 78 up, CUSUM medium 76, HKZ-type on r² 87 up and 53 down, HKZ-type on X 97 up. A scan of the up threshold with the down threshold fixed shows why: under recalibration after every alarm, the counts are far from monotone (HKZ-type on r², up threshold 0.05 to 20: up counts 98, 91, 93, 84, 85, 100, 107, 106, ...; a small change of one alarm time moves every later calibration window), so a bisection finds one crossing of a jagged function, not a good match.

**HYPOTHESIS.** A local search after the bisection, and a second start from a two-dimensional grid when the local search stays more than two alarms away, will bring every restart rule within a few alarms of the targets.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** (1) `match_restart_counts` gained a local search (each threshold in turn scanned over 41 values h exp(s), |s| ≤ 0.5, the other fixed, minimising |up count - target| + |down count - target|, up to 6 rounds) and, if the error is still above 2, a second start at the best point of a 41 x 41 grid (15 x 15 for SR betting, whose single pass is 25 times slower) over h exp(s), |s| ≤ 2, around the bisection result, followed by the same local search; the better of the two results is kept. A unit test checks that the search never ends worse than the bisection. (2) Decided before any real-data run: the Gaussian-matched threshold of BOCPD-t for 1440-minute days is computed with the real-data variance grid (`GRID_REAL`), so that the same filter is calibrated and applied. (3) The queue was relaunched detached from the session (the first launch would have been stopped by the two-hour limit of a background task); the null run that was killed after one minute was restarted from scratch with the same seeds.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** `calib` (20,000 Gaussian paths, seeds 21000 to 21009; Wiener simulations seeds 21050 to 21052; open-end check seeds 21060, 21061, 21070), `e13-calib` (E13's seeds 13300, 13301), `e13-null` (E13's seeds 13310 to 13316 and seed 21400 for Student t3). Matching checks on BTC and DOGE, Jul to Dec 2025, both bands (scratch scripts, not reported).

**RESULTS.**
* The e-detector's first-day rate on the 20,000 Gaussian calibration paths is 1.305% (E13 measured 1.55% on its 4000 paths with decrease bets common to a batch; reproduced exactly by `e13-calib`). All Gaussian-calibrated thresholds hit their targets on the calibration set (`results/e21_thresholds.csv`). SR betting matched to 1.305% needs log thresholds 10.00 (up) and 9.13 (down) against Ville's log(2A) = 9.66: the e-detector's up side spends more than half of its rate, so the matched threshold is higher than 2A on the up side and lower on the down side.
* Asymptotic HKZ-type critical values at 5%: 2.234 (γ = 0; exact value of the law of sup |W| on [0, 1]: 2.241), 2.375 (γ = 0.25), 2.797 (γ = 0.45); strucchange boundary 2.795. Finite-sample check with m = 2000 and 20,000 monitored steps on 2000 Gaussian paths: 4.15%, 4.60%, 6.40% and 6.30%, against 3.84%, 4.28% and 4.60% for the limit restricted to the same range of t (`results/e21_hkz_open_end_check.csv`). For γ = 0.45 and the strucchange boundary the finite-m rate is above the limit by about three standard errors (0.55 points).
* BOCPD-t matched to E13's 1.55% needs a short-run log-odds of -0.805 (`results/e21_bocpd_thresholds.csv`).
* Matching error (|up count - target| + |down count - target|) on BTC, band 0.25, with the bisection alone: 6, 4, 17 and 25 alarms for CUSUM small, CUSUM medium, HKZ-type on r² and HKZ-type on X (0 for SR betting). The local search alone left errors of 5 to 29 in five of 16 coin, band and rule cases (BTC and DOGE, both bands; all five HKZ-type), and 0 or 1 in the other eleven; the final search (local, then the grid start) ended at 0 or 1 in all four cases on which it was tried, three of them among those five (DOGE band 0 HKZ-type on X: 29 to 1).

**IMPROVEMENTS.** On the cases tried, rate matching of the restart rules ends within about one alarm instead of up to 29; the real-data runs report the achieved counts for every case.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The chaotic dependence of restart counts on the thresholds is a property of the restart design, not of the search: two thresholds a few percent apart give different alarm sequences. It is reported with the real-data results (achieved counts are in `results/e21_real_*_matched_thresholds.csv`).

**NEXT ITERATION.** Synthetic runs (`null`, `change`, `e13-change`), then the real-data runs.

## Iteration 3. Synthetic runs (Q21a, Q21b, Q21c)

**OBJECTIVE.** Run the pre-registered synthetic designs and check the hypotheses of iteration 1.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** None new; this iteration only runs what was fixed.

**HYPOTHESIS.** As in iteration 1, (1) to (5).

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** None to the methods. The figure title of `e21_null_false_alarms.png` was corrected (it also shows bootstrap thresholds).

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** `null` (8 laws x 4000 paths, bootstrap rules on 2000; 4 to 8 minutes per law with two workers), `change 0|1` and `change-merge` (36 cells x 1000 paths, bootstrap on 500; about 70 s per cell), `e13-change 0|1` and `e13-change-merge` (30 cells x 200 paths of E13; about 30 s per cell). The E13 e-detector's alarms were reproduced exactly on the null and change paths (asserted in the script).

**RESULTS.** (`results/e21_null.csv`, `e21_change.csv`, `e21_e13_null.csv`, `e21_e13_change.csv`.)
* Hypothesis (1) holds: at the e-detector's Gaussian rate, SR betting with calibrated thresholds detects increases in 106, 41, 20 steps against 100, 39, 19 and decreases in 144, 92, 67 against 162, 102, 75 (E4 design, Gaussian).
* Hypothesis (2) is half right. Gaussian thresholds hold for jumps and ticks, but not for Student t: CUSUM on X matched to 1.3% reaches 5.0% to 8.2% under t3 and t5; SR betting at the 5% threshold reaches 6.9% and 7.5%. They also fail for the HKZ-type rule on raw r² under jumps (11.8%).
* Hypothesis (3) holds: bootstrap thresholds hold the rate for i.i.d. laws (1.4% to 2.2% against 1.3%; 4.9% to 6.3% against 5%, the largest under price noise, which is not i.i.d.), and fail under GARCH and slow SV like every other rule.
* Hypothesis (4) holds for false alarms (BOCPD-t under jumps 0.7% against 73.2% for BOCPD; t5 3.3% against 53.2%), but BOCPD-t loses power for small changes (56% and 77% detected at sd x 1.25 and x 0.8, Gaussian, against 98%), which was not anticipated.
* Hypothesis (5) holds: the HKZ-type rule on r² fails under jumps and Student t3 (false alarms 10% to 15% in the first day), and its delays are about twice the e-detector's; the reason is the size of its boundary (about c σ sqrt(m)), derived in the report.
* Not anticipated: BOCPD-t's Gaussian rate on E13's null paths is 0.5% (0.2% to 1.2%) against 1.55% on the calibration paths (E13's Gaussian BOCPD on the same paths: 1.0%). A diagnostic on the calibration seeds (scratch, not reported) gave 1.6% and 1.5% on the two halves and the same median and 90% quantile of the maximum log-odds as on the null paths; only the upper 1% tail differs, so this is read as sampling variation shared by the BOCPD variants on those paths.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The referee's questions (i) and (ii) now have measured answers on synthetic data.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** Real data (Q21d).

**NEXT ITERATION.** Real-data runs.

## Iteration 4. Real data (Q21d), report

**OBJECTIVE.** Run the five coins in E13's real-data design for every new rule, rate-matched to the e-detector, and write the report part.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The real-data job had not been run end to end; a failure late in a two-hour queue would have cost the BOCPD-t runs.

**HYPOTHESIS.** As in iteration 1; no hypothesis on real data was stated in advance beyond E13's design.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** None to the methods. Before the real runs, a smoke test (`real_job` on DOGE, both half-years, CUSUM small and the HKZ-type rule on r² only, BOCPD-t replaced by random numbers; scratch outputs, not reported) checked the pipeline end to end. It showed real outcomes for those two rules on DOGE; nothing was changed after it. The null figure was redrawn on a log scale (the first version used a symmetric-log axis that was hard to read). After the run, the report part of E13 was edited at the integrator's request to say over which alarms each share is computed (alarms with a complete next day; no number or CSV changed).

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** `real-calib` (E13's seeds 13500, 13501; the e-detector's 3.95% reproduced exactly), `real-bocpd 250|720|1440` (about 7, 20 and 36 minutes for both halves together; arrays cached outside the repository), `real h2`, `real h1` (3 to 7 minutes per coin; the e-detector's alarms reproduced exactly from E13's event files), `real-pooled`, `figures`.

**RESULTS.** (`results/e21_real_pooled.csv`, `e21_real_{h1,h2}_{alarms,summary,matched_thresholds}.csv`, `e21_real_thresholds.csv`.)
* Matched to the e-detector's alarm counts, SR betting and the two CUSUMs on X are never significantly less precise than the e-detector on the next-day outcome (24 comparisons; differences -5.0 to +3.0 points); SR betting is once significantly more precise (week-clustered p = 0.019, z-test p = 0.35).
* The HKZ-type rules are below the e-detector in 13 of 16 comparisons (6 significant); BOCPD-t in 20 of 24 (14 significant), with w = 250 the worst (7 of 8 significant) and w = 1440 the closest (3 of 8). BOCPD-t improves on E13's Gaussian BOCPD at w = 250 (Jul to Dec 2025, band 0.25: 69.0% and 70.7% against 67.8% and 61.7%).
* Matching error per series: 0 to 4 alarms for the restart rules, 0 to 9 for BOCPD-t (E13's bisection).
* Not anticipated, and the main real-data finding: the e-detector's advantage over BOCPD and the VR scan in E13 is shared by classical rules run on the same statistic in the same restart design. The report states this as the most likely reading, not as a proof: the rules differ from the e-detector only in the threshold (SR betting) or in the statistic's recursion (CUSUM).

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The referee's M5 has a complete answer: a classical detector on the same statistic with simulated and bootstrap thresholds, a robust BOCPD, and an econometric monitoring procedure, on simulated and real data, next to E13's results.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** (1) The β-divergence BOCPD and the exact HKZ and CSW procedures could not be checked against their papers. (2) The bootstrap is i.i.d.; a block bootstrap was not tried. (3) The real-data p-values are not adjusted for multiplicity (64 comparisons at matched rates; 21 below 0.05, about 3 expected by chance). (4) On real data the restart rules' alarm counts depend chaotically on the thresholds, so "rate-matched" means within a few alarms.

**NEXT ITERATION.** None planned for E21.
