# E7 iteration notes: Nifty 50 application

All numbers below come from the scripts `experiments/e07_nifty.py`, `experiments/e07c_india_vix.py` and `experiments/e07d_events.py`, saved under `results/e07*`. Data is [REAL] unless marked otherwise.

## Iteration 1. Data check before any monitor run

* **OBJECTIVE.** Make sure the Nifty minute returns are fit for a volatility monitor before running it.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The loader keeps 2,363 regular sessions (6 short special sessions dropped by `regular_sessions`). Runs of exact zero returns of 5 minutes or more never happen by chance in this data (natural runs are at most 3 minutes long), but there are 76 such runs: exchange outages (10 July 2017, 24 Feb 2021 with 238 frozen minutes), the two lower-circuit trading halts of 13 and 23 March 2020 (44 and 43 minutes), a stretch on 7 March 2022, and from 3 August 2026 a frozen index in the last 12 to 14 minutes of every session (62 runs). A web search tied the last one to the start of the NSE Closing Auction Session on 3 August 2026 (source in `data/events/nifty_events.csv`). A frozen price produces zero returns, which the monitor would read as evidence of very low volatility, and a calibration window with more than half zeros would give a MAD of zero (the coordinator warned that `VolatilityMonitor` then divides by zero).
* **HYPOTHESIS.** Treating frozen stretches as missing data, not as zero returns, removes these artefacts without touching real price moves.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `load_clean()` in `e07_nifty.py`: returns inside a zero run of 5 or more minutes, and the first return after the run (it spans the whole halt), are set to NaN. Sessions with more than 25% of returns flagged are dropped (10 July 2017 and 24 Feb 2021). The monitor is fed NaN for missing minutes (it skips them), never zeros. I did not edit `monitor.py`; the cleaning alone avoids the zero-MAD case.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Zero-run length table, zero share by year and by minute of day, NSE daily close files as an outside check (E7c).
* **RESULTS.** 2,361 sessions, 882,001 returns, 998 returns flagged (`results/e07a_frozen_runs.csv`). The NSE published daily high and low match the minute data within 0.01% on about 94% of days (`results/e07c_nse_crosscheck.csv`).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** No artificial "volatility collapse" alarms during halts, outages or the closing auction.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** This check looked at the whole sample, including the held-out years, but it uses only the data's own zero pattern, not any monitor output.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Deseasonalisation.

## Iteration 2. The intraday profile on a 375-bar session

* **OBJECTIVE.** Remove the predictable U-shape of Nifty volatility (the first minute after the open is about 2.8 to 3.1 times as volatile as a typical minute).
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** `avmonitor.data.intraday_scale` smooths the profile circularly across the clock, which is right for 24-hour crypto but wraps 15:23 to 15:29 into 09:16 to 09:22 on a session market. On the development years, after dividing by it, the 09:16 minute was still 1.84 times too volatile and the last five minutes 0.61 times (`results/e07b_deseason_check.csv`).
* **HYPOTHESIS.** A session-aware profile (no wrap-around, NaN-aware, edges truncated) fixes the open and the close.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `session_profile()` in `e07_nifty.py`: per-minute median of |r| over the previous 20 sessions, centred 5-minute moving average truncated at the open and the close, normalised to median 1. The package function was not changed (I do not own it).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** On 2017 to 2021 only: flatness of median |u| across minutes for smoothing windows 1, 5 and 15, and the within-session spread of log |u| for look-backs 20 and 60 sessions.
* **RESULTS.** Session profile, smoothing 5: 09:16 at 1.15 and last five minutes at 1.03 (against 1.84 and 0.61). Without smoothing the average shape is flattest (1.02) but each day's profile is noisy: the spread of log |u| was 1.091, worse than no deseasonalisation at all (1.074). Smoothing 5 gave 1.043, smoothing 15 gave 1.037, look-back 60 with smoothing 5 gave 1.034. The differences between the smoothed variants are below 1%, so I kept the settings fixed before the check (look-back 20, smoothing 5). Smoothing also blurs a one-minute volatility spike at 15:00 (median |u| 1.78 with smoothing 5); alarms do not cluster there (7 of 611 primary alarms fell in 15:00 to 15:04 against 8.2 expected).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** Opening-minute bias cut from 84% to 15%; closing bias from minus 39% to plus 3%.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** Single-minute spikes (09:16, 15:00, 15:29) are not captured by a smoothed profile. A profile that is smoothed except at known spike minutes would be better.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Run the monitor on the development period.

## Iteration 3. Q7a, daily IQ and the truncation bias

* **OBJECTIVE.** Compare the MAD truncation bias on Nifty and on crypto, and explain the difference.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The first run showed the plain kernel IQ on raw Nifty returns at 1.03 times MedRQ while plain RQ was at 1.23. The kernel average starts at minute h = 30, so the most volatile part of the session (the first half hour) gets partial weight. On raw returns the robust kernel number therefore mixes the truncation bias with this edge effect.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** Truncated RQ without a kernel isolates the truncation bias.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Added `TruncRQ_scaled_bc` and `TruncRQ_raw` (`avmonitor.robust.truncated_rq_mad`) and a back-of-envelope for how exact zeros lower the MAD (`results/e07a_zero_share_effect.csv`).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Daily IQ on raw and deseasonalised returns, Nifty (2,361 sessions) and Bitstamp BTC (184 days).
* **RESULTS.** Median ratio of truncated RQ (scaled MAD, corrected for Gaussian clipping) to MedRQ: Nifty 0.71 raw and 0.86 deseasonalised; BTC 0.33 raw and 0.40 deseasonalised.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** The kernel edge effect is now visible and separated from the truncation effect.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** MedRQ is a reference, not the truth.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Monitor.

## Iteration 4. Q7b, development period first, then held-out

* **OBJECTIVE.** Run the monitor with settings fixed from first principles and judge it on held-out data.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The E5 crypto script fed missing returns as zeros (`nan_to_num(u, nan=0.0)`). On Nifty every session starts with a missing return (the first bar has no intraday return), so zeros would add a small push towards "volatility fell" every morning.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** Feeding NaN (skipped by the monitor) avoids that.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `run_stream()` feeds NaN. A = 7500, c = 3, calibration 375 or 750, band 0, 0.25 or 0.5, pre-registered primary configuration calibration 750 and band 0.25 (chosen because E4d found a 1-session calibration slightly above the bound and E4c found band 0.25 to 0.5 controls drift). Validation: next-session robust volatility against the alarm's baseline; control: random minutes of the same run while it is monitoring, compared with the baseline in force at that minute.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** `python experiments/e07_nifty.py q7b-dev` (2017 to 2021 only, outputs `*_devonly.csv`), then `q7b` (whole sample). The development numbers of the full run equal the development-only run exactly (656, 389, 206, 434, 300, 166 robust alarms), which confirms the monitor is causal.
* **RESULTS.** Primary configuration: 0.26 alarms per session in both periods; up alarms followed by a more volatile next session 73% (dev) and 78% (held-out), against 46% and 47% at random times; down alarms followed by a calmer next session 86% and 76%, against 55% and 53%. Gaussian benchmark (calibration 750): up 62% and 61%, down 82% and 76%.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** Nothing was changed after looking at the held-out period.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** After each alarm the monitor recalibrates for 750 steps (2 sessions) and is blind meanwhile. On 4 June 2024 it raised an up alarm at 09:26 and then could not see the rest of the crash or the next session.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Event study and India VIX.

## Iteration 5. Q7d event dates and a flawed first control

* **OBJECTIVE.** Compare alarm rates on verified event days with ordinary days.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (a) Web fetches of official sites (RBI, Wikipedia) are blocked from this environment, so every date was checked with web searches and the cited URL is a search result that states the date. For 41 RBI policy days a URL clearly tied to the date was found; meetings whose date could not be tied to a source (for example June 2017, February 2018, February, August and October 2020, December 2021, February and April 2022, June, August and October 2023, four in 2024, October 2025, April and October 2026) were left out. (b) My first robustness check kept sessions where the monitor was in monitoring mode for at least half the session. That filter is circular: an alarm inside a session starts a recalibration, so sessions with alarms have a lower monitored share. It removed most alarm sessions (budget days fell from 11 to 1).
* **HYPOTHESIS.** Whether the monitor was monitoring at the first bar of the session is decided before the session starts, so it is a fair filter.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Replaced the filter with "monitoring at open". Monthly expiry days come from the verified weekday rule (last Thursday, last Tuesday from September 2025); the 5 months where that weekday was a holiday were left out instead of applying an unverified holiday rule.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Fisher exact tests for any, up and down alarms, all sessions and sessions monitoring at open; time of day of alarms.
* **RESULTS.** Budget days 9 of 11 with an alarm (82%) against 25% on ordinary days (Fisher p = 0.0001), election result days 2 of 2 (both up alarms within 40 minutes of the open), monthly expiry 38% (p = 0.002), RBI days 32% (p = 0.36), March 2020 window 29% (p = 0.62).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** A filter that does not depend on the outcome.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** Only 11 budget and 2 election days. The March 2020 result says more about the recalibration rule than about the crash (see the report).
* **NEXT ITERATION.** India VIX.

## Iteration 6. Q7c India VIX

* **OBJECTIVE.** An outside check with a forward-looking volatility measure the monitor never sees.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** No VIX file is in the repo. The NSE archive works without a spoofed user agent, but one file per day means about 2,370 requests.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** The daily index close files carry an India VIX row (verified on the first file).
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `e07c_india_vix.py download`: cached, 1 s pause, retries with back-off, log in `data/raw/india_vix/download_log.csv` (git-ignored). The analysis also cross-checks NSE's official Nifty 50 high, low and close against the minute data.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED, RESULTS, REMAINING ISSUES.** See `docs/report_parts/E7.md`, section Q7c.
