## Iteration 1. Theory, implementation, tests

**OBJECTIVE.** E23's primary monitor P1 could not raise an increase alarm for 28% of its monitored time on BTC in 2026, and three of the sixteen releases fell in such blind segments. E19 had proposed a guard (discard a blind calibration window and calibrate again) and left it unproved, because the windows kept are then selected by their bounds. Derive a guard with a proof, implement it without touching the audited files, and test it.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (1) Is the blind condition decided at a stopping time, and how much power does a window just above it keep? (2) Does keeping only non-blind windows break the coverage of the kept window's bounds, and with it Theorem 6.4, Corollary 6.6 and Corollary 1? (3) What do the alternatives (a time-out, a fallback to E14's rank monitor) guarantee?

**HYPOTHESIS.** The selection does break per-window coverage, but a union over the windows tried, with the error budget spent over them (δ_1 + ... + δ_K ≤ δ), gives the unconditional bound that the proofs actually use, so every guarantee survives with the tolerance taken relative to the kept window.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.**
* `docs/theory_blind_guard.md`: Lemma 1 and Proposition 1 (the blind event is in `F_T`); Proposition 2 (near-blind: exact delay floor `d_min(ρ)`, attained by `X ≡ τ²`, and a rate bound); Lemma 2 (Theorem 6.2 restarted at a stopping time); Theorem 1 (`P(kept window's bounds fail | F_S) ≤ Σ δ_k P(window k tried)`); Proposition 3 (a law inside the tolerance under which a window is kept exactly when its upper bound fails; without spending the failure probability is `1 - Π(1 - p_k)`, about K times one window's); Theorem 2 (Theorem 6.4, Corollary 6.6, Corollary 1 unchanged); Remarks 1 to 6, among them that a calendar day is not a period of a segment (an upper bound with the expected number of segments started, and a sketch that an adaptive null can exceed δ + m/A on a fixed day; this is not specific to the guard); Proposition 4 (time-out: δ/D extra alarms per period); Theorem 3 (rank fallback: Σδ_k + m/A, tolerance on the cs branch, exchangeability after the fallback on the rank branch, long-run bound by a per-segment optional-stopping argument because the branches live in different filtrations).
* `src/avmonitor/guard.py`: `spending_schedule`, `near_blind_delay_floor`, `GuardedVolatilityMonitor`, `GuardedPeriodicVolatilityMonitor` (a mixin that overrides the end of calibration, the reset after an alarm and, for the rank fallback, the monitoring step). Docstrings state the guarantee and its hypothesis.
* `tests/test_guard.py`, 14 tests [SYNTHETIC]: the blind condition and Lemma 1 on constructed data; discard-then-keep; η on both sides of ρ - 1; the delay floor (and that a monitor fed 2τ attains it); the spending schedule; the retry bookkeeping (the kept window's U and L equal `cs_baseline` on that window alone at δ_k/2); budget reset after an alarm; both fallbacks; identity with the unguarded monitor with one try (Gaussian and t3; plain and periodic) and when no window is blind; Proposition 3's construction against its formula; Monte Carlo null checks of the first-m and per-period bounds (Gaussian and t3, step in volatility inside the τ part); a rank-fallback null check.
* `experiments/e24_blind_guard.py`, part `theory`: `results/e24_near_blind.csv`.
* Commit `4c294e4`, pushed.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.**
* `python -m pytest -q tests/test_guard.py` (14 passed, about 15 s); full suite (529 passed) before the commit, after the audit's commit `9e81de1` had changed `monitor.py` and `periodic.py` (bet ranges checked, `null_band ≥ 0`, `c_down` in known mode); the guard needed no change.
* `python experiments/e24_blind_guard.py theory` (seconds).
* Exploratory diagnostics on E23's committed BTC run (scratch scripts outside the repository; known data, **post hoc**): the windows of P1 on BTC in 2026 with `τ²/m↑ ≤ 1.2`.

**RESULTS.**
* [NUMERICAL] For P1 (A = 36,000, λ grid up to 0.5) the increase side cannot alarm within a day, whatever the data, when ρ = τ²/m↑ ≤ 1.0105 (`e24_near_blind.csv`); d_min is 1503 steps at ρ = 1.01, 371 at 1.05, 91 at 1.25.
* [REAL, post hoc] The mechanism of E23's blind BTC windows is price discreteness, not a quiet regime: the four BTC windows of 2026 with ρ ≤ 1.2 all start on weekends (24 January, 1, 9 and 16 August); in their τ part (the first 360 minutes) 25% to 35% of returns are exactly zero and the robust scale is about 2 × 10⁻⁷, about one price tick (0.01 USDT at BTC's price), so τ is set by bid-ask bounce; the robust scale of the sequence part is 38 to 1012 times larger, and 62% to 91% of its returns exceed τ. The per-window numbers are re-derived by part `real` (`e24_real_guard_windows.csv`). This finding shaped the synthetic design `frozen` (a share of frozen minutes, in ticks, that falls after the τ part), which was therefore chosen after seeing real data.
* [SYNTHETIC, smoke run of 3 paths per design] With normal returns, a step in standard deviation by k after the τ part gives ρ near 2.1 (k = 2), 1.2 (k = 4), 1.0 (k = 6) and 0.95 (k = 8): steps of 2 to 4 do not make P1 blind. Steps of 6 and 8 were added to the designs for that reason.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The guard has a proof; E19's worry about selection is confirmed as a coverage failure and repaired by spending, not by an argument that the selection is harmless.

**REMAINING ISSUES and failures recorded.**
* A scratch `numbers.py` in the shared scratch directory shadowed the standard library module and broke `numpy`'s import; E24's scratch scripts were moved to their own directory.
* The first version of the selection test was too weak to fail (few paths, small budget); it now uses 600 paths with δ_k = 0.3 and compares the observed rate with `1 - (1 - p_1)^3` within 3.5 standard errors and with `rate > 1.8 p_1`.
* The rank-fallback test first asserted only that "something happened"; it now checks the alarm, then that the next segment calibrates again with window 1 and δ_1.
* Smoke run of the synthetic part: the rank monitor's first segment starts after 120 returns, before the step, and alarms correctly at the step, which contaminated its null endpoints; fixed by taking the null endpoints from the first segment that starts after the pre-change law has settled (`T_NULL`). In the `frozen` and `drift` designs the rank monitor sometimes had no such segment before the change, which gave NaN summaries and a ZeroDivisionError; detection now uses all segments and the summary guards against empty groups.
* In the illustration of Proposition 3 the per-window budget had been set to 0.9 (meaningless); fixed to 0.3 before any run.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Run the synthetic designs; fix the recommended guard from them, before any real-data rerun.

## Iteration 2. Synthetic experiments, and the recommended guard

**OBJECTIVE.** Measure, in designs that make blindness common, the blind share, the false alarm probabilities against the proved bounds, detection of a later increase, and the calibration cost, for the unguarded P1 (U), three guarded versions (G: K = 3, η = 0, keep; GR: K = 3, η = 0, rank fallback; GE: K = 3, η = 0.25, rank fallback) and E22's rank monitor S2 (R); then fix the recommended guard before any real-data rerun.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The smoke run (3 paths per design) suggested that η = 0 leaves near-blind windows (ρ just above 1) that cannot detect anything, so E19's criterion alone would not be enough.

**HYPOTHESIS.** GE detects in the near-blind designs where G does not, at the price of one extra calibration day only when the first window is near-blind, with no change in the null rates.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** None to the monitors. `experiments/e24_blind_guard.py`, part `synthetic` (designs and monitors fixed before the run, as in the code comments): 11-day streams, the change (standard deviation times 2) at a uniform minute of day 9; designs `step2_gauss`, `step4_gauss`, `step6_gauss`, `step8_gauss`, `step8_t3`, `frozen`, `drift8_gauss`, `calm_btc_days`, `calm_btc_perm`. After the run, two corrections to the summary only (no simulation rerun; part `synthetic_summary` re-reads `e24_synthetic_paths.csv.gz`): (1) one-sided Clopper-Pearson 95% upper limits, because every null rate was 0 and its plug-in standard error is 0; (2) the per-period rates skip the empty first period: `analyse` stores period 1 as [segment start, first day boundary), which is empty when the segment starts on a boundary, as every cs segment here does (found when the calm-day rates showed the maximum in "period 2").

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** `nohup nice -n 10 python experiments/e24_blind_guard.py synthetic --procs=2 --paths=800` (07:21 to 08:24 UTC, 3790 s; 9 designs, 800 paths, 5 monitors; seeds `[24, design, path]` for the data and `[24, 99, design, path]` for the rank tie-breakers).

**RESULTS.** [SYNTHETIC] (`results/e24_synthetic.csv`, figure `results/figures/e24_synthetic.png`.)
* Null rates: under every design whose law is constant after the first τ part (the five steps, `frozen`, `calm_btc_perm`), 0 of 800 paths had an alarm in the first day, or in any of the first four days, of the first null segment, for every cs monitor (upper limit 0.0037; bound 0.05); the rank monitor also 0 (710 to 800 paths with a null segment; 4 in `frozen`, where its first segment starts before the frozen share falls and lasts to the change). Long-run bound 0.27 to 0.31 expected alarms per path; observed 0.
* Median first-window ρ (U): 2.09, 1.20, 1.03, 0.96 for steps 2, 4, 6, 8 (0.96 t3; 0.88 `frozen`). Blind share of U's monitored time before the change: 0, 0, 13%, 99%, 97.5%, 100%; every guarded monitor 0 (no window was kept blind, no rank fallback was needed).
* Detection within a day of the doubling: U 100%, 0.25%, 0%, 0%, 0%, 0% (steps 2 to 8, t3, `frozen`); G and GR 100%, 0.1%, 16%, 99%, 98%, 100%; GE 100%, 86.5%, 100%, 100%, 100%, 100%; R 100% everywhere (99.75% t3). Median delay among detections: GE about 50 minutes (64 in `frozen`, 115 t3), R 26 to 30 (127 t3).
* Calibration cost: GE spent one extra window (1440 minutes) in 86% of `step4` paths and in every `step6`, `step8` and `frozen` path, none in `step2` or `calm_btc_perm`; G 234 minutes on average in `step6` (16% of paths) and about 1430 in `step8`.
* `drift8_gauss`: ρ ≈ 1.6, no window discarded; every cs monitor alarms on the first monitored day of every path (the tolerance fails relative to the first window, whose sequence part spans the drift; correct alarms in the theorem's sense). `calm_btc_days`: first-day alarm share 0.78 (U), 0.76 (guarded), 0.62 (R): whole days with different levels break both hypotheses; alarm rates, not false alarm rates.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The recommended guard is fixed: **GE (K = 3, halving, η = 0.25, rank fallback)**, `docs/theory_blind_guard.md` section 6.1, before the real-data reruns. In `experiments/e24_blind_guard.py` it is the real-data arm G1; G0 (K = 3, η = 0, keep, the guard closest to E19's proposal) is kept for comparison. The choice of η = 0.25 was made before the synthetic run (it is in the monitor list), after the 3-path smoke run; the full run confirmed it and did not change it.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** No synthetic design used the rank fallback, so its contribution is shown only by the test `test_null_bound_rank_fallback` and by real data. The null checks cannot distinguish the monitors (all 0); the bound is far from tight in these designs, as in E19 and E22.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Proposition 3's numbers (part `selection`), then the real-data reruns with G1 and G0.

## Iteration 4. Forward pre-registration (written while the synthetic part ran)

**OBJECTIVE.** Fix, before the data exist, a confirmatory release test on releases after E23's data: the primary monitor (G1, the recommended guarded P1, or S2, the rank monitor), the single primary test, the sample size for power 0.8 and the end date, and make the script refuse to run early.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (1) The release dates after 28 October 2026 are scheduled, not released, and the official hosts cannot be fetched from this machine. (2) On 28 October 2026 the Fed chair said one 2027 meeting will be a strategy meeting without a statement, without saying which. (3) Some 2027 CPI dates on third-party calendars are projections that differ from the BLS schedule. (4) E23's October 2026 Binance files are cached partial months (to 24 October 23:59); a naive download would reuse them.

**HYPOTHESIS.** A calendar built only from the official schedules, with an explicit update rule and a script that refuses to run while any release is unverified, keeps the forward test honest; the planning uncertainty about the statement-free meeting can be handled conservatively.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.**
* `data/events/us_macro_events_e24_future.csv` (24 rows): the released FOMC statement of 28 October 2026 (E23's row) and, with status `scheduled`, 14 CPI releases (10 November 2026 to 10 December 2027, BLS schedule as indexed by search; the 10 November 2026 date also on the BLS CPI home page) and 9 FOMC statements (9 December 2026; the eight 2027 meetings of the tentative schedule announced on 5 September 2025). Each row records what the search showed, that the official host could not be fetched, and the update rule. No 2028 date is in the file.
* `experiments/e24_blind_guard.py`, parts `power` (planning inputs, the power grid, the decision rule in the docstring, written before the part was first run), `future` (refusals; download that replaces partial cached months; E23's data checks; the arms G1, S2 and P1 through `E22.job`; pooled rows; the families of section 6; guard bookkeeping) and `future_gate` (the same pipeline on E23's period must reproduce E23's committed P1 and S2 test rows exactly).
* Planning conventions, fixed before the first run of `power`: the statement-free 2027 meeting is taken to be the earliest (27 January 2027), which gives the fewest statements for every end date; 2028 dates, if needed, are placeholders (2027 date plus 364 days), with two 2028 FOMC meetings dropped (two strategy meetings are expected in 2028); placeholders never enter the event file.
* `tests/test_guard.py`: two tests without market data (the refusals; the event file's schema, UTC column, one CPI row per month, and the planning calendar).

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Web searches on 3 November 2026 (federalreserve.gov and bls.gov cannot be fetched from this environment: `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND`); a quick power check for S2 alone on the planning calendar (scratch, 2,000 replications) to see whether more than a year would be needed: no (S2 pooled about 0.88 by July 2027).
