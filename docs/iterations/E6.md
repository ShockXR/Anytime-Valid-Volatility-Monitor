# Iteration notes: E6 (five coins, Tables 5 and 6, monitor across coins, venues and years)

Scripts `experiments/e06a_five_coins_tables.py` and `experiments/e06b_five_coins_monitor.py`; report section `docs/report_parts/E6.md`. One entry per substantial iteration, failed attempts included. Dates are UTC, 2 November 2026.

## Iteration 1. Table 5 on Binance and the first DOGE hypothesis (failed as stated)

* **OBJECTIVE.** Put Table 5 for all five coins next to Binance and test concrete explanations for each mismatch.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The quick check before this work said "DOGE Q1 = Q3 = 0 in the paper, 3.2% zero returns on Binance". The obvious explanation, closes rounded to 4 decimals, had not been tested.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** H1: DOGE prices stored with 4 decimals give at least 50% zero returns (needed for Q1 = Q3 = 0) and leave the other coins unchanged.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `q6a()` with the side-by-side table and a hypothesis table (`results/e06a_hypotheses.csv`).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Rounding Binance DOGE to 4 and 3 decimals and to grids of 0.0002 and 0.0005 USD; a stale-price variant (H2); the same 4-decimal rule on the other four coins.
* **RESULTS.** 4 decimals gives only 27.4% zeros and Q1, Q3 = ∓5.8 × 10⁻⁴: **H1 as stated is falsified**. A grid of 0.0002 USD gives 45%, 0.0005 USD 70.7%, 3 decimals 84.5%. Every variant that reaches Q1 = Q3 = 0 also has MAD = 0, hence a printed robust kernel of exactly 0, while the paper prints 2.00 × 10⁻¹² (the same as XRP).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** Added a finer grid scan (0.00025, 0.0003, 0.0004 USD) to locate the threshold: 0.00025 USD is the first grid with Q1 = Q3 = 0 (51.6% zeros). Turned the MAD = 0 consequence into an internal-consistency check of the paper's DOGE rows.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** Rounding does not change the extremes, so it cannot explain the smaller published DOGE extremes.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** The means and the extremes.

## Iteration 2. ETH sign, time zones, and a window length read off Table 6

* **OBJECTIVE.** Explain the ETH sign and the 2% gaps in the other means.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** A mean of one-minute log returns equals log(last / first close) / n, so it pins the window. Two candidate explanations: a time-zone shift of the window (H4), or a sign slip for ETH (H3).
* **HYPOTHESIS.** H4: one offset (for example India time, UTC+5:30) fits all five means. H3: the published ETH mean is the Binance value with the sign flipped.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Offsets from 0 to 30 hours in steps of 30 minutes (`results/e06a_window_offsets.csv`); first and last closes (`e06a_mean_log_change.csv`).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** As above, then (after iteration 3) a test of the window length implied by Kernel / RQ (H4b, `e06a_window_length.csv`).
* **RESULTS.** **H4 failed**: no offset fits within print precision; UTC is among the best (worst coin 2.5%), India time is worse (5.4%). H3: the ETH log change is +0.1789 on Binance and -0.1775 implied by the paper, the same size within 0.8%. H4b (same endpoints, about 262,950 returns): all five means within 1.7%, better than the full half year; a window that ends early or starts late is far worse (15% and 17%).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** The write-up says "consistent with a venue with about 0.75% missing minutes", not "the paper used venue X".
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** No hypothesis matches the means within print precision, so prices differ slightly too (another venue, or USD against USDT).
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Table 6.

## Iteration 3. Table 6 per coin, and an "RMSE" check that first gave a misleading answer

* **OBJECTIVE.** Repeat the formula identification of E5a for five coins and find a definition that reproduces the RMSE column.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (a) RQ, MinRQ, MedRQ and Kernel ratios swung from 0.6 to 230 across coins, so single-coin matches (like ETH RQ at 1.02) were not evidence. (b) The first RMSE check compared |Q - 2.2 × 10⁻⁶| with the published RMSE to within half a unit of the last digit, and flagged most rows as "not reproduced", for example BTC RQ (1.208 × 10⁻⁶ against 1.20 × 10⁻⁶). That was wrong: the published estimate Q is itself rounded, and some RMSEs look truncated rather than rounded.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** (a) The 10 October 2025 crash dominates the fourth-power estimators on Binance; removing it separates crash from bulk. (b) With rounding intervals, the simple definition explains almost everything, and the exceptions are real.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Every formula also computed with 21:00 to 23:00 UTC on 10 October removed; an interval-based consistency check (`consistent()` in `q6b`); the Jensen bound for any averaged RMSE; the truncated target κ₄ θ; the implied sample length from Kernel / RQ; a raw against scaled MAD comparison adjusted to the paper's own quartiles; a power scan for BPQ and TPQ.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** `q6b()` (`results/e06b_*.csv`).
* **RESULTS.** The single-estimate definition is consistent with 29 of 35 entries. The exceptions: the robust kernel for BTC, ETH, XRP and DOGE (2.12 × 10⁻⁶ printed), MedRQ for BTC, and the last digit of BPQ for BTC. No averaged definition can print 2.12 × 10⁻⁶. |Q - 0.9638 θ| gives exactly 2.12 × 10⁻⁶. Kernel / RQ is 3.986 × 10⁻⁵ on Binance for every coin, as the algebra predicts, and the published ratios imply n between 262,854 and 263,052. After quartile adjustment the raw MAD fits the robust kernel (0.81 to 0.96) and the scaled MAD does not (1.86 to 2.48). BPQ and TPQ behave like powers 2 and 1.25 to 1.5 of |r|; one third of bipower variation and of eq. (5) with p = 1/2 fit BTC within 1% and 2%.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** Added these two "1/3" candidates to the formula table after the power scan pointed to them. Recorded as an inference.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** The MedRQ RMSE for BTC (2.22 × 10⁻⁶) and SOL's robust-kernel RMSE (2.20 × 10⁻⁶ by the simple definition) remain unexplained.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Daily IQ (Q6c), then the monitor.

## Iteration 4. Daily IQ and how much truncation removes

* **OBJECTIVE.** E5b for five coins, plus a direct measure of the fourth moment removed by clipping.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** E5b reported ratios to MedRQ but not the share of the fourth moment clipped away, and did not separate fat tails from intraday seasonality.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** Clipping removes far more than the Gaussian theory says (29.5% raw MAD, 3.6% scaled), and deseasonalising explains part of it.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `q6c()`: daily IQ for ten estimators; shares kept for one-day and six-month MADs on raw and deseasonalised returns (profile warmed up on June 2025).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** 184 days per coin.
* **RESULTS.** Same ranking on all five coins. One-day clipping keeps 10% to 17% (raw MAD) and 21% to 35% (scaled MAD) of Σ r⁴. **Deseasonalising helps only 1 to 5 points, so the second half of the hypothesis failed**: the cause is fat tails within the day. A six-month MAD keeps under 2%.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** None needed.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** MedRQ is a reference, not the truth.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** The streaming monitor.

## Iteration 5. Monitor runs, gaps, and a biased HAR forecast

* **OBJECTIVE.** Q6d and Q6e with the settings frozen from E5.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (a) E5 replaced NaN returns with 0, which looks like evidence of low volatility. (b) The E5 control (next day against previous day) is not matched to how an alarm is scored (next day against a calibration window that ended some time before). (c) In the first full run the HAR forecast, fitted on 3 months of history (from April 2025), was biased: at random times realised volatility was above the forecast 67% of the time for BTC, with a median log ratio of +0.18 (`results/e06e_iteration1_short_har_history.csv`). The EWMA forecast was unbiased (48% to 52%).
* **HYPOTHESIS.** (a) NaN should be skipped, not zeroed. (b) A matched-delay control is fairer. (c) A longer training history reduces the HAR bias. None of these touch the monitor's settings.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** NaN passed to the monitor (skipped when monitoring, dropped when calibrating) and to the rolling medians; the matched-delay control; HAR history from January 2024 for the July to December 2025 period (the out-of-sample period already started there); EWMA used for the figure.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Full rerun (about 5 minutes on 2 cores).
* **RESULTS.** The mirror has no missing minutes, so (a) changed nothing on Binance. The HAR bias for BTC fell from 67% to 63% (median log ratio +0.09) and remains, also on Bitstamp. It is reported, and every hit rate is compared with random times under the same forecast. The monitor results did not change, because the intraday profile only uses the previous 20 days.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** Results no longer depend on the forecast choice: EWMA and HAR give the same conclusions.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** HAR on robust daily volatility mean-reverts towards the long-run level, which lags BTC in late 2025.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** The competitor.

## Iteration 6. A rate-matched competitor: too slow at first, then a negative result for precision

* **OBJECTIVE.** Compare the e-detector with a fair non-sequential rule that raises the same number of alarms.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The first implementation rebuilt the candidate alarm list with `np.union1d` in every bisection step. With about 3,000 threshold evaluations per series and up to 790,000 minutes per evaluation it would have taken 10 to 25 minutes per run. After that, the question was whether the two rules flag the same events.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** The e-detector, which accumulates evidence, is more precise than a threshold on a ratio of window volatilities at the same alarm rate.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Precomputed ratio series per short window and a boolean mask instead of `union1d` (a few milliseconds per evaluation); coordinate-wise bisection for the two thresholds with a 1,440-minute dead time; overlap and timing of the two rules' alarms; pooled tables with two-proportion tests (`results/e06e_pooled.csv`, `e06d_pooled.csv`).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Two short windows (60 and 240 minutes, fixed before running), matched to each band, five coins, two periods, plus Bitstamp.
* **RESULTS.** **The hypothesis largely failed.** At band 0.25 the ratio rules are within 0 to 5 points of the e-detector, not significant. Only at band 0.5 out of sample, with the EWMA forecast, is the e-detector better (about 4 points for increases, 10 points for decreases against the 60-minute rule). About half of the alarms coincide within 6 hours, and when both fire the e-detector is earlier (median 5 and 30 to 50 minutes).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** The write-up states that the e-detector's advantage is the false alarm guarantee with fixed settings, not extra precision, and that the competitor's thresholds were tuned with hindsight.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** Cross-coin correlation makes pooled p-values optimistic. A cost-aware use (risk switch) is the real test of usefulness and is outside E6.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** None planned inside E6. Candidates: a block bootstrap by day for the pooled intervals; the same forecast check for the confidence-sequence baseline of E8.
