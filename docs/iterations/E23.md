## Iteration 1. Freshness check, event dates, power, pre-registration

**OBJECTIVE.** Turn the one pre-specified real-data test (E12's FOMC and CPI test, rerun with a guaranteed monitor in E22) into a confirmatory one, on releases nobody in the project had looked at: those of 2026.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** E12, E14 Q14g and E22 all used the releases of 2024 and 2025; E22 was designed after E12's answer was known (referee round 2, F15). Nifty 50 data of 2026 had already been used (E7, E7c, E7d, E19, the live study), so a fresh sample had to be crypto only, and it had to be shown that no committed work had used crypto returns of 2026.

**HYPOTHESIS.** If E22's contrast is a property of the releases and of the monitor, it should reappear on 2026 releases at about E22's rates, and a test chosen for power at those rates should pass.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.**
* Freshness check (no market data loaded): every committed crypto load in `experiments/`, `alternatives/`, `scripts/` and the notebook ends at 2026-01-01 (exclusive); no result CSV holds a crypto date or epoch timestamp in 2026 (the 2026 dates in results are all Nifty); the git history adds no crypto load past 2026-01-01; no Binance 2026 file was on disk. The Bitstamp BTC file on disk runs to 2 November 2026, but every reader cuts it at 2026-01-01 and E23 does not read it.
* Event dates verified by web search on 3 November 2026 (the official hosts do not resolve from this machine; search results that index the Federal Reserve press releases and BLS archive pages were used): 7 FOMC statements and 10 CPI releases, among them the January 2026 CPI moved from 11 to 13 February 2026 by the lapse in appropriations that began on 31 January 2026. No unscheduled FOMC statement was found. The 11 September CPI rests on BLS regional releases of that date and the BLS current-release page, not on the national archive page, which the search did not surface. `data/events/us_macro_events_2026.csv`, same columns as E12's file plus `access_date`.
* Power part of `experiments/e23_fresh_releases.py` (written before the commit, committed in iteration 2 with that part unchanged except that the calendar end became an argument and the column `planned_end` was renamed `calendar_end`; the numbers reproduce exactly): Monte Carlo power of E12's stratified permutation test under E22's P1 BTC rates, on the 2026 calendar with control counts from `E12.control_anchors` on a gap-free minute index to 1 November 2026.
* `docs/preregistration_e23.md`, committed alone with the event file as `b5c1573` (06:35:40 UTC, 3 November 2026) and pushed, before any 2026 file was downloaded.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** `nice -n 10 python experiments/e23_fresh_releases.py power` (3 s): two designs (BTC FOMC alone; BTC FOMC and CPI together), four scenarios (E22's rates; half of E22's excess; E22's FOMC rates and no CPI effect; null), 20,000 replications, seed 23,000.

**RESULTS.** [SYNTHETIC] Power at E22's rates: 0.690 for FOMC alone (7 releases), 0.818 for the pooled test (17 releases), so the pooled test became the primary. Half of E22's excess: 0.330 and 0.405. FOMC effect only: 0.690 and 0.534. Under the null, FOMC alone rejected 5.8% of the time (Monte Carlo s.e. 0.17 points): when releases share their control windows, E12's treatment of their strata as independent can exceed the nominal level a little; the pooled test rejected 3.9%. Reference on the known data (E22's windows): pooled 13 of 38, p = 6.5e-6.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** A design, test, families and reading on record before any fresh return was seen; the choice of test documented by its power.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** E22's rates are probably optimistic (they are the reason for the question). The power model ignores the clustering of alarms in time.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Script, synthetic tests, gates, download, run.

## Iteration 2. Script, synthetic tests, gates, data, run

**OBJECTIVE.** Run the pre-registered analysis on the 2026 data with nothing but the period and the event list changed.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** E12's functions read their period from module constants (`EVENTS`, `START`, `MONITOR_START`, `END`), and E22.job calls E12.read_events itself; the pooled test is not one of E12's outputs.

**HYPOTHESIS.** Switching E12's constants (in the main process and in each worker through the pool initializer) and replacing `E12.read_events` by E12's reader plus the 6-hour inclusion rule lets `E22.job` run unchanged; a switch set to E12's own period must then reproduce E22's committed rows exactly. The pooled test can be computed exactly from E22's window rows, since each stratum is (y, n, k) with k = n times the matched control rate.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.**
* `experiments/e23_fresh_releases.py`: `read_events` (E12's reader, then the inclusion rule; it also corrects the reason E12's reader gives a release at or after the end, which it labels as inside the warm-up), `e12_period` (context manager that restores E12), `run_jobs`, `strata_from_windows`, `stratified_test` (E12's Poisson-binomial tail and stratified Fisher test over any set of strata), `pooled_rows`, `check_pooled_against_e12`, `planning_rates`, `calendar_strata`, `simulate_power`, `power_part`, `gate_part`, `download_months`, `data_end`, `data_checks`, `add_families`, `per_day_table`, `delay_table`, `blind_segments_job` (post hoc listing), `figure`, `e22_reference`. Parts `power`, `gate`, `run`.
* `tests/test_e23.py` (13 tests, synthetic or on the committed event file): event file schema and E12's UTC check; the switch restores E12 even after an exception; the inclusion rule at its boundary (a window ending exactly at the end is in, one minute later is out); controls on the right weekday and clock time, at least 2 days from every event day, inside the data; the pooled function equals `E12.stratified_tests` per type and with all releases relabelled as one type (4 seeds), and a p-value by hand; power on toy cases (power 1 and 0, the exact value 0.3 for one release with 19 controls, reproducibility); power increases with the effect; Holm family sizes (1, 2, 6, 6, 6, 6) and one primary test; the data end from synthetic files.
* Failures on the way: two tests failed at first. (1) E12's reader lists a release after the end as "inside the intraday-profile warm-up"; the reason is now corrected in E23's reader and tested. (2) A test built millisecond timestamps with `DatetimeIndex.asi8`, which under pandas 3 is in microseconds for this index; fixed in the test (the script reads the mirror's millisecond timestamps as E12 does, and the data checks show the right dates).

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.**
1. `nice -n 10 python experiments/e23_fresh_releases.py gate` (10 s, data of 2024 and 2025): the switch on E12's own period against E22's committed P1 BTC rows.
2. `nice -n 10 python experiments/e23_fresh_releases.py run --procs=2`, first at 06:37 UTC (73 s): gate, download of the monthly files from December 2025 (the mirror had no November 2026 file), data checks, the 12 arm and coin jobs, tests, diagnostics, figure.
3. The run part was then extended with two outputs and rerun (97 s, identical results): the power at the actual number of releases (required by section 9 of the pre-registration when fewer releases are included) and, post hoc, the list of blind segments with their dates (the pre-registration asked only for E19's per-run summary).

**RESULTS.** [REAL unless marked]
* Gate: 0 mismatches (30 test rows of 14 columns, 38 window rows of 27 columns, the summary counts); pooled function against E12's per-type test: 0 mismatches in 48 rows.
* Data: the mirror's October 2026 files stop at 24 October 23:59 UTC, so the stream is 1 January to 24 October 2026 (297 days, 427,680 minutes per coin); no duplicate, no missing minute, no non-positive price. The FOMC statement of 28 October falls after the end: 16 releases (6 FOMC, 10 CPI). Power at 16 releases [SYNTHETIC]: 0.779 (pooled) at E22's rates.
* **Primary test fails:** P1, BTC, pooled, 2 of 16 releases with an up alarm within 6 hours against a matched control rate of 5.9%, p = 0.25 (Fisher 0.29).
* Secondary: P1 pooled ETH 4 of 16 (p = 0.067), SOL 5 of 16 (p = 0.039), Holm 0.079 both; no P1 cell passes Holm. S2 FOMC 4 of 6 on every coin, Holm 0.016 (BTC), 0.033 (ETH), 0.055 (SOL); P0 ETH FOMC 4 of 6, Holm 0.040; no CPI cell passes under any arm.
* Per-day: P1 alarmed in 37.8%, 48.0% and 46.0% of its monitored periods (bound 5% under the tolerance).
* Watched: P1 34 of 48 releases, P0 17, S2 48. On BTC, P1 was blind at 3 releases (two segments with τ² ≤ m_up, 10 to 15 August and 17 August to 3 October 2026, 28% of its monitored time) and recalibrating at 2; it alarmed after 2 of the 11 releases it watched.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The project's one confirmatory real-data test has been run on fresh data, with its gates passed and its outcome reported as registered.

**REMAINING ISSUES.**
* The release contrast under the guaranteed monitor is not confirmed; it rests on 2024 and 2025 data that were known at design time. The README, the paper's abstract and the E12 and E22 sections need to say so (the integrator's files).
* The blind segment of 47 days on BTC shows that E19's failure mode does occur on a fresh stream; E22's statement that it "does not materialise" holds for 2024 and 2025 only.
* E12's test can exceed its nominal level slightly when several releases share their control windows (5.8% for seven FOMC statements in the planning model); an exact test that conditions on the shared controls would remove this.
* The October 2026 FOMC statement and the November CPI release can be added when the mirror publishes the rest of October and November; that would be an update, not part of this pre-registered test.

**NEXT ITERATION.** None planned for E23 itself. Candidates for other owners: a design that bounds the blind time of the confidence-sequence monitor (for example a restart of calibration when a segment's increase side cannot alarm), pre-registered and tested on data after October 2026.
