## Iteration 1. Verified event times and the pre-registration

**OBJECTIVE.** Fix the events, their reference times, the streams, the methods, the rate matching and the outcomes before any return in the event windows is looked at, and commit that on its own.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (1) The fetch tool could not resolve the news and archive hosts (cnbc.com, presidency.ucsb.edu); only the search tool worked, as in E7 and E12. (2) Three of the four shocks started while the monitored market was closed or at a weekend (the Indian vote count began at 08:00 IST, before the 09:15 open; the August 2024 and April 2025 sell-offs broke in Asia on a Monday morning). (3) For 10 October 2025 the sources disagree on the minute of the 100% tariff post (search results gave 4:50 PM and 5:08 PM EDT from secondary sites). (4) E13's rate matching counts alarms over the whole sample, which includes the event windows, and E13 has no crypto stream for August 2024. (5) Some results around these dates were already in the repository (E6, E7, ALT1, ALT4) and had been read while preparing the task.

**HYPOTHESIS.** A single rule for the reference time (the announcement if the market is open, else the next open; the Tokyo open for the two Monday sell-offs in crypto) avoids picking times after seeing the data. Matching thresholds on alarm counts outside the event windows removes the event windows from the thresholds. Disclosing what was already known lets the reader judge how blind the design is.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Web searches on 3 November 2026 for each event: the Election Commission's 08:00 IST start of counting (Deccan Herald, PTI); the Nikkei's 12.4% fall on 5 August 2024 (Reuters via Kontan) and Tokyo's 09:00 JST open (JPX press release on trading hours); bitcoin's fall "into the Asia open" on 7 April 2025 (Bloomberg) and "Sunday evening" (CNBC); for 10 October 2025, "after the close" (ABC News, AP), CNBC's article on the post at 4:57 PM EDT, CoinDesk's "late Friday afternoon", and the earlier threat shortly before 11 a.m. EDT. Event C3 is kept but marked, with T = 20:00 UTC (US close) and delays also from 20:57 UTC. The existence of every minute (crypto) and every bar (Nifty) on the event days was checked by counting rows only. Streams: E13's H1 2025 and H2 2025 crypto samples, a new H2 2024 crypto stream built the same way, and E13's Nifty 2024 to 2025 stream. Competitors and windows as in E13 (first run and review round 3), without the studentised BJV. Matching: E13's bisection with alarms counted only outside [T − 8 days, T + 2 days). Secondary designs: fresh start at T (E12's second design), another start date for the e-detector (E12's and E7's streams), matching on the whole sample (E13's rule). Committed alone as `b8ee0f7` at 00:37 UTC, before `experiments/e18_landmark_events.py` was written.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** None on returns. Test suite before the commit: 319 passed.

**RESULTS.** Six event and market rows (N1, C1, N2, C2, N3, C3), 18 event and asset panels, all with complete data. No event dropped; C3 marked.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** A design fixed before the run, with sources and a list of what was known.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The exact minute of the 10 October post is unknown. Some primary outcomes (C2, C3, N1 to N3) are determined by E13 computations made before the pre-registration, only not looked at.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Write the script exactly as pre-registered and run it once.

## Iteration 2. The script, one run (interrupted once), checks and the report

**OBJECTIVE.** Implement the pre-registration exactly, run it once, check it against E13, and report every pre-registered outcome.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (1) A container restart at about 01:10 UTC killed the first run: the Nifty BOCPD arrays had been saved, the crypto BOCPD runs and every finished series were lost. (2) The first figure (rows by asset, columns by date) left empty panels, because the coins have no 4 June event and Nifty has no 10 October event. (3) After the run, the e-detector's crypto alarms before T needed context: were they reactions to the start of each episode or ordinary alarms?

**HYPOTHESIS.** Saving each BOCPD job and each finished series as soon as it ends lets a rerun redo only what was lost, with identical numbers, since nothing in the computation is random. A layout with one column per date and market (three crypto dates, three Nifty dates) fills all 18 panels.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `experiments/e18_landmark_events.py`: (a) `bocpd_all` writes one file per (window, truncation) job as soon as it finishes (`bocpd_<market>_w<w>_t<0|1>.npz` in `E18_CACHE`), and `run` pickles each finished series; both only when `E18_CACHE` is set, and no setting changed. (b) `figure` lays out six rows (five coins and Nifty) by three dates. (c) After the run, a `context` command (descriptive, **not pre-registered**, labelled so in the report) writes `results/e18_context.csv`: mean absolute raw minute returns around each e-detector up alarm before T on the crypto events, relative to the first five of the seven days before T, and price changes over the same spans.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** One full run with `nice -n 10` and two processes (1940 seconds, reusing the surviving Nifty BOCPD file), then `figure` and `context`. Reproduction checks inside the script: the e-detector's alarms on the H1 2025 and H2 2025 crypto samples (ten series) and on the Nifty stream are identical in time and direction to E13's saved band-0.25 alarms; under E13's whole-sample rule (S3) the matched thresholds equal E13's saved thresholds in all 96 rows (largest relative difference 0). Test suite before the commit: 373 passed.

**RESULTS.** Primary design: on Nifty the e-detector flagged all three shocks (11, 104 and 172 minutes after the open; on 5 August 2024 and 7 April 2025 it first had to finish recalibrating after a down alarm a few sessions earlier). On crypto it missed all fifteen coin and event pairs in the 24 hours after T, because it had raised up alarms 4.5 to 6.9 hours before T, with the start of each episode, and was recalibrating; its next alarm was a down alarm the following evening. Competitors at matched rates were mostly paused for the same reason (1, 3 and 5 of 45 configurations watching at T); 22 of their 35 crypto catches came in the first minute after a pause ended. The BJV-type test with k = 1440 was watching on all five coins on 10 October 2025 and flagged the cascade within 17 to 73 minutes of 20:00 UTC (on ETH and XRP before 20:57). Fresh start (S1): the e-detector flagged all 18 cases (15 to 207 minutes); earlier than the short-window competitors on 5 August 2024 and on the coins on 7 April 2025, later than plain BOCPD on 10 October 2025 (by 5 to 10 minutes on four coins) and on Nifty on 4 June 2024 and 7 April 2025 (by 14 and 20 minutes). S2: e-detector outcomes identical from the other start dates. S3: same state at T in all 153 competitor rows; 23 first-up-alarm outcomes differ (18 shifts of 1 to 29 minutes, 4 misses become alarms, 1 alarm becomes a miss). In the 7 days before T all methods raised 0.76 to 1.10 times their expected numbers of alarms.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** A complete, pre-registered record for each case, including misses and alarms before T, and a check that the run reproduces E13 where they overlap.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** Four events; the coins co-move. The crypto outcomes depend on the registered reference time, which falls after the start of the C1 and C2 falls. Matching uses data after the events (favours the competitors) and is exact for 79 of 141 competitor series (within two alarms for 133). The context table was added after the run and is descriptive only. The 24-hour pause after each alarm, for every method, decides most crypto outcomes; shorter pauses were not tried because the pre-registration fixed E13's settings.

**NEXT ITERATION.** None planned. A natural follow-up, which would need its own pre-registration, is a shorter recalibration (or a second, faster monitor) for the period after an alarm.
