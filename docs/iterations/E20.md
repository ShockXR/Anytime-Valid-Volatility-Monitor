## Iteration 1. Pre-registration

**OBJECTIVE.** Answer referee items 4, 5, 9, 15, 17 and 18, section 4.1 and M6 (`docs/review/referee_report_paper.md`): say which real-data tests of the paper were pre-specified, adjust them for multiplicity and for dependence across days and coins, and test whether the alarms carry information beyond a strong volatility forecast.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The paper reports dozens of real-data p-values and intervals (Section 6, Supplement S5, and the real-data sentences of Sections 5.5 and S4.3), mostly two-proportion tests or Wilson intervals that treat alarms as independent, with no multiplicity plan except E12's primary family, and a forecast comparison (Table 7) that uses hit rates against an EWMA and a HAR forecast of robust volatility, not a regression on realised variance.

**HYPOTHESIS.** Comparisons of alarms with random times survive any reasonable adjustment (differences of 20 to 40 points); comparisons between methods (e-detector against ratio rules and competitors) are fragile; a forecast that sees the last hour of returns absorbs much of the alarms' information.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `docs/preregistration_e20.md`, committed alone (`bbc8f87`, 01:52 UTC on 3 November 2026) before any alarm list was reloaded for E20 and before any adjusted result: classification rules, 25 families (F1 to F18), Holm within families, circular block bootstrap over calendar days (7 days, 5 Nifty sessions; 14 and 10 as sensitivity), B = 9,999, seed 20, the circular-shift test for the Nifty events, the 5-point equivalence margin, the outcomes, forecasts, regression and QLIKE comparison of Part 3, primary families F17 and F18, and the claim status categories. It says plainly that the data and alarms had been seen, so E20 is a re-analysis.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** None before the commit.

**RESULTS.** Design fixed.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** Every choice that could be tuned to the outcome is on record before its result.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The families and the equivalence margin were chosen by people who had read the original results.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Reload or rerun the alarm lists and check them.

## Iteration 2. Alarm lists and checks (part `checks`)

**OBJECTIVE.** Pre-registration 3.2: use the stored alarm lists, or rerun E6 and E17 with their own functions (imported, not modified), and check every list against the committed summaries.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** E6 does not store ratio-rule alarms, random times or matched-delay control draws per item; E17 does not store the matched one-minute monitor's alarms.

**HYPOTHESIS.** Calling E6's `summarise` and `forecast_hits` in E6's job order with E6's generator (seed 6) regenerates every random draw exactly; mirroring the generator state before each call recovers the draws themselves.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `part_checks` and `_other_checks` in `experiments/e20_inference.py`; per-item files `results/e20_items_e6.csv.gz` (1.3 MB) and `results/e20_items_e17.csv.gz`.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** `nice -n 10 python experiments/e20_inference.py checks`, one core, about 90 seconds (6 to 11 seconds per coin-period plus loading).

**RESULTS.** First run: every E6 summary comparison failed. Cause: under pandas 3, `astype(str)` keeps missing values as missing, so key columns with NaN (the band of the random-time rows) never compared equal; the numbers themselves were right. Fixed by mapping keys with `map(str)`. Second run: all 76 checks passed (`results/e20_checks.csv`): the E6 summary, forecast-hit and competitor rows for ten coin-periods (counts exactly, shares to 1e-12), the mirrored random times and matched-delay control draws, the ratio-rule thresholds, E17's matched one-minute counts at the stored levels for all ten coin and target pairs, and the E6 pooled, E17 pooled, E13, E7, E14 and E15 lists.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** Every later number rests on alarm lists shown to equal the published ones.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** E7's random-time control shares are stored only as shares, so they are treated as fixed in the bootstrap (as pre-registered).

**NEXT ITERATION.** Bootstrap, Holm and the event shift test.

## Iteration 4. Information beyond a forecast (part `forecast`)

**OBJECTIVE.** Pre-registration Part 3 (referee items 4 and M6).

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** Table 7 compares hit rates against two daily forecasts; it does not say whether alarms improve a forecast of realised variance that already uses intraday information.

**HYPOTHESIS.** Against HAR with the last hour's realised variance the alarms add little.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `_coin_grid` (hourly grid, realised variance and bipower variation over the next 1440 and 360 minutes, regressors from past returns only), `_fit_forecasts` (daily refitted expanding-window OLS per coin, model, horizon and measure, at least 720 training points), `_alarm_indicators`, `ols_dk` (Driscoll and Kraay standard errors, scores summed per UTC day, Bartlett bandwidth 5 days, factor D/(D-1)), `qlike`, `qlike_kappa`.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** `nice -n 10 python experiments/e20_inference.py forecast`, 52 seconds. Then the alarm-count check was rewritten (see below) and the part rerun (66 seconds); the regression and QLIKE files were byte-identical to the first run.

**RESULTS.** Strongest forecast on July to December 2025: HAR-I (mean QLIKE 0.537, against 0.562 for HAR and 0.565 for EWMA). F17 (held-out, next-day realised variance, HAR-I, band 0.25): e-detector up +0.038 (Holm 0.32), down -0.064 (Holm 0.046); ratio rule 240 down -0.087 (Holm 0.0007), up +0.063 (Holm 0.056); ratio rule 60 neither. Against EWMA and HAR every alarm coefficient is significant (Holm $p \le 0.002$). F18: scaling HAR-I at alarms with constants fitted on July to December 2025 raised held-out QLIKE by 0.29% to 0.38% (Holm 0.11, 0.025, 0.11); none of 72 loss comparisons shows a gain. The held-out regression sample starts on 23 February 2024 because the HAR regressors need 22 days and the first fit 720 points (one common sample for all forecasts, as registered).

A check that did not do its job: the first version of `e20_forecast_alarm_counts.csv` counted indicators on grid points outside the periods ("outside" = 0), which cannot see an alarm that maps to no grid point at all. Comparing the counts with E6's summary showed 369 grid up alarms in July to December 2025 against 442 in the summary; the difference is Bitstamp BTC/USD (73 up, 53 down), which is in E6's summary but not one of the five coins. Rewritten to count, per indicator and period, the alarms of the five coins, those on the grid, those past the end of the data, and same-hour pairs: every alarm lands on a grid point of its own period except one e-detector down alarm of BTC in each band at 23:25 and 23:53 UTC on 31 December 2025 (no outcome window), and there are no same-hour pairs.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** A direct answer to M6, with a forecast that sees the last hour and an out-of-sample loss test.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** Realised variance from raw one-minute returns is noisier than E6's robust volatility; only three simple forecasts were tried; the development-period mean of $y/F$ at alarms (the QLIKE constant) is far from its held-out value (1.54 against 1.15 for e-detector up alarms), and E20 did not look for the cause.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Classification and the report.
