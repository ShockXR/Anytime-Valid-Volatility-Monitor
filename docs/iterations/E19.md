## Iteration 1. Design and pre-registration

**OBJECTIVE.** Answer referee comment M1 (`docs/review/referee_report_paper.md`): show the anytime-valid guarantee on real data, with (a) the confidence-sequence baseline as the primary real-data configuration, (b) null streams with the real marginal law, and (c) the forecast-relative null on real data; and minor comment m32 (Table S2 with the confidence-sequence baseline).

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** Every main real-data run of the paper uses the plug-in baseline, which has no proof (E14, Proposition A.6), and on real one-minute data the null "volatility equals its calibration level" is false on most days, so real-data alarm rates are not false alarm rates. No experiment so far ran the four monitors on streams that keep the real distribution of one-minute returns (zeros, ticks, jumps) while making the null true by construction.

**HYPOTHESIS.** Permuting the returns of calm days gives an exchangeable stream with the real marginal law. On it the rank detector is exact, the known and confidence-sequence baselines are exact up to a finite-population effect (exact on i.i.d. draws from the pool), and the plug-in baseline should behave as on i.i.d. synthetic data (E14: well above the known-baseline bound over five days). Stationary-bootstrap streams of the calm days keep real clustering inside blocks and should push every baseline above its bound, the more so the longer the blocks.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `docs/preregistration_e19.md`, committed (8fbd573) before any real return was loaded for this study: calm-day rule, pools, five stream types, four monitors and their settings, three endpoints with their bounds, the primary endpoint (one-day rate, permuted streams, band 0, five coins pooled), numbers of streams and seeds, Parts 2 to 4.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Before the commit, the vectorised engine was timed on a synthetic pool (Student t4 with 4% zeros, 130,000 values; scratch files outside the repository): 500 streams of about 30,000 steps take 3 to 5 seconds for the X-based monitors and about 23 seconds for the rank detector. On that synthetic pool the plug-in had 4.6% one-day and 41% five-day false alarms and 0.106 alarms per monitored day (500 streams); these numbers are not reported anywhere else and did not change the design.

**RESULTS.** Design fixed.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** Every choice that could be tuned to the outcome is now on record.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The permuted streams are drawn without replacement, so for the known and cs baselines the null holds only up to the finite-population effect; the i.i.d. stream type is the exact check.

**NEXT ITERATION.** The vectorised monitors and their tests.

## Iteration 2. Vectorised monitors, checked against the classes

**OBJECTIVE.** Run 10,000 to 20,000 streams of 30,000 to 90,000 steps per cell in reasonable time, with exactly the package's monitors.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** `VolatilityMonitor` and `ConformalMonitor` process one return at a time in Python (13 to 45 µs per return, E14); 10^10 row-steps would take days. The plug-in and cs monitors recalibrate at row-specific times, and calibration windows can be degenerate (more than half zeros), which the class handles by extending the window and, at the cap, by a fallback scale.

**HYPOTHESIS.** A row-wise state machine (calibrating or monitoring, per row) with full-array updates, the calibration done row by row when a window completes, reproduces the classes exactly.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `x_engine` (known, plug-in and cs baselines; the extension and fallback rules of `monitor.py` reimplemented; cs bounds from `confseq.eb_confseq`, which has the same arithmetic as the streaming sequence), `rank_engine` (smoothed conformal p-values from one Fenwick tree per row over the dense ranks of |u| in the pool; the reference set emptied after an alarm), `StreamGen` (permutation prefixes, i.i.d. draws, stationary bootstrap, generated step by step so that every monitor sees the same streams from the same seed). `tests/test_e19_real_null.py`: alarm-for-alarm agreement with the classes (including streams where the extension and fallback rules fire), the known-baseline mode against a direct recursion with restarts, the streams, the calm-day rule, and the Part 4 split clip.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** The tests (10 cases), before any real-data run.

**RESULTS.** All agree exactly: first alarm, its side, the numbers of up and down alarms within m monitored steps and the monitored step count, for every stream.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** For the X-based monitors about 0.2 to 0.3 µs per stream and step (500 streams of about 30,000 steps in 3 to 5 seconds, iteration 1), against 13 µs per return for the plug-in class (E14): 40 to 65 times faster.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** None known.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Pools and the Part 1 run.

## Iteration 3. Part 1 too slow under load: one pass for all configurations

**OBJECTIVE.** Finish Part 1 (144 jobs of 500 streams) in a few hours on two workers.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** With the machine shared (load average 7 to 8 on 4 cores), the first run took about 90 seconds per permutation job but 360 to 520 seconds per stationary-bootstrap job: under clustering the plug-in and cs monitors alarm often, each alarm adds a 1440-step calibration, and the streams run to 55,000 steps or more. A profile on a synthetic clustered pool showed that the time goes into the fixed per-step overhead of the NumPy calls (about half of it in the engine's own loop), not into the per-row arithmetic: the six X-based configurations (three baselines, two bands) each paid that overhead separately. The estimated total was over five hours.

**HYPOTHESIS.** Stacking the six configurations as rows of one engine, so that one pass over the streams serves all of them, and doubling the batch to 1000 streams, cuts the overhead by a factor of about 6 for the X-based monitors.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `x_engine_multi` (per-row mode, band and A; `x_engine` is now a one-configuration wrapper, so the class-agreement tests cover the stacked engine), a test that each stacked configuration equals its own single run, batch size 1000 (the seeds remain `SeedSequence([19, data set, stream type, batch])`; the pre-registration did not fix the batch size), and a checkpoint per job so that an interrupted run resumes. The cs calibration loop now visits only rows at a decision point. The first run was stopped after 18 of 144 jobs; its results were never written to disk or looked at (they existed only in the workers' memory), so nothing was chosen after seeing an outcome.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Tests (11 cases, all pass). Parts 4 and 2 ran while the new Part 1 waited for free workers.

**RESULTS.** See iteration 4.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** Same streams for all configurations by construction (one generator per job).

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The rank detector still needs one pass per band (its reference set restarts at row-specific times).

**NEXT ITERATION.** Part 1 results.

## Iteration 4. Part 4 (Table S2 with cs) and Part 2 (cs on the five coins)

**OBJECTIVE.** Referee m32 and M1(a): the Gaussian row of Table S2 with the cs baseline, and cs as the primary real-data configuration with E6's validation.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** None new; both parts were run exactly as pre-registered.

**HYPOTHESIS.** From E8: cs keeps its null rate far below 5% at a delay cost, and on real data acts like an extra band, cutting mostly decrease alarms.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `first_alarms_cs` (vectorised cs split clip, two sequences at δ / 2 when c_down differs from c), `part4`, `p2_job`, `part2` (E6's `summarise` and `forecast_hits` imported unchanged; the plug-in rerun only for monitoring time). After the run, the day-block bootstrap was moved into `primary_bootstrap` so that it can be recomputed from `e19_cs_events.csv` (`python experiments/e19_real_null.py p2boot`); it gives the same 95% intervals (same seed and draws) and adds Bonferroni 97.5% columns for the two directions (post hoc multiplicity statement).

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Part 4: 2000 null paths and 3 × 600 change paths, 3 values of c_down, both baselines. Part 2: 11 series (five coins in two periods, Bitstamp BTC), 3 bands, both baselines; Q6d, Q6e, the ratio-rule competitor, monitoring time and lags.

**RESULTS.** Part 4 [SYNTHETIC] (`e19_split_c_cs.csv`): plug-in rows reproduce Table S2; cs null at most 0.05% per day; cs medians 24% to 142% above the plug-in; c_down = 2 faster than 3 in all cells; c_down = 1.5 slower than 3 at a 20% fall under cs (+45.3 steps, SE 9.8). Part 2 [REAL] (`e19_cs_pooled.csv`, `e19_cs_primary_bootstrap.csv`): primary quantity, band 0.25, July to December 2025: up 78.2% (cs) against 79.6% (plug-in), difference -1.3 points (95% -5.8 to 3.2); down 96.3% against 88.3%, difference +8.0 (2.7 to 13.7; 97.5% 2.1 to 14.5). cs monitors 43% to 50% of the minutes against 29% to 34%. The plug-in rerun reproduced E6's alarm counts in all 33 runs.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** M1(a) answered with E6's own validation code.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** One cs run (BTC, band 0.5) had only 18 alarms against 55 to 66 for the other coins; see iteration 5.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Part 3, and the BTC band-0.5 anomaly.

## Iteration 5. Part 3, and a blind mode of the cs baseline (post hoc)

**OBJECTIVE.** Referee M1(c): the forecast-relative null on real data. Then explain the BTC band-0.5 anomaly of iteration 4.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** BTC, July to December 2025, cs, band 0.5: no alarm at all after 17 August 2025, through 10 October (the day with the largest sum of squared deseasonalised returns in the period, 2.7 times the next) and 21 November (the highest robust volatility, 3.4 times the median day). The segment record of `VolatilityMonitor` shows why: τ was set from the first 360 minutes of the calibration window, which were very quiet (τ² = 5.37e-9), while the rest of the window gave U = 3.67e-9, so m_up = 1.5 U = 5.50e-9 > τ². Since X ≤ τ² < m_up, every increase factor 1 + λ (X / m_up - 1) is below 1 and the increase statistic can never exceed the number of monitored steps; the decrease side needs E[X] < 0.5 L = 1.58e-9, while the mean of X afterwards was 4.47e-9. The monitor was formally monitoring (89.7% of the minutes) but could not alarm. This is not a validity problem (fewer alarms), but it is a cost of the cs configuration that the pre-registration did not foresee.

**HYPOTHESIS.** The same can happen in any cs run whose calibration window starts quiet and then becomes busy, more often with a wider band; it cannot happen with the plug-in unless B < 1 + b.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Post hoc diagnostic `side_activity` and part `blind` (`results/e19_blind.csv`): for every Part 2 and Part 3 run, the monitored minutes in segments with τ² ≤ m_up (increase side off) or with the decrease side off, the longest such stretch, and quantiles of τ² / m_up. Test `test_increase_side_switched_off_when_tau2_below_m_up`. In `fig_real` the alarm-rate panel uses a linear axis, because one configuration has a rate of 0.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Part 3 as pre-registered (five coins, GARCH and EWMA 360 inputs, both baselines, bands 0 and 0.25). The `blind` part. A slip: the queued Part 1 rerun had already started when the `blind` run was launched, so for about three minutes three worker processes ran instead of two; the `blind` run was then paused (SIGSTOP) until Part 1 ends, and a duplicate Part 1 queue was removed before it started.

**RESULTS.** Part 3 [REAL] (`e19_fr_cost.csv`, `e19_fr_pooled.csv`): GARCH-relative, plug-in, band 0.25: 39 alarms over five coins, 0.89 of the nominal bound, all 39 validated; cs band 0.25: 17 alarms (0.47 of its bound), none on XRP and SOL; band 0: 2.5 (cs) to 7.4 (plug-in) times the bound. EWMA-relative: 17 to 89 times the bound, validated 62% to 82%. Blind diagnostic (`e19_blind.csv`): the increase side was off in 5 of 7031 cs segments (all on BTC series: 136.3, 5.2, 5.2, 3.2 and 2.6 days), never for the plug-in (0 of 9184); the decrease side was off once (1397 minutes). The XRP and SOL GARCH-relative cs runs without alarms were not blind.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The blind mode is documented with its mechanism and frequency.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** A guard against it would change `monitor.py`, which this study does not own, so it is proposed, not implemented. The simple guard (if τ² ≤ m_up after calibration, discard the window and calibrate again) needs its own proof: windows are then selected by their confidence bounds, and the windows kept are those with a smaller U, so the per-segment coverage δ no longer holds as such; a union bound over the windows tried gives δ per window tried instead of per segment.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Part 1 results.

## Iteration 6. Part 1 results, and a day-standardised bootstrap (post hoc)

**OBJECTIVE.** Referee M1(b): rates of the four monitors on null streams with the real marginal law, against their bounds.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** None in the run: 72 jobs, 2 workers, 6929 seconds; no stream ran out of returns on the exchangeable streams. On the bootstrap streams the plug-in and cs ran out of returns before 20 monitored days in many streams (they recalibrate after each of several alarms a day), as the pre-registration allowed; their per-day rates use the monitored days they had.

**HYPOTHESIS.** As pre-registered: (i), (iii), (iv) at or below their bounds on permuted and i.i.d. streams; the plug-in above over five days; every baseline above on the bootstrap streams.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** None to the design. Figure layout (legend below the panels, symmetric log axis down to 0.001 so that the cs rates are visible). After seeing the bootstrap results and the spread of the calm days' levels (a factor of 1.55 to 2.12 between the 10% and 90% quantiles of their robust volatility), a post hoc part `p1std`: the same bootstrap with each calm day's returns divided by that day's robust volatility, 500 streams per data set and stream type, seeds `SeedSequence([19, 100 + data set, stream type, 0])` (`results/e19_null_std.csv`, `results/e19_pools_std.csv`).

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Part 1 as pre-registered; `p1std`.

**RESULTS.** [REAL marginal, constructed streams] Primary endpoint, five coins pooled, permuted, band 0, one day: known 2.26% (2.06 to 2.48), plug-in 5.50% (5.19 to 5.82), cs 0.01% (0.00 to 0.04), rank 3.38% (3.14 to 3.64); bound 5%. No interval of (i), (iii) or (iv) lies above its bound in any of the 252 exchangeable-stream cells; the plug-in is above at five days and per day everywhere (41%, 0.103 per monitored day for the coins) and at one day for the pooled coins and XRP. Every bootstrap cell is above its bound for every monitor. Day-standardised bootstrap (`e19_null_std.csv`): five coins, band 0: 96% to 99% within one day (cs 68% to 86%) and 3.1 to 4.9 alarms per monitored day (cs 1.1 to 1.8), 3% to 42% below the unstandardised rates; Nifty 30% to 48% (cs 9% to 10%) and 0.46 to 0.75 per session; the permuted standardised streams as before (known 2.0%, rank 3.3%, cs 0.1%, plug-in 5.2% at one day). Most bootstrap alarms on the coins come from dependence within days.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** M1(b) answered with the pre-registered endpoints; the source of the bootstrap alarms is separated post hoc.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The permuted streams are exact for the rank detector only; for the known and cs baselines the finite-population drift (at most 1.19% of m0) was measured but not turned into a bound. The rates per monitored day of the bootstrap streams were compared with the real series after the fact.

**NEXT ITERATION.** None planned in E19. Candidates: the guard against blind cs segments (needs a proof), and Parts 2 and 3 on Nifty 50.
