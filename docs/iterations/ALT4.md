# ALT4 iteration notes: correlation monitor (alternative A7)

Folder: `alternatives/correlation_monitor/`. Dates are UTC, 2 November 2026. Every number comes from `alternatives/correlation_monitor/results/` and is labelled in `RESULTS.md`.

## Iteration 1: formulation and first prototype

* **OBJECTIVE.** Turn the volatility monitor's recipe into an anytime-valid detector of changes in the dependence between two assets, robust to jumps, that does not mistake a volatility change for a correlation change.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The natural "truncated covariance" statistic (product of standardised, truncated returns) is bounded and jump-robust, but its mean scales with the volatility of each asset, so a volatility break looks like a correlation break. The null for a correlation monitor must be stated on a quantity that volatility cannot move.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** The sign concordance `S = sign(r1) sign(r2)` is bounded in [-1, 1], has mean `(2 / pi) arcsin(rho)` for elliptical pairs, and is invariant to any positive predictable rescaling of either asset. A betting e-detector on it inherits Theorems A and B of `docs/math.md` verbatim and is immune to volatility changes by construction.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `corrmon.py` (statistics, calibration with an additive band, vectorised bounded betting e-detector with per-path null values, Gaussian correlation LR benchmark, naive additive CUSUM with a tuned threshold, streaming `CorrelationMonitor`, 5-minute aggregation), `simulate_pairs.py` (Gaussian or multivariate t pairs, correlation and volatility changes, jumps and co-jumps), 10 tests.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Unit tests only at this stage: arcsin law for Gaussian and t4 pairs, scale invariance, co-jumps with independent signs, false alarm bound at A = 300 over 60 steps for both statistics and both sides, detection of 0.3 to 0.8 and 0.8 to 0.3, no reaction to a volatility x3 change, unit mean of the Gaussian LR factor, streaming loop with NaNs.
* **RESULTS.** All tests pass in 3 seconds, except the first version of the streaming test (next point).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** A working prototype with a proof sketch in the module docstring.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** The streaming test with band 0 and a 1000-step calibration window raised a "down" alarm 1400 steps before the planted change. Diagnosis: the calibration sign mean was 0.228 against a true 0.194, about one standard error (0.03) too high, so the strict decrease null was false. This is the plug-in-baseline effect that E4d found for the volatility monitor. Not a bug; the test now uses band 0.1 and the issue is recorded in `RESULTS.md`.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Theory table and simulations.

## Iteration 2: theory table, validity and delay simulations

* **OBJECTIVE.** Quantify what the sign statistic costs in speed against the truncated product and the Gaussian-optimal detector, and check validity under fat tails, jumps and pure volatility changes.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** A sign statistic throws away magnitude information; the expected efficiency loss against a Pearson-type statistic is large near zero correlation (classical Pitman efficiency of sign tests about 0.4). Whether this matters against a *bounded* product was unclear.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** Bapat's truncation at `c = 3` bounds the product at 9, which caps the admissible bet at about 0.11, well below the growth-optimal bet of about 0.25 for a 0.3-unit shift, so the product's advantage in signal-to-noise should be eaten by its bet cap.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `run_experiment.py` Q0 (growth rates and predicted delays), Q1 (eight null scenarios, four detectors, estimated and true baselines, bands 0 and 0.05), Q2 (six transitions, three innovation types, five detectors). Naive CUSUM threshold tuned once on Gaussian pairs (h = 24.75).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Q0, Q1 (2000 paths per row), Q2 (1000 paths per row). 170 seconds on one core.
* **RESULTS [SYNTHETIC].** Q0 confirmed the hypothesis: predicted delay for 0.3 to 0.6 is 403 steps (sign) against 477 (product c3) from a cold start. Q1: the sign monitor is inside its bound in all eight scenarios (2.3% to 3.1% per day against 5%) and shows 23.6% to 23.8% per 5 days under pure volatility changes, the same as with no change; product c3 alarms on 98% to 100% of volatility-change paths, the Gaussian LR on 80% to 100% (in the wrong direction), the naive CUSUM on 48% to 100%. Under t4 the Gaussian LR and naive CUSUM give 80% and 96% false alarms per day. Q2: sign 174 steps for 0.3 to 0.6 against 74 (Gaussian LR), 246 (product c3), 158 (product c2), 125 (naive); unchanged under t4 and jumps, where the two parametric detectors alarm before the change on 70% to 100% of paths.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** The first Q0 "cold start" prediction overstated the Q2 delays by a factor of about 2, because at a change at step 500 the Shiryaev-Roberts statistic is already near 500 and the best bet has weight 1/6 in the mixture. Added a second prediction `(log(2A / 500) + log 6) / growth`, which matches Q2 within 25% for the sign (219 against 174) and within 10% for the product.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** The sign monitor's 90th percentile delay for small changes is about 2.5 times its median (482 against 174 steps). With band 0 the 5-day false alarm rate sits close to the bound (23% against 25%) because of baseline error; band 0.05 brings it to 3% to 4.5%.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Real data.

## Iteration 3: real data, and a wrong yardstick caught

* **OBJECTIVE.** Run the streaming monitor on BTC/ETH, BTC/SOL and BTC/DOGE for 2024 to 2025 and check what the alarms coincide with.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (a) At one minute the Epps effect biases correlations toward zero and varies with trading activity. (b) The first run compared the post-alarm sign mean with each monitor's own `m0`, which for the product monitor is on the product scale (about 0.7) and not comparable with the sign scale (about 0.6), so the product monitor's "confirmed" share came out as 0% for up alarms and 100% for down alarms, which was obviously an artefact.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** (a) Aggregating to 5-minute bars removes most of the Epps effect for these liquid pairs. (b) Comparing every monitor's post-alarm window with the sign mean (and Pearson correlation) of its own calibration window gives a common, fair yardstick.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Q3a Epps table at 1, 5, 15, 30 and 60 minutes; Q3b monitors on 5-minute bars (A = 5760, calibration 1440 bars, bands 0, 0.1, 0.2 for sign and product, 0 and 0.1 for the Gaussian LR), per-alarm windows (dependence after against the calibration window, BTC robust scale after / before, BTC 24-hour return before and after), 2000 random times as control, matched up alarms across pairs within 2 days, a 1-minute run for comparison, figure 2.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Two Q3 runs (110 seconds each): the first with the wrong yardstick (discarded), the second with the calibration-window yardstick (reported).
* **RESULTS [REAL].** Epps effect: 1-minute Pearson is 0.02 (ETH) to 0.06 (DOGE) below the 30-minute value, mostly closed at 5 to 15 minutes. Sign monitor, band 0.1: 23 to 24 alarms per pair in two years; 31 of 31 up alarms followed by a higher Pearson correlation over the next 5 days than in the calibration window (control 47% to 49%), 75% to 92% of down alarms confirmed. Product c3 down alarms coincide with BTC volatility drops (median ratio 0.83); Gaussian LR up alarms coincide with volatility drops (0.83 to 0.86) and 78% to 88% of its alarms are "down" and near chance. Sign up alarms follow a negative 24-hour BTC return in 80% of cases and a bottom-decile return in 36% to 50% (10% at random). On 1-minute bars the sign monitor gives 57 alarms (43 down) against 23 (13 down) on 5-minute bars for BTC/ETH.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** The volatility-invariance result of Q1 is visible on real data through the volatility-ratio table, and the sell-off clustering gives the practical case.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** Mean reversion of the rolling correlation can inflate the 5-day confirmation (up alarms start from low calibration correlations); the control sets the base rate but a forecast-based yardstick would be stronger. Band 0.2 leaves only 1 to 4 alarms in two years, so the band is coarse on the sign scale. No event attribution was attempted. The k-asset case was not built.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** (Not done.) Confidence-sequence baseline for `m0` as in `docs/math.md` section 6; a rank or winsorised statistic with a predictable scale to recover part of the efficiency gap; a portfolio-level dependence statistic for k assets; a forecast-relative null as in E10 of the main report.

## Iteration 4: volatility clustering as a null, and a basket of four assets

* **OBJECTIVE.** (a) Test the invariance claim in its strongest form: persistent GARCH volatility clustering, the case in which the volatility monitor's own strict null fails (E9, Q9c). (b) Answer the "multivariate" item beyond pairs with one guarantee for a basket of k assets.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (a) Under clustering, products of returns have a conditional mean that moves with the two conditional variances even when the correlation of the innovations is fixed, so every Pearson-type monitor should alarm. (b) With k assets, running all k(k-1)/2 pairwise monitors needs a Bonferroni-type split of the alarm level and gives no statement about the basket as a whole.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** (a) The sign monitor's false alarm rate under GARCH equals its rate under constant volatility. (b) The average pairwise sign concordance is bounded in [-1/(k-1), 1], so one e-detector covers the basket; it should be faster than the pairwise union for basket-wide changes and slower for a change confined to one pair (dilution by k(k-1)/2).
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `simulate_pairs._garch_multiply` and a `garch` option in `pair_returns`; `basket_returns` and `equicorrelation`; `corrmon.basket_concordance`, `basket_bounds`, `BasketMonitor`; three new tests (GARCH keeps the sign mean, basket bounds and invariance, basket monitor detects a basket-wide increase and ignores a one-asset volatility x4); a GARCH row in Q1; Q4 (synthetic validity and delay, basket against "any of six pairs at 6A"; real basket of BTC, ETH, SOL, DOGE on raw 5-minute returns).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Q1 rerun (all earlier rows reproduced exactly, seeded) and Q4, about 4 minutes together.
* **RESULTS.** [SYNTHETIC] GARCH(0.05, 0.94) in each asset, correlation fixed: sign 2.9% per day and 23.9% per 5 days (same as constant volatility); product c3 2.1% / 63.6%; Gaussian LR 25.4% / 83.7%; naive CUSUM 33.3% / 87.2%. Basket monitor, k = 4: 1.7% to 2.8% per day and 19% to 24% per 5 days under Gaussian, t4 and one- or all-asset volatility changes (bounds 5% and 25%); the union of six plug-in pairwise monitors at 6A: 27% to 28% per 5 days with band 0 (2 to 3 points above the bound, baseline error over six pairs), 2.5% to 3% with band 0.05. Delay: basket 72 steps against 132 for all correlations 0.3 to 0.6, 21 against 37 for 0.3 to 0.9, but 451 against 91 when only one pair moves from 0.3 to 0.8. [REAL] Basket of four coins, band 0.1: 31 alarms in two years (15 up, 16 down); 93% of up alarms and 94% of down alarms followed by the basket concordance moving as flagged over the next 5 days (random times: 48%); up alarms follow a negative 24-hour BTC return in 80% of cases and a bottom-decile one in 53% (8 of 15).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** The invariance result now covers the realistic case (clustering) as well as the stylised one (a single volatility jump), and the direction has a multivariate deliverable with a measured trade-off.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** The union of plug-in pairwise monitors needs a band or a confidence-sequence baseline to respect its bound. The basket statistic dilutes single-pair changes; weighted or index-based variants were not tried. The real basket run uses only four assets.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** (Not done.) Weighted basket statistics; an index-based statistic (each asset against the equal-weight index of the others); a confidence-sequence baseline; a forecast-relative null as in E10.
