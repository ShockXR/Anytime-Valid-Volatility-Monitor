# PANEL iteration notes: monitoring a panel of assets with false discovery control (E15)

All numbers below come from `experiments/e15_panel.py` and `tests/test_panel.py`, saved under `results/e15*`. Synthetic results are [SYNTHETIC], Binance and Nifty results are [REAL]. Theory: `docs/theory_panel.md`. Report part: `docs/report_parts/E15.md`.

## Iteration 1. From Shiryaev-Roberts statistics to e-values

* **OBJECTIVE.** Build a K-asset monitor whose false discovery rate (FDR) is controlled at every data-dependent time, reusing the single-asset bet chains of `detectors.py`.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** e-BH (Wang and Ramdas 2022) needs e-values, and the Shiryaev-Roberts statistic M_t of the single-asset monitor is not one (it grows like t under the null). The obvious fix, M_t / t, is an e-value at every fixed t but not at a stopping time. I proved this with a two-step counterexample (`docs/theory_panel.md`, Proposition 1: stop at 1 if the first factor is at least 1, else at 2; then E[M_σ / σ] = 1 + E[(1 - f_1)⁺] / 2 > 1).
* **HYPOTHESIS.** A mixture over possible change times, with weights that sum to at most 1 plus a "cash" term for the remaining weight, is a test supermartingale up to the change. With weight 1/h on each of the first h start times it equals M_t / h + (h - t) / h exactly, so the threshold K/α corresponds to the single-asset monitor at A·K and nothing is lost relative to Bonferroni. e-BH on these e-processes, with each discovered asset stopped at its discovery value ("sticky e-BH"), should give FDR ≤ α at every stopping time, a monotone discovery set, and a time-uniform bound on the false discovery proportion.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `src/avmonitor/panel.py`: `ebh`, `change_weights` (window and Pareto), `PanelEProcess` (own clock per asset, missing returns skipped, inactive assets fixed at 1), `run_block` (vectorised over paths and assets), rules `ebh`, `bonferroni`, `naive`, `none`, `fdp_summary`, `calibrate_panel` (plug-in or confidence-sequence baseline with δ/K per asset), streaming `PanelMonitor` with fixed block starts. `tests/test_panel.py` with 15 tests. Theory note sections 1 to 5 (Lemma 2, Theorem 1 with proof, Remarks 1 to 6).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** The tests: e-BH against its definition on 500 random e-value vectors; weights sum to 1; the window identity with the Shiryaev-Roberts recursion (relative error 1e-10); E[E] ≤ 1 at a fixed time and at a stopping time on 20,000 null paths, both weight families; Proposition 1 on 400,000 draws; the pathwise order naive ≤ e-BH ≤ Bonferroni; sticky sets stay self-consistent; global-null family-wise error and FDR under a common factor; missing returns; inactive assets; streaming against the vectorised code.
* **RESULTS.** All 15 tests pass in about 5 s. The first run raised a divide-by-zero warning in the Pareto weights at n = 0 with h = 1 (the value was masked, but the warning was real); fixed by clamping n to at least 1. Committed as 6f988c9.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** A panel monitor with a proof, and an exact link to the existing single-asset detector, so the panel's Bonferroni rule is the old monitor at A·K.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** What happens over many blocks; how the construction relates to existing work on multi-stream change detection; how fast an e-process can be for late changes.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Literature check and the delay question.

## Iteration 2. Literature check, positioning and a delay lower bound

* **OBJECTIVE.** Attribute every ingredient correctly and find out what is already known before claiming anything.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** Direct fetching of arxiv.org, the Semantic Scholar API, alphaxiv and Project Euclid failed from this environment (WebFetch and curl both blocked), so full texts could not be read. Web searches found that the closest work is Dandapanthula and Ramdas (2025): they prove that any multi-stream procedure with a finite average run length under the global null has worst-case FDR and family-wise error equal to one at some stopping time, and they control "error over patience" with e-BH applied to e-detectors. Wang, Dandapanthula and Ramdas (2025) make the local versus global filtration point for e-BH on stopped e-processes. Tavyrikov, Goeman and de Heide (2026) show that e-BH on running suprema of e-processes does not control the FDR. My first draft of section 6(c) said more about two older papers (Chen, Zhang and Poor 2020; Chen and Li 2023) than their abstracts support.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** The construction is a legitimate second route next to error over patience: give up a finite average run length over an unbounded horizon and get ordinary FDR control at every stopping time, either by one e-process for the whole horizon (which must get slower for late changes) or by blocks on a fixed calendar.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Theory note: section 3 states the impossibility theorem and the two routes; Remark 1 explains why the null must condition on the whole panel's past and cites the stopped e-BH paper; Remark 2 explains why running maxima would not do; section 6(c) now says only what the abstracts say, and makes no priority claim. Added Proposition 2 (any e-process for a fixed null law with delay at most d with probability 1 - β for every change time up to N needs d·KL ≥ (1 - β) log(c⌊N/d⌋) - log 2) with proof, and the matching upper bound from the window identity (Pollak 1985; Lai 1998). A reference list with a URL for each item and an explicit note of which full texts were not read.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Web searches only (2 November 2026), for 14 references: title, authors, venue, year and the specific statement used.
* **RESULTS.** All 14 references found with a URL. The DOI of Chen, Zhang and Poor (2020) and the volume and pages of Wang, Dandapanthula and Ramdas (2025) could not be confirmed and are marked as such.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** The note now says precisely what is new (the error criterion and the way it is bought) and what is not (e-BH, e-detectors, stopped e-BH).
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** Six papers were checked against abstracts only. A reader with full-text access should check Remarks 1 and 2 against Wang, Dandapanthula and Ramdas (2025) and Tavyrikov, Goeman and de Heide (2026).
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Synthetic validity and power.

## Iteration 3. Synthetic validity and power (Q15a, Q15b) [SYNTHETIC]

* **OBJECTIVE.** Measure the FDR, the worst false discovery proportion over a block, and the power and delay of e-BH against per-asset Bonferroni and naive monitors, with independent and strongly dependent assets.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** Theorem 1 says nothing about how loose the bound is or how much power e-BH buys; and with an estimated baseline the bound becomes α + δ.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** FDR far below α (the single-asset monitors are conservative); power gain over Bonferroni growing with K and with the share of assets that change; a clear loss of power with the confidence-sequence baseline.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `e15_panel.py synthetic`: K = 5, 10, 20, 50; independent or common factor (correlation 0.7) with common jumps; 7 designs; exact null mean in closed form; 56 jobs on 2 processes (2,000 paths under no change, 600 otherwise), seeds 16 to 71; plus `robustness_cs` (K = 20, 1,440 calibration steps, δ = 0.01 split over K, seed 1500).
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** As above, about 4 minutes on 2 cores.
* **RESULTS.** No change: P(any discovery) 0.30% to 0.90% for e-BH and Bonferroni, 4.45% to 37.2% for the naive rule (37.2% at K = 50, independent). With changes, the worst e-BH FDR over 48 designs is 0.96% (largest standard error 0.29 points), the worst E[max FDP] 1.0%. e-BH finds on average 5.0 points more changed assets than Bonferroni (at most 21.3 points: 77.7% against 56.4%), and with 60% of 50 factor-driven assets changing together its median delay is 138 steps against 174. Negative results: e-BH's probability of at least one false discovery reaches 8.0% (K = 50) and its expected number of false discoveries 0.115, both not controlled by the theory and both larger than Bonferroni's (1.17% and 0.012). With the confidence-sequence baseline no false discovery was made, but power fell from about 93% to 36% to 41%.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** Evidence that the multiplicity layer works under strong dependence and that its gain over Bonferroni is real at K ≥ 20.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** The provable baseline costs far more than the multiplicity correction saves. Only one block was studied.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Long horizons.

## Iteration 4. One guarantee per block or one for the horizon (Q15c) [SYNTHETIC]

* **OBJECTIVE.** Check the two long-horizon constructions of the theory note, section 6, and measure the log(1/w) delay cost predicted by Proposition 2.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** Per-block FDR control says nothing about false discoveries pooled over blocks; a single e-process for the whole horizon should get slower for later changes.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** Daily e-BH blocks have a much higher chance of some false discovery over the horizon than daily Bonferroni blocks once some assets change; the Pareto e-process keeps one guarantee for the horizon and its delay grows with the change time.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `e15_panel.py horizon`: K = 20 independent assets, ten blocks of 390 steps, 0 or 6 assets change (sd × 1.5) at uniform times, 400 paths, seed 151.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Four rules: daily blocks with e-BH or Bonferroni, one block for the horizon with Pareto weights (h = 390) or a window of 3,900.
* **RESULTS.** No change: 4.75% (daily, both rules), 1.75% (Pareto), 2.0% (window). With changes: P(at least one false discovery) 23.5% for daily e-BH against 4.25% for daily Bonferroni and 5.75% for both horizon-long rules. Pareto median delay 63, 71, 72 and 76 steps for changes in the four time bins; daily e-BH 62, 59, 54, 53.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** Both predictions confirmed. The 23.5% has a clear mechanism: in this design baselines are never recalibrated, so changed assets are discovered again in every later block and keep lowering the e-BH bar for the null assets.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** The last time bin is censored. With recalibration at every block (as on real data) the mechanism above is weaker but not absent; that version was not simulated.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Real data. Committed with the theory note as 2eaa2e3.

## Iteration 5. Real data: five coins and Nifty on one clock (Q15d, Q15e) [REAL]

* **OBJECTIVE.** Run the panel on the E6 coins (daily UTC blocks, as in E6) and on a mixed panel with the Nifty 50, handling trading hours without synchronising returns, and validate discoveries as in E6.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** First test run, mixed panel, July 2025 only (inside the E6 development period), band 0.25: 139 e-BH discoveries in 31 days, 4.48 of 6 assets per day. The per-block loop took 12.5 s per month, and the category assignment failed with a pandas TypeError (a Series combined with a Python list in a boolean mask). With 4.5 of 6 assets discovered per day, the daily strict null is false on most days, so the run says little about multiplicity at that band alone.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** At a wider band (0.5, the other band of the E6 grid, fixed before E15) fewer hypotheses are false and the rules differ more. The difference between e-BH and Bonferroni on real data shows up in timing more than in the discovery set.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `run_block(return_path="all")` returns the unstopped e-value path of every block, so all blocks of a period run at once (3.0 s per month), with a step-by-step offline e-BH check on the first 40 blocks of every run. Numpy boolean masks for the categories. Band 0.5 added next to 0.25, both reported. Timing statistics (minutes by which e-BH precedes Bonferroni), nested categories (e-BH only, naive only), a rate-matched hindsight threshold, Wilson intervals and two-proportion tests. Nifty: E7's session profile made strictly past-only, own clock (h = 374, calibration 750 returns), discoveries outside the session counted. The band 0.5 decision was made after seeing only the July 2025 test; no setting was chosen from the out-of-sample period.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Five coins, Jul to Dec 2025 and Jan 2024 to Jun 2025; five coins plus Nifty, Feb 2024 to Dec 2025; bands 0.25 and 0.5; about 4 minutes.
* **RESULTS.** e-BH discovers 86% to 87% of coin-days at band 0.25 and 58% to 60% at band 0.5. It finds only 3.7% to 8.7% more coin-days than Bonferroni, but flags first on 71% to 77% of the coin-days both find (median 2 to 4 minutes, mean 24 to 38 minutes earlier). Up discoveries confirmed 78% to 83% (controls 46% to 50%), down 85% to 97% (controls 49% to 55%). Negative result: the up discoveries that only e-BH makes are confirmed 42% to 57% of the time, close to the control rate. A hindsight threshold matched to e-BH's count confirms at the same rate (p-values 0.56 to 1.00). Nifty: 226 of 472 sessions discovered at band 0.25, confirmed 87% to 92%; 4 Nifty discoveries made after its session closed, through e-BH's borrowing of strength.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** A working mixed-hours panel with a per-day guarantee; a clear statement of where e-BH helps on real data (earlier alarms) and where it does not (more alarms).
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** The strict daily null is false on most crypto days, so the FDR guarantee is nearly empty there; a forecast-relative null (E10) is the obvious next step. The panel null (conditioning on all assets' past) was not tested against spillovers. The validation measures persistence of the regime, not the truth of the null.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Figures and report.
