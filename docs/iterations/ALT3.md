# Iteration notes: ALT3 (robust local plug-in estimator of integrated quarticity, A5)

Prototype in `alternatives/robust_iq_estimator/`. One entry per substantial iteration, failed attempts included. Every number below is in a file under `alternatives/robust_iq_estimator/results/`, except where an entry says a check was exploratory and not saved; such checks are described without numbers. Dates are UTC, 2 November 2026.

## Iteration 1. Estimator, exact corrections and theory; a biased local scale

* **OBJECTIVE.** Turn Bapat's two ideas (truncation at a multiple of a robust scale, local smoothing) into an IQ estimator that is consistent, close to the efficient bound `8 int sigma^8`, robust to jumps and equipped with a confidence interval, and fix the four issues of `docs/estimator_issues.md` on the way.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** Four issues are known: unnormalised kernel weights, a truncation bias that does not vanish with a fixed `c x MAD`, an averaged estimator that is truncated RQ in disguise (so the kernel adds nothing for the integrated quantity), and clipped jumps that leave `tau^4` each. Behind them is one structural point: averaging local fourth powers can never beat the RQ variance `32/3 int sigma^8`; only squaring a local variance (the plug-in principle of Jacod and Rosenbaum 2013) can approach 8.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** (a) A block plug-in, `(1/k) sum over blocks of (block mean of r^2 / delta)^2`, with an exact local-Gaussian correction for squaring and truncation, is unbiased under constant volatility at any n and has AVAR `8 (k + 3) / (k + 2)`. (b) A local scale makes the truncation rule track the volatility path, and a growing cut-off `sqrt(2 log n)` makes the truncation bias vanish. (c) Dropping a truncated return and re-counting the block removes a detected jump completely, while clipping leaves a residual of about `2 (a^2 - 1)` times `sigma^4 delta`.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `riq.py`: truncated even moments `T_q(a)` by integration by parts; the moments of a clipped or dropped squared normal; `E[(S/m)^2]` and `E[(S/m)^4]` of a block mean through cumulants (so the correction is exact for any block size m, and the same algebra gives `int sigma^8` and any higher power); `block_plugin` (clip or drop, NaN-safe, standard error); `kernel_plugin` (Bapat's kernel, aggregated by squaring); `truncated_rq_local`; closed forms of the AVAR of RQ, TPQ, QPQ and the block plug-in; clipped and undetected jump residuals; the expected value of the paper's cross-validation criterion (eq. 17 as printed). Twelve synthetic tests.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Constant-volatility simulations (n = 1440, 8000 days) of every estimator's AVAR; a 40,000-path check of the jump residuals; a first bias check of the feasible estimators with a local scaled MAD at n = 390 and 1440; the scale ablation (`part_scale_ablation`, seed 13) after the fix.
* **RESULTS.** The closed forms held: AVAR simulated against closed form 10.81 against 10.67 (RQ), 13.48 against 13.65 (TPQ), 8.46 against 8.20 (PI-LU, k = 38); clipped-jump residual 17.56 against 17.55 (plug-in, a = 3), undetected 2 sd jump 8.74 against 8.73 (`q1_avar.csv`, `q1_jump_residuals.csv`). **Failed first version of the scale:** with the local scaled MAD alone the corrected estimators were biased down at n = 390: mean ratio 0.993 (clip at 3), 0.987 (drop at 3), 0.996 (clip at `a_n`), 0.988 (drop at `a_n`) against 1.000, 0.999, 1.000, 0.999 with the true sigma (`q1_scale_ablation.csv`). A MAD from a few dozen returns is noisy, and a noisy threshold truncates more on average than the Gaussian correction assumes. A second, smaller miss: the first test expected the printed CV to be minimised at h = 6 to 12; the closed form puts the minimum at h = 5 (expected values 74.3 at h = 5, 77.2 at h = 10, `q3_cv_theory.csv`), so the test was corrected to the derived property (minimiser at most 10, increasing on the paper's grid).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** Two-step local scale: scaled MAD, then the truncated RMS at 4 times that MAD divided by `T_1(4)`, over a block and its neighbours. Mean ratios became 1.004, 1.020, 1.000, 1.006 at n = 390 and 1.002, 1.010, 0.999, 1.000 at n = 1440. Dropping at a fixed a = 3 now overshoots by 1% to 2% (the threshold uses returns it then judges), so dropping is only used with the growing cut-off, where it is within 0.6%. The first README draft quoted a few numbers from ad hoc runs; they were removed and the ablation was added to `run_experiment.py` so that every number is reproducible.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** The correction assumes local Gaussianity. The CLT of the feasible estimator is not proved.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Simulation grid with stochastic volatility, intraday pattern and jumps; cross-validation study; real data.

## Iteration 2. Simulation grid, cross-validation and real data

* **OBJECTIVE.** Answer Q2 to Q5 of the brief: rates of the truncation rules, the paper's cross-validation, a full comparison with 95% intervals, and agreement with MedRQ and MinRQ on Binance BTCUSDT and DOGEUSDT (July to December 2025).
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The theory of iteration 1 predicts that Bapat's rule (3 times one daily MAD) is inconsistent under stochastic volatility and an intraday pattern, because a daily scale truncates a fixed share of the returns of busy periods. That had to be measured, together with the cost of clipping jumps at practical n. Two bugs appeared on the way: (a) the first full grid run crashed at the end with "need at least one array to concatenate", because the block-length accumulator was created for every scenario but filled only for S1, S3 and S5; (b) `block_plugin` required at least two returns per block, so k = 1, which should reproduce RQ exactly, gave NaN.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** Every local-scale estimator converges at the `sqrt(n)` rate (slope of log RMSE on log n near minus 0.5); daily-scale rules do not; dropping with `sqrt(2 log n)` (PI-LU) beats clipping when there are jumps; the printed CV picks the smallest bandwidth for scale reasons alone; on real data local estimators agree with MedRQ and MinRQ better than MAD-truncated ones.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `run_experiment.py` parts `sim` (6 scenarios, 3 values of n, 15 estimators, 500 days per cell, two processes), `cv` (printed and corrected criteria against the oracle spot MSE, 300 days per cell) and `real`; the accumulator created only for the scenarios that use it; blocks with one return allowed (k = 1 now equals RQ, a test checks it), and the whole grid rerun after that change.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** The full grid (`q2_q4_sim_summary.csv`, `q2_rates.csv`, `q3_block_length.csv`, `q4_coverage.csv`), the CV study (`q3_cv_choice.csv`, `q3_iq_by_bandwidth.csv`) and the real-data run with a frequency and a sensitivity analysis (`q5_real_*.csv`).
* **RESULTS.** [SYNTHETIC] In S5 (strong stochastic volatility plus intraday pattern) Bapat's normalised estimator RK keeps a bias of minus 14.4% at n = 23,400 and its RMSE slope is minus 0.11 (minus 0.14 with the kappa correction); every local estimator has slope minus 0.48 to minus 0.52. With medium jumps (S3) PI-LU has the lowest RMSE (0.179 at n = 390, 0.082 at 1440); clipping versions carry a jump residual (PI-L3 plus 13.6% at n = 390). PI-LU coverage is 0.926 to 0.972 in all 18 cells. The printed CV picks h = 5 on 58% to 97% of days; the corrected CV tracks the oracle spot bandwidth, but that bandwidth oversmooths for the integrated quantity (KPI-L3 RMSE 0.250 at the corrected choice against 0.198 at h = 10, S5, n = 390). [REAL] RK and RK-k are 0.33 to 0.44 of MedRQ at the median day, the local plug-ins 0.66 to 0.73.
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** The rate and coverage claims are now measured, not argued; the CV analysis separates the scale artefact of the printed criterion from the deeper point that spot-optimal and IQ-optimal smoothing differ.
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** The real-data gap was not closed: the local plug-ins still sit 27% to 34% below MedRQ, and on real data, unlike in the smooth simulations, both knobs matter: with the cut-off fixed at 3.81, shortening the block raises PI-LU from 0.58 (k = 160) to 0.77 (k = 2) of MedRQ on BTC, and raising the cut-off from 3 to no truncation raises it from 0.59 to 0.87 (`q5_real_sensitivity.csv`). Either the fourth-power estimators are biased up on real data, or the plug-ins remove real volatility.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** Find out which, since the answer decides which estimator to recommend.

## Iteration 3. Raised bar: volatility bursts, a calibrated model and the neighbour guard

* **OBJECTIVE.** Explain the real-data gap, decide who is right, and if the plug-ins are wrong, repair them without losing jump robustness.
* **PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The sensitivity of iteration 2 says that real volatility moves within blocks of tens of minutes and that a sizeable share of quarticity sits in returns beyond 3.8 local standard deviations. Under the semimartingale model those returns can be jumps (to be removed) or parts of short volatility bursts (to be kept). The two give different signatures: isolated jumps leave squared standardised returns uncorrelated and have ordinary neighbours; bursts make large returns cluster.
* **HYPOTHESIS.** The large returns cluster; a volatility factor with a half-life of minutes reproduces both the clustering and the estimator ratios; in such a model the scale-truncated estimators are the ones that are wrong, and a threshold that also looks at a return's nearest neighbours fixes them.
* **CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** `run_experiment.py` part `fastvol`: burst diagnostics (acf of `min(z^2, 25)` at lags 1, 5, 10; trimmed kurtosis; share beyond 3; share of exceedances beyond `sqrt(2 log n)` with a neighbour beyond 2), a two-factor log-OU simulator on a fine grid with the exact IQ, a 3 by 3 calibration grid (fast-factor sd 0.5, 0.75, 1; half-life 1, 3, 10 minutes; 100 days each), and the evaluation of every estimator on the chosen model. `riq.py`: `neighbour_scale` (median of the six nearest absolute returns, the return itself excluded), `nn_block` (`k = round(n^(1/4))`) and the scale option `local+neighbour`; the new estimator PI-NN. One test that the guard keeps a burst and still drops an isolated jump. Figure 3.
* **EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Diagnostics on the real data and on the grid (`q6_burst_diagnostics.csv`); the chosen model at n = 390, 1440, 23,400 with and without jumps, 300 days per cell (`q6_fastvol_estimators.csv`); 1-minute against 5-minute (`q6_fastvol_frequency.csv`); block length with and without the guard (`q6_fastvol_block_length.csv`). **Failed attempt (exploratory, not saved):** a squared-return cross-validation of the window length on the burst model and on 60 BTC days, to see whether CV could detect bursts and pick short windows by itself. It picked long windows in both, as on the smooth model, so it cannot see minute-scale bursts. **Design of PI-NN (exploratory, not saved, seeds different from the reported ones):** k in {2, 5, 10} crossed with 4 or 6 neighbours, on the burst model with and without jumps and on S3 and S4; six neighbours were kept because a single jump among them moves their median less than among four, and `k = n^(1/4)` as a compromise between burst resolution and efficiency.
* **RESULTS.** [REAL] 65% (BTC) and 44% (DOGE) of exceedances have a neighbour beyond 2 local sd, and the lag-1 acf of capped `z^2` is 0.114 and 0.099, fading by lag 10. A model without a fast factor gives 0% and minus 0.008. [SYNTHETIC] The grid point sd 0.75, half-life 3 minutes gives 41% and 0.120 and reproduces the real ratios to MedRQ (PI-LU 0.68 against 0.66 to 0.69, PI-NN 0.95 against 0.95 to 0.96) and the 1-minute over 5-minute ratio of MedRQ (1.41 against 1.33 to 1.45). In that model every scale-truncated estimator is far too low (median error RK minus 0.57 to minus 0.59, PI-LU minus 0.26 to minus 0.49), and PI-NN has the lowest RMSE of the robust estimators at every n: 0.39, 0.28, 0.07 without jumps, 0.36, 0.26, 0.07 with jumps. The guard is what matters: at n = 1440 and k = 5 the bias is minus 19% with it, minus 34% without it. The price: with isolated jumps and smooth volatility at n = 390, PI-NN is worse than PI-LU (S3 RMSE 0.320 against 0.179), and its eighth-power estimate is not robust (bias 1.65 in S3 at n = 390).
* **IMPROVEMENTS.** The prototype now has two estimators with a stated domain each (PI-LU for smooth volatility with isolated jumps, PI-NN for bursty minute data), a diagnostic that tells them apart on a given data set, and a concrete warning that agreement with a 5-minute benchmark is not evidence of accuracy (in the model, 5-minute MedRQ is 0.57 of the true IQ at the median).
* **REMAINING ISSUES.** PI-NN's settings were chosen after seeing the real data and the burst model, so its numbers are not out of sample in the strict sense. The burst reading is a model fit, not a proof: clustered small jumps or microstructure effects could give similar signatures, and the daily-scale estimators fit the real data less well (RK 0.53 in the model against 0.33 to 0.42 real). No CLT for either estimator, and none at all for the guard. At one-minute sampling every estimator is still 9% to 40% low in the model; only finer sampling resolves within-minute bursts.
* **NEXT ITERATION.** For a paper: a CLT for PI-LU with the data-driven threshold (extending Jacod and Rosenbaum 2013), then the guard; an out-of-sample check of PI-NN on other coins, another venue and another period with the settings frozen; a model with microstructure noise.
