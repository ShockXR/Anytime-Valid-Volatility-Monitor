## Iteration 1. What a legitimate adversary is, and a first adversary

**OBJECTIVE.** Find out how close a law that satisfies every condition of Theorem B (`docs/math.md`, 5.4) can push the full default two-sided detector (six increase bets, five decrease bets, each side at 2A, A = 7800) to its bound m / A.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** E9 measured 1.1% to 1.4% per day for four natural laws against 5%, and E11 (Q11d) showed that a single bet can be pushed to 95% of its bound by an exact dynamic programme, while the best i.i.d. law found for one side of the default grid reached 42%. Nobody had searched for the worst law for the full two-sided grid, nor allowed the law to depend on the detector's own state, which the theorem permits.

**HYPOTHESIS.** (1) Only the law of Y = X / m0 on [0, B] matters, with B = τ² / m0, and the adversary must keep E[Y_t | F_{t-1}] = 1 exactly (the two-sided null with band 0). (2) Both statistics are affine in Y_t given the past, so, as in E11's Lemma 2a, a two-point law with mean 1 is optimal at each step: the adversary's choice at each step is a pair (y_low, y_high). (3) "Bold play" from gambling theory should be close to optimal: use the widest spread {0, B} (it makes every bet's wealth either grow or collapse as fast as possible), and when one draw can take a side exactly to 2A, put the high (or low) point exactly there, so that no wealth is wasted as overshoot.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** A vectorised engine that runs the detector on Y with a per-step callback (`engine`), the bold-play policy with exact landing (`bold_policy`; the landing point is closed form because each side's statistic is affine in Y), and an 8-parameter family around it: the high point is 1 + (B - 1) clip(θ0 + θ1 zu + θ2 zd + θ3 tl, 0, 1) and the low point clip(θ4 + θ5 zu + θ6 zd + θ7 tl, 0, 1), with zu, zd the log statistics over log 2A and tl the share of time left; θ = (1, 0, ..., 0) is bold play.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Pilot (scratch seeds 1 to 6 and 99, never reused): bold play on the single bet 0.5 at level A, to compare with E11's dynamic programme; bold play on the two-sided default; a grid of constant spreads {y_low, y_high}; each side alone at 2A; a first look at five days and at B from 2 to 39.

**RESULTS.** Pilot numbers, used only to choose the design (the reported values are in iterations 4 and 5): bold play reached about 0.90 of the bound for the single bet 0.5 where the dynamic programme reaches 0.95, so the policy family is close to optimal where the optimum is known. On the two-sided default it reached about twice the Gaussian rate at one day, and among constant spreads {0, B} was the best. Each side alone reached a bit over half (increase) and under half (decrease) of its own budget m / (2A), and the two-sided rate was close to the sum of the two.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** A strong, simple adversary with a known relation to the single-bet optimum.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** Is there a better state-dependent policy? Does B matter? Is the remaining slack structural?

**NEXT ITERATION.** Policy search with common random numbers.

## Iteration 2. Policy search: the in-sample trap

**OBJECTIVE.** Search the 8-parameter family properly and decide how results are reported.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The false alarm rate is a small probability (2% to 3% at one day), so with 5,000 paths its Monte Carlo standard error is about 0.2 points, and the maximum over many random parameter draws is biased upwards.

**HYPOTHESIS.** Random search on a few thousand paths will pick lucky draws; Nelder-Mead on common random numbers (the same uniforms for every parameter value) will make the objective a fixed function of θ, but its value at the optimum is still an in-sample maximum.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Pilot search (scratch): 40 random parameter draws on 5,000 paths, Nelder-Mead (150 evaluations) on 20,000 common paths from the two best and from bold play; then the best and bold play on 40,000 fresh paths.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** As above.

**RESULTS.** Pilot: the best random draw looked better than bold play on its 5,000 paths but was worse on the 20,000 common paths; Nelder-Mead from it did not reach bold play; Nelder-Mead from bold play did not move (the clipped corner is flat), so the search returned bold play itself.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** Rule fixed for the reported runs: every reported rate is from fresh seeds that the search never saw (16900 to 16988 in part a, 16991 to 17016 for the plug-in part; the searches use 16101 to 16135 and 16300 to 16436); the in-sample value is kept in the CSV files only as `train_rate`. Random search is used only to choose starting points, on a quarter of the paths.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The family may be too narrow. A "hold, then bold" family (Y = 1 for a while, so that every bet accumulates wealth t without risk, then bold play) is added, because the time profile of the policy is not covered well by a clipped linear function of the share of time left.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Smoke runs of every part.

## Iteration 3. Smoke runs: three fixes before the full run

**OBJECTIVE.** Run every part at 2% to 5% of its size (`E16_SCALE`, output to a scratch folder via `E16_OUT`) and check the tests.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** (1) Returns rounded to a tick of 1.25 sd are zero 47% of the time, so about 9% of 390-step calibration windows have a median absolute return of 0 and the plug-in calibration is degenerate (the monitor would extend the window; the vectorised plug-in does not implement that). (2) The truncated mean of Student t by numerical quadrature made one plug-in run take 40 seconds. (3) Price-level ticks up to 2 sd showed no failure and almost no conditional mean drift, so the map did not reach the region where the assumption breaks. (4) Several fresh evaluations would have held more than 1 GB in memory.

**HYPOTHESIS.** (1) Ticks up to 0.9 sd (35% zeros) never give a degenerate window at 390 steps. (2) T² / (ν + T²) is Beta(1/2, ν/2) distributed, which gives E[T²; |T| < s] = ν / (ν - 2) times a regularised incomplete beta function, in closed form. (3) Larger ticks (3 to 6 sd) make price rounding behave like a bid-ask bounce.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Tick grid of the plug-in part capped at 0.9 sd (the true-baseline part keeps ticks up to 1.3 sd, where no calibration is needed); `t_trunc_mean` in closed form (checked against quadrature to about 1e-15); price-tick grid extended to 6 sd; all fresh evaluations run in chunks of at most 8 million numbers.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** Smoke runs of a1, a2, a3, b1, b2, b3 and the figures; `tests/test_e16_adversarial.py` (20 tests: every searched law keeps the conditional mean, the return-law families use their true τ and m0, the vectorised detector matches `VolatilityMonitor` alarm for alarm with the true baseline and with the plug-in baseline, and a time-varying baseline matches a plain loop).

**RESULTS.** All tests pass; the smoke numbers are not reported (too few paths).

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The full run fits in about an hour on two cores.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** None known before the full run.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Full run of part a and part b, in parallel (two processes, `nice -n 10`).

## Iteration 4. Full run, part a: inside the class

**OBJECTIVE.** Find the worst legitimate law for the full default detector at one and five days, on fresh seeds, and explain the slack that remains.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** The pilot put bold play at about half of the bound. Three questions were open: does a searched state-dependent policy beat it on fresh paths, does B matter, and is the rest of the slack structural?

**HYPOTHESIS.** (1) Bold play is close to the best policy for the two-sided default, as it is for single bets. (2) Because B enters the decrease bets through 1 / (B - 1), small B could help the adversary. (3) The remaining slack is wealth stranded on paths that do not alarm (F2 in the identity of `docs/theory_delay.md`, 8.1), not overshoot, because bold play lands exactly on 2A.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** None to the method. After the i.i.d. search put its optimum at B = 2.05, the lower edge of the searched range [2, 40], part a4 was added (bold play and the i.i.d. law {0, B} at B = 1.2, 1.5, 1.9 and 2), run separately with its own fresh seeds.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** a1 (2,246 s on a shared machine): six i.i.d. families, searched on 20,000 (one day) and 6,000 (five days) common paths, re-run on 200,000 and 60,000 fresh paths, plus the Gaussian reference. a2 (1,019 s): shape switching (16 grid points), the 8-parameter policy search (30 and 16 random starts on a quarter of the paths, Nelder-Mead with 120 and 50 evaluations), hold-then-bold, fresh evaluation; bold play at 7 values of B (60,000 fresh paths of five days each) and horizon curves to m = A (30,000 paths of 7,800 steps). a3 (79 s): decomposition on 100,000 fresh paths per law; single bets and each side alone on 41,020 fresh paths. a4 (206 s): 60,000 fresh paths per B.

**RESULTS.** (1) Held: the searched policy looked better in sample (2.82% against 2.63% at one day) but was not better on fresh seeds (2.37% against 2.40%; five days 17.2% against 17.6%); hold-then-bold was within noise of bold play (2.39%; 17.8% after a hold of 1,000 steps). (2) Partly: the one-day rate peaks near B = 2 (2.62%, interval 2.50% to 2.75%; an independent run 2.67%) and falls slowly on both sides, to 2.40% at B = 1.2 and 2.03% at B = 39; at five days the maximum is 18.1% at B = 3 and 5. (3) Held: under bold play F1 = 0.99, F3 = 1.00 and F2 = 0.49, with 60% of the stranded wealth in the decrease bets and 36% in the three smallest increase bets. For single bets bold play matched E11's dynamic programme within 0.01 to 0.03 of the bound at one to ten days, and beat it for the bet 0.05 (0.26 against 0.18 at one day). No rate was above the bound anywhere in part a.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** The worst legitimate law is now pinned down to about 0.52 of the bound at one day and 0.73 at five days for the default detector, against 0.19 and 0.50 for Gaussian data.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** A search gives lower bounds only; a certified upper bound below m / A for the default grid would need an exact computation over an 11-dimensional state.

**NEXT ITERATION.** Part b.

## Iteration 5. Full run, part b: outside the class

**OBJECTIVE.** Map where the bound fails when a condition of Theorem B is broken, by how much, and check that each failure is explained by the broken condition and not by something else.

**PROBLEM IDENTIFIED.** E9 showed that GARCH clustering breaks the guarantee, E4e and E14 that a plug-in baseline does, and ALT1 that price noise does, each at a few settings. What was missing is the boundary (how much of the broken condition it takes) and a control that isolates the broken condition.

**HYPOTHESIS.** (1) GARCH: what matters is the drift of the conditional mean μ_t = E[X_t | past] away from m0, so the same detector betting against the true μ_t (known in closed form for Gaussian innovations) is back inside the class and stays below the bound. (2) Plug-in: the excess comes from the error of m-hat against the true mean at the estimated level τ-hat, so the detector with τ-hat and the true mean m(τ-hat) (the "τ-hat oracle") stays below the bound, and the plug-in's false alarms sit in the tails of the baseline error. (3) Price-level ticks make returns dependent: after a zero return the next squared return is smaller on average, after a one-tick move larger, so large ticks break the conditional mean condition while rounding each return independently does not.

**CHANGES IMPLEMENTED.** Parts b1, b2 and b3 as described in the script docstring. The τ-hat oracle and the full oracle run on the same paths as the plug-in. A shape adversary for the plug-in: after calibration on Gaussian returns, each monitored X is the law's own draw with probability q and the constant m(τ-hat) otherwise, which keeps the conditional mean.

**EXPERIMENTS PERFORMED.** b1: 64 GARCH cells, 6,000 paths each, five days. b2: 37 return laws per calibration length (390 and 1440) searched on 6,000 paths, the worst per family and horizon re-run on 30,000 fresh paths; shape adversary at q = 1, 0.5, 0.2, 0.05, 0 (30,000 paths each); rates by decile of the baseline error. b3: 11 tick sizes from 0.05 to 6 sd, 10,000 paths each, plus 1,000 paths of 10,000 steps per tick size for τ, m0, the conditional means and the lag-one correlations. Run time 582 s (b1), 1,502 s (b2) and 132 s (b3) on a shared machine.

**RESULTS.** All three hypotheses held (numbers in the report, Q16d to Q16f). GARCH: the bound is exceeded (lower end of the 95% interval above it) in 43 of 64 cells at one day and 51 of 64 at five days; the mean absolute drift of the conditional mean predicts the failure almost perfectly (Spearman correlation 0.99 with the ratio to the bound at one day); the detector betting against the true μ_t never exceeds 0.24 (one day) or 0.56 (five days) of the bound. Plug-in: with i.i.d. returns no law found pushes the one-day rate significantly above 5% at a 390-step calibration, but every law searched gives 40% to 44% at five days (bound 25%; 6,000 paths per law, and 41% to 42% for the worst law of each family on fresh paths); the τ-hat oracle never exceeds 0.59 of the bound; the shape adversary pushes the one-day rate to 32.5% (calibration 390) and 16.1% (calibration 1440) while the τ-hat oracle on the same paths has no false alarm. Price ticks: below the bound up to 3 sd, above it from 4 sd (five days) and 6 sd (one and five days), with the conditional mean after a zero return at 0.88 and 0.82 of m0; rounding each return independently stays at or below 0.56 of the bound.

**IMPROVEMENTS.** Each failure now has a measured size of the broken assumption next to it, and a control inside the class on the same paths.

**REMAINING ISSUES.** The GARCH map uses Gaussian innovations (μ_t is then exact); Student t innovations would need μ_t by numerical integration. The plug-in search covers four i.i.d. families and one shape adversary; a shape adversary that also uses the detector's state was not tried (the q = 0 case already makes the baseline error deterministic).
