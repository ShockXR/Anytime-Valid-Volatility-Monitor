# Blind segments of the confidence-sequence baseline, and a proved guard (E24)

E19 found that a segment of the monitor with the confidence-sequence baseline (`baseline="cs"`, `docs/math.md`, section 6) can be unable to raise an increase alarm for its whole life, and E23 found the same failure on fresh data: on BTC in 2026 the primary monitor P1 (`PeriodicVolatilityMonitor(baseline="cs", delta=0.01, A = 36,000, period=1440)`, band b = 0.25) could not alarm upward for 28% of its monitored time, one segment for 47 days, and three of the sixteen releases fell in such segments (`docs/report_parts/E23.md`). E19 proposed a guard (discard the window and calibrate again) and left it unproved, because the windows kept are then selected by their bounds. This note proves a version of that guard, shows by a construction that the selection does matter, and states what the alternatives (a time-out and a fallback to the rank monitor) guarantee. Code: `src/avmonitor/guard.py`. Tests: `tests/test_guard.py`. Experiment: `experiments/e24_blind_guard.py`, report part `docs/report_parts/E24.md`.

Labels: **[PROVED]** (a complete proof is given here, using only Theorem 6.2 of `docs/math.md`, Ville's inequality, optional stopping at bounded stopping times, and Proposition B.1 of `docs/theory_running_tau.md` for the rank fallback), **[NUMERICAL]** (exact arithmetic or Monte Carlo of a closed-form quantity), **[SYNTHETIC]** (simulation of the monitors).

## 0. Summary

1. **The blind condition is decided when calibration ends** [PROVED, Proposition 1]. A segment is blind when `τ² ≤ m↑ = (1 + b) Ū_T`; then every increase factor is at most 1, the increase side cannot alarm within 2A - 1 monitored steps of a (re)start, and with P1's daily restart (period m = 1440, carry 0, m < 2A) it can never alarm. Both τ² and Ū_T are known at the end of calibration T, a stopping time, so the event is in `F_T`. More generally, with `ρ = τ² / m↑ ≤ 1 + η` ("near-blind") every increase factor is at most `1 + λ η`, the expected log growth of every increase bet is at most `log(1 + λ_max η)` under any law, and no increase alarm can come within `d_min(ρ)` steps of a restart, an exact floor attained by `X ≡ τ²` (Proposition 2). For P1 (A = 36,000, the default grid with λ_max = 0.5) the increase side cannot alarm within a day for any data when `ρ ≤ 1.0105`; `d_min` is 1503 steps at ρ = 1.01, 371 at 1.05, 91 at 1.25 and 30 at 2.
2. **A retry guard with error spending** [PROVED, Theorem 1]. If the window just calibrated is (near-)blind, discard it and calibrate again on the next returns, with error budget δ_k for the k-th window of the segment, `δ_1 + ... + δ_K ≤ δ`, at most K windows, then fall back. Then P(the bounds of the kept window fail) ≤ `Σ_k δ_k P(window k is tried) ≤ δ`, given everything before the segment. The proof uses only that whether window k is tried is decided by the time its sequence starts (`T_{k-1} ≤ σ_k`), and that each sequence starts at a stopping time with its own budget δ_k.
3. **Choosing which window to keep does break per-window coverage** [PROVED, Proposition 3]. There is a law (adaptive; inside the tolerance relative to the kept window, not relative to the discarded ones) under which a window before the K-th is kept exactly when its upper bound fails: given that such a window is kept, its bound fails with probability 1. With no cap on the number of windows (E19's proposal) the kept window's bound then fails with probability 1. With a cap K and the same budget δ for every window it fails with probability `1 - Π_k (1 - p(δ))`, about K times the single-window failure probability p(δ). For P1's window p(δ) is about 1/20 of δ/2, so on this law the naive guard with K = 3 stays below δ (7.6 × 10⁻⁴ against 0.01) and exceeds δ only from K = 40. Spending δ over the windows makes the bound hold for every law and every K.
4. **Theorem 6.4, Corollary 6.6 and Corollary 1 hold unchanged** [PROVED, Theorem 2], with δ the total budget, T the end of the kept window, and the tolerance (H↑), (H↓) relative to the kept window's confidence-sequence part. The tolerance is an almost-sure hypothesis about conditional means, so the selection does not enter the proofs; but which window is kept depends on the sequence's output (δ_k, the bets, η), so this null depends on the guard's tuning, and it differs from the unguarded monitor's null (Remark 3). A sufficient condition that does not is the tolerance relative to each of the K windows the guard may try ((H*), section 1), which implies both the guarded and the unguarded hypotheses. It holds under a constant conditional law from the end of the first window's τ part on (Corollary 6.5 of `docs/math.md`), which covers a step in volatility inside that τ part.
5. **Alternatives** [PROVED, Proposition 4, Theorem 3]. A time-out after D periods without an alarm keeps every per-segment bound and costs `δ / D` alarms per monitored period in the long-run bound: `E[N_M] ≤ (δ (1 + ⌊M / (D m)⌋) + M / A) / (1 - δ)`. A fallback to E14's awake rank monitor after K blind windows gives the mixed guarantee `P(an alarm within m monitored steps of a segment) ≤ Σ_k δ_k + m / A`, where the cs branch needs the tolerance relative to the kept window and the rank branch needs the returns after the fallback time to be exchangeable given the past; the per-period and long-run bounds carry over, the latter with a per-segment proof because the two branches live in different filtrations.
6. **A calendar day is not a period of a segment** [PROVED upper bounds; not specific to the guard, Remark 6 and Corollary 2]. Corollary 1 bounds a given period of a given segment. For a fixed calendar day d the proof gives `δ E[number of segments started by the end of day d] + (m + κ) / A`, which is at most `δ (1 + (δ + d m / A) / (1 - δ)) + (m + κ) / A` for the d-th day under the tolerance in every segment (5.05% on day 1 and 6.02% on day 25 for P1); whether a null attains more than `δ + (m + κ) / A` on some day is open. The share of monitored periods with an alarm, as reported in E22 and E23, is a third quantity: by counting, its long-run expectation is at most `(m + κ) / (A (1 - δ))`, 4.04% for P1 (Corollary 2).
7. **Recommended guard** (section 6; the monitor chosen from the synthetic experiments, before any real-data rerun; η = 0.25 was set before those experiments, after a scratch look at P1's real BTC windows): K = 3 windows with budgets δ/2, δ/4, δ/4, near-blind margin η = 0.25, and the fallback to the rank monitor. With η = 0 (E19's criterion) near-blind windows (ρ ≈ 1.03 to 1.2) are kept and almost never detect a later doubling of the volatility; with η = 0.25 they are replaced, at the cost of one extra calibration day where the first window is near-blind.

## 1. Set-up

As in `docs/math.md`, 6.1 to 6.3. Returns `r_1, r_2, ...`, `F_t = σ(r_1, ..., r_t)` (with the monitor's tie-breaking draw U_t added to F_t at the step t where it is used; the draws are i.i.d. uniform and independent of the returns, so conditional means of the returns are unchanged). A **segment** starts at a stopping time S (the start of the stream, or the step after an alarm). Its calibration consists of one or more **windows**. Window k starts at a stopping time `S_{k-1}` (`S_0 = S`). Its τ part is the next `n_τ` returns (longer if they are degenerate, `monitor.py`), which fix `τ_k` at a stopping time `σ_k`. Its confidence sequence (CS) part runs on `X_t = min(r_t², τ_k²)` for `t > σ_k` and ends at a stopping time `T_k > σ_k`, where the upper and lower bounds `Ū^{(k)}`, `L̄^{(k)}` are read; their error levels are `δ_k / 2` each. The next window, if any, starts at `S_k = T_k`. So the windows are disjoint, consecutive, and each starts at a stopping time.

The window that is used for monitoring is the **kept window** κ, with `T = T_κ`, `m↑ = (1 + b) Ū^{(κ)}_T` and `m↓ = (1 - b) L̄^{(κ)}_T`. The monitoring rule, the bets, the two-sided alarm and the optional restart at period boundaries are those of `docs/math.md` 6.3 and `docs/theory_per_period.md` section 3. `μ_t = E[X_t | F_{t-1}]` with the kept window's τ, and `ν̄↑`, `ν̄↓` are the bet-weighted calibration averages of Theorem 6.2. The **good event** of window k is `G_k = {ν̄↑_{T_k} < U^{(k)}_{T_k}} ∩ {ν̄↓_{T_k} > L^{(k)}_{T_k}}` (normalised units, as in Theorem 6.4), and `G = G_κ`.

The **tolerance relative to the kept window** (horizon h) is (H↑) and (H↓) of `docs/math.md` 6.3 with T = `T_κ`, the minimum and maximum taken over the CS part `σ_κ < i ≤ T_κ` of the kept window, and `μ` computed with `τ_κ`:

* (H↑) `sup_{T_κ < t ≤ T_κ + h} μ_t ≤ (1 + b) min_{σ_κ < i ≤ T_κ} μ_i`, and (H↓) `inf_{T_κ < t ≤ T_κ + h} μ_t ≥ (1 - b) max_{σ_κ < i ≤ T_κ} μ_i`, almost surely.

The **tolerance relative to every window** (H*) (horizon h): for every k ≤ K, (H↑) and (H↓) hold relative to window k (T = T_k, the minimum and maximum over window k's CS part, μ computed with τ_k) for `T_k < t ≤ T_k + h`, almost surely, where window k is the k-th window the guard would build from S (defined on every path, whether or not it is tried). (H*) depends on the law, c, b, K and the window lengths, not on δ_k, η or the bets. It implies the tolerance relative to the kept window (κ ≤ K), and its k = 1 part is the hypothesis of the unguarded monitor.

## 2. The blind condition

**Lemma 1 (a blind segment cannot alarm upward) [PROVED].** If `τ² ≤ m↑`, every increase factor `1 + λ (X_t / m↑ - 1)` is at most 1, and each increase bet's Shiryaev-Roberts statistic satisfies `R_t ≤ R_{t-1} + 1`, so `R_t ≤ t - t₀` for t monitored steps after a (re)start at `t₀`. The increase side alarms only when the average of its bets reaches 2A, so it cannot alarm within 2A - 1 monitored steps of a (re)start; with a restart every m steps and a carry of at most κ it can never alarm when m + κ < 2A (P1: m = 1440, κ = 0, 2A = 72,000).

*Proof.* `X_t ≤ τ² ≤ m↑` gives `X_t / m↑ - 1 ≤ 0`, and λ ≥ 0. Then `R_t = f_t (R_{t-1} + 1) ≤ R_{t-1} + 1`, and `R_{t₀} = 0`. An average of numbers each at most `t - t₀` is at most `t - t₀`. With a carry, the side average is at most κ after each restart, so within a period of m steps it stays at most `κ + m`. ∎

The decrease side is not affected: in E23 both blind BTC segments ended with a down alarm.

**Proposition 1 (decided at a stopping time) [PROVED].** For a fixed `η ≥ 0`, the near-blind event `B = {τ² ≤ (1 + η) m↑}` of a window is in `F_T`, and `T_B = T` on B, `T_B = ∞` off B, is a stopping time. With η = 0 this is the blind event of Lemma 1, E19's criterion.

*Proof.* τ is `F_σ`-measurable with σ < T, and `Ū_T = τ² min(U_T, 1)` is a function of the CS observations up to T and of the predictable bets, hence `F_T`-measurable; b and η are constants. For every t, `{T_B = t} = {T = t} ∩ B ∈ F_t` because `B ∩ {T = t} ∈ F_t` for `B ∈ F_T`. ∎

Since τ, `m↑` and the bets stay fixed for the life of the segment, B is also the event that the segment is (near-)blind from start to end: blindness is never discovered later.

In normalised units the condition reads `min(U_T, 1) ≥ 1 / ((1 + η)(1 + b))`: with b = 0.25 and η = 0, the upper bound must reach 80% of τ². For i.i.d. normal returns `E[X] / τ² ≈ 0.11` (c = 3), so this needs most CS-part returns to exceed τ: the CS part must be far more volatile than the τ part. With normal returns a step in standard deviation by a factor k after the τ part gives a median `τ² / m↑` of 2.09 (k = 2), 1.20 (k = 4), 1.03 (k = 6) and 0.96 (k = 8) for P1's configuration (0.96 for k = 8 with Student t3 returns; 0.88 for E24's tick design `frozen`) [SYNTHETIC, 800 paths each, `results/e24_synthetic.csv`, column `ratio_first_window_median`, monitor U]. So a step of 2 to 4 in standard deviation does not make P1 blind; a step of 6 makes about 13% of the windows blind and most of the others near-blind (ρ ≈ 1.03, below the floor of Proposition 2 for a day only when ρ ≤ 1.0105, but with an increase side that gains at most log(1.015) per step).

**Proposition 2 (power of a near-blind increase side) [PROVED].** Let `ρ = τ² / m↑` and let λ ∈ [0, 1) be an increase bet.

(a) Every factor satisfies `1 + λ (X_t / m↑ - 1) ≤ 1 + λ (ρ - 1)`, with equality exactly when `X_t = τ²`.

(b) For bets `λ_1, ..., λ_K` averaged with equal weights, the largest possible value of the side average d monitored steps after a (re)start, over all data, is `a_d(ρ) = (1/K) Σ_k Σ_{i=1}^{d} (1 + λ_k (ρ - 1))^i`, attained by `X ≡ τ²`. So the increase side cannot alarm before `d_min(ρ) = min{d : a_d(ρ) ≥ 2A}` steps after a (re)start, and this floor is attained. With a restart every m steps and carry 0, the increase side can never alarm if `d_min(ρ) > m`.

(c) For any law, `E[log f_t | F_{t-1}] ≤ log(1 + λ (μ_t / m↑ - 1)) ≤ log(1 + λ (ρ - 1))`, so each increase bet's expected log growth per step, the rate at which it gathers evidence after any change, is at most `log(1 + λ_max η)` when `ρ ≤ 1 + η`. An increase can be detected at all only if the post-change truncated mean exceeds `m↑`, and the truncated mean can never exceed τ²: the side sees at most a factor ρ above its threshold.

*Proof.* (a) `X_t ≤ τ² = ρ m↑`. (b) The recursion `R_t = f_t (R_{t-1} + 1)` is nondecreasing in `f_t` and in `R_{t-1}` (both nonnegative), so by induction every `R_t` is at most the value obtained with every factor at its maximum `g_k = 1 + λ_k (ρ - 1)`, which is `Σ_{i=1}^{d} g_k^i`; the data `X ≡ τ²` attain it for every bet at once. (c) Jensen's inequality for the concave logarithm, then (a) applied to `μ_t ≤ τ²`. ∎

For P1 (A = 36,000, the default grid 0.02 to 0.5): `d_min = 72,000` at ρ = 1, 9854 at 1.001, 2703 at 1.005, 1503 at 1.01, 827 at 1.02, 371 at 1.05, 202 at 1.1, 91 at 1.25, 51 at 1.5 and 30 at 2; P1's increase side is blind in effect (no alarm possible within a day, whatever the data) for `ρ ≤ 1.0105` [NUMERICAL, exact recursion, `results/e24_near_blind.csv`]. The floor of (b) is weak above ρ ≈ 1.05, because it is attained only when every return exceeds τ; the rate bound (c) is the useful one there: with ρ = 1.25 the largest bet gains at most `log 1.125 = 0.118` nats per step against `log 3 = 1.10` at a typical ρ = 5.

## 3. The retry guard

**The guard.** Fix in advance an integer `K ≥ 1`, budgets `δ_1, ..., δ_K > 0` with `Σ_k δ_k ≤ δ`, and `η ≥ 0`. For k = 1, 2, ...: calibrate window k with budget δ_k; if it is not near-blind (`τ_k² > (1 + η) m↑^{(k)}`), keep it; if it is near-blind and k < K, discard it and start window k + 1 at the next return; if k = K, fall back: either keep window K anyway ("keep"), or switch to the rank monitor for the segment ("rank", Theorem 3). After an alarm the next segment starts again with window 1. Default schedule (`guard.spending_schedule`, "halving"): `δ_k = δ 2^{-k}` for k < K and `δ_K = δ_{K-1}`, so the budgets sum to δ; K = 1 gives `δ_1 = δ`, the unguarded monitor.

**Lemma 2 (Theorem 6.2 at a stopping time) [PROVED].** Let σ be a stopping time, let τ be `F_σ`-measurable, and run the sequence of `docs/math.md` 6.2 on `Z_t = min(r_t², τ²) / τ²`, `t > σ`, with predictable bets. Then on `{σ < ∞}`, `P(ν̄↑_t ≥ U_t for some t > σ | F_σ) ≤ δ_u` and the same for the lower bound with `δ_l`.

*Proof.* Put `K_t = 1` for `t ≤ σ` and multiply, for `t > σ`, by the factor `exp(λ↑_t (ν_t - Z_t) - ψ↑_t e_t)` of the proof of Theorem 6.2. Since `{σ < t} ∈ F_{t-1}` and τ, the centre and the bet are `F_{t-1}`-measurable on that event, the computation of Theorem 6.2 gives `E[K_t / K_{t-1} | F_{t-1}] ≤ 1`, so K is a nonnegative supermartingale with `K_σ = 1`. For `A ∈ F_σ`, `(1_A K_{σ+j})_{j ≥ 0}` is a nonnegative supermartingale in `(F_{σ+j})` (optional sampling for nonnegative supermartingales), and Ville's inequality gives `P(A ∩ {sup_{t > σ} K_t ≥ 1/δ_u}) ≤ δ_u P(A ∩ {σ < ∞})`. The event in braces is the event that the bound fails at some t > σ (proof of Theorem 6.2). ∎

**Theorem 1 (the kept window's bounds) [PROVED].** For every segment with start S, on `{S < ∞}`,

`P(Gᶜ | F_S) ≤ Σ_{k=1}^{K} δ_k P(window k is tried | F_S) ≤ Σ_k δ_k ≤ δ`,

where `Gᶜ` is the event that a window is kept and its bounds fail (with the "keep" fallback a window is always kept; with the "rank" fallback none is kept when all K windows are blind). The discard rule may be any rule decided at `T_k` from the data up to `T_k`; near-blindness is one.

*Proof.* Let `W_k = {window k is tried} = {the windows 1, ..., k - 1 were all discarded}`, an event in `F_{T_{k-1}} ⊆ F_{σ_k}` (empty for k > K). If window k is kept and its bounds fail, then `W_k ∩ G_kᶜ` occurred, and `G_kᶜ` is contained in the event that window k's sequence fails at some t > σ_k (it is read at `T_k > σ_k`). By Lemma 2 applied at `σ_k` with `A = W_k ∩ A'`, `A' ∈ F_S`, `P(A' ∩ W_k ∩ G_kᶜ) ≤ (δ_k / 2 + δ_k / 2) P(A' ∩ W_k)`. A union over k gives `P(A' ∩ Gᶜ) ≤ Σ_k δ_k P(A' ∩ W_k)` for every `A' ∈ F_S`. ∎

What the proof uses: whether window k is tried is known before its sequence starts (`W_k ∈ F_{σ_k}`), and window k's sequence is a fresh sequence started at its own stopping time; that the windows do not overlap is not needed; and each window has its own share of δ. It does not use, and does not give, coverage of the kept window given that it was kept.

**Proposition 3 (the selection breaks per-window coverage) [PROVED].** Fix c, b, η, K and the window length (τ part `n_τ`, CS part `n_cs`), and put `q = 1 / ((1 + η)(1 + b)) < 1` (any b > 0 or η > 0). Consider the adaptive law: in the τ part of every window the returns are `±s` with fair random signs (s > 0), so `τ = c × 1.4826 s` exactly; every other return (CS parts and monitoring) is `±τ` with probability q and 0 otherwise, independently of everything before. Then:

(a) the tolerance (H↑), (H↓) holds relative to the kept window of every segment, for every horizon up to the segment's alarm and every b ≥ 0 (μ_t = q τ² in every CS part and during monitoring). It fails relative to a discarded window k: the next n_τ steps are window k + 1's τ part, where `μ_t = s² = τ² / (1.4826 c)²`, below `(1 - b) q τ²` (for c = 3, b = 0.25 and η = 0: 0.051 τ² against 0.6 τ²). So the law is in the null of Theorem 2, but not in the selection-free null (H*) of section 1;
(b) a window is near-blind exactly when `min(U_T, 1) ≥ q`, and its upper bound fails exactly when `U_T ≤ q = ν̄↑_T`; so a window that is not near-blind has a failing upper bound, and `P(Gᶜ | window k kept) = 1` for every k < K with positive probability of being kept;
(c) with budgets `δ_1, ..., δ_K`, `P(Gᶜ) ≥ 1 - Π_{k=1}^{K} (1 - p(δ_k))`, where `p(δ) = P(U_T < q)` for one window's upper sequence at level δ / 2 on i.i.d. Bernoulli(q) data. With the same budget δ for every window (no spending) this is about `K p(δ)`, K times the single-window failure probability; it exceeds δ once K is about `δ / p(δ)`. The bound `Σ_k δ_k` of Theorem 1 is attained by this law only up to the factor `p(δ) / (δ / 2)` (1/19 to 1/32 for P1's window at δ = 0.01, 0.005 and 0.0025);
(d) with no cap on the number of windows and the same budget δ for every window (E19's proposal), `P(Gᶜ) = 1` whenever `p(δ) > 0`, as it is in every configuration below.

*Proof.* (a) `μ_t = E[min(r_t², τ²) | F_{t-1}] = q τ²` outside the τ parts; after the kept window every step up to the segment's alarm is a monitoring step, so no τ part follows it within the segment. (b) In normalised units `ν_i = q` for every CS observation, so `ν̄↑_T = q` whatever the bets; near-blind means `τ² ≤ (1 + η)(1 + b) τ² min(U_T, 1)`, that is `min(U_T, 1) ≥ q`; and `G↑ᶜ = {U_T ≤ ν̄↑_T} = {U_T ≤ q}`. A window k < K that is kept is not near-blind, so `U_T < q` and its upper bound fails. (c) The CS data of the windows are i.i.d. Bernoulli(q) sequences (times τ²), independent across windows, so the events `{U^{(k)}_{T_k} < q}` are independent with probabilities `p(δ_k)`. If one of them occurs, let k be the first: windows 1 to k - 1 have `U ≥ q` and are discarded, window k is not near-blind and is kept, and its upper bound fails. Hence `Gᶜ` contains the union of these events, whose probability is `1 - Π_k (1 - p(δ_k))`. (d) Without a cap the windows are i.i.d., and each is kept, with a failing upper bound by (b), with probability `p(δ) > 0`; so a window is kept after a geometric number of tries, almost surely, and its bound fails. The CS data of a window take finitely many values, so any sequence with `U_T < q` has positive probability; the simulations below find such sequences in every configuration. ∎

So E19's worry is right in this sense: conditioning on being kept destroys the per-window coverage; without a cap the kept window's bound fails with probability 1, and with a cap only the unconditional union over the windows tried survives. [NUMERICAL, `results/e24_selection.csv`] For P1's window (n_cs = 1080, δ = 0.01, b = 0.25, η = 0, so q = 0.8), one window's upper sequence fails with probability p(δ) = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ under the construction (400,000 replications per level; the budget δ/2 = 0.005 is conservative by a factor of 20 here); with three windows and δ for each, the kept window's bound fails with probability 7.6 × 10⁻⁴, three times as often but still 13 times below δ (the naive guard exceeds δ on this law from K = 40, the first K with `1 - (1 - p(δ))^K ≥ δ`); with the halving budgets of Theorem 1, 1.9 × 10⁻⁴. The factor between δ_k/2 and p(δ_k) is 20, 24 and 29 at δ_k = 0.01, 0.005 and 0.0025 in `e24_selection.csv`, and 19, 23 and 32 in the round 10 review's recomputation with 1,000,000 replications per level (`docs/review/round10_scripts/outputs/r10_selection.txt`, which gives K = 39 with its estimate p(δ) = 2.59 × 10⁻⁴). In an illustration with a large budget (n_cs = 150, δ = 0.3 per window) the formula gives 0.0139 for one window and 0.041 for three (the naive guard exceeds δ = 0.3 on this law from K = 26); a direct run of `GuardedVolatilityMonitor` on the construction (4000 paths, three windows, δ = 0.3 each) kept a window with a failing upper bound in 4.3% of paths (s.e. 0.32 points), against 4.1% from the formula with p = 0.0139 (4.26% with that run's own estimate, p = 0.0144 from another 200,000 replications). The construction makes the coverage event fail; whether that produces false alarms depends on how far the bound undershoots (the increase side drifts upward only if `(1 + b) U_T < q`), so the false alarm rate itself need not rise by the same factor. Theorem 6.4 charges the coverage event, which is what the proof needs.

**Theorem 2 (the guarantees hold unchanged) [PROVED].** Run the guard with any K, budgets with `Σ_k δ_k ≤ δ` and the "keep" fallback, and let T be the end of the kept window.

1. (Theorem 6.4 of `docs/math.md`.) Parts 1 to 4 hold as stated, with δ the total budget and (H↑), (H↓) relative to the kept window. In particular, under the tolerance with h = m, `P(an alarm within the first m monitored steps of the segment) ≤ δ + m / A`.
2. (Corollary 6.6.) If the tolerance holds with h = ∞ in every segment relative to that segment's kept window, then `E[N_M] ≤ (δ + M / A) / (1 - δ)` for the number `N_M` of alarms in the first M monitored steps. Calibration steps, including those of discarded windows, are not monitored steps.
3. (Corollary 1 of `docs/theory_per_period.md`.) With a restart at every period boundary (carry at most κ), for every period `(b, b']` of the segment with `T ≤ b` and `b' - b ≤ m`, if the tolerance holds relative to the kept window for `T < t ≤ b'`, then `P(the segment raises an alarm in (b, b']) ≤ δ + (m + κ) / A`, marginally.
4. Parts 1 to 3 hold in particular under (H*), which does not depend on which window is kept.

*Proof.* Part 4: on `{κ = k}`, (H*) gives the tolerance relative to window k, the kept window. Parts 1 to 3: each of the three proofs uses the calibration only through: (i) T is a stopping time; (ii) τ, `m↑`, `m↓` and the bets are `F_T`-measurable; (iii) G is in `F_T` and `P(Gᶜ) ≤ δ` (given `F_S` for Corollary 6.6); (iv) on G, the tolerance gives `μ_t ≤ (1 + b) min_i μ_i ≤ (1 + b) μ̄↑_T ≤ m↑` and the mirror inequality for the decrease side (Step 2 of Theorem 6.4). Under the guard, (i) `T = T_κ` with `{κ = k} = W_k ∩ B_kᶜ ∈ F_{T_k}` (B_k the near-blind event of window k; with the "keep" fallback `{κ = K} = W_K`), so `{T = t} = ∪_k {κ = k, T_k = t} ∈ F_t`; (ii) on `{κ = k}` these are window k's quantities, each `F_{T_k}`-measurable; (iii) `G = ∪_k ({κ = k} ∩ G_k) ∈ F_T`, and Theorem 1 gives the bound; (iv) on `{κ = k} ∩ G_k` the inequalities are those of Step 2 for window k, with the tolerance relative to window k. Steps 3 and 4, parts 2 to 4 of Theorem 6.4, the good-segment supermartingale of Corollary 6.6 and the supermartingale of Corollary 1 use nothing else. In Corollary 6.6 the bad-segment term uses `P(Gᶜ_j | F_{S_j}) ≤ δ` for segment j, which is Theorem 1 conditionally on the segment's start. ∎

**Remark 1 (cost of spending).** The first window gets `δ_1 < δ` (δ / 2 with halving), so its bounds are a little wider than the unguarded ones: the half-width scales roughly like `√log(2 / δ_1)`, a factor `√(log 400 / log 200) ≈ 1.06` for δ = 0.01. The total δ, and so the choice `A = m / (α - δ)`, is unchanged.

**Remark 2 (a retry without spending is a time-out with D = 0).** If each window kept its own δ, each window would be a segment of its own in the sense of Corollary 6.6; the long-run bound would charge δ per window tried, `E[N_bad] ≤ δ E[number of windows tried among the first M monitored steps]`, which no bound on the number of retries controls unless K is capped, and the per-segment bound would be `K δ + m / A`. Proposition 3 shows that the coverage failure does grow about K-fold on a suitable law (from p(δ) to about K p(δ), with p(δ) well below δ/2 for P1's window), and reaches probability 1 when the retries are not capped.

**Remark 3 (the tolerance relative to the kept window).** (H↑), (H↓) are almost-sure conditions on conditional means, which are properties of the law given the past; the proofs need them on every path, for the window that is kept on that path, and no conditional statement given the selection is used. So the selection does not break the hypothesis as a hypothesis. It does change what the hypothesis says:

* Which window is kept depends on `U_T`, so on δ_k, the predictable bets and η: two guards with different tunings can have different nulls on the same law. `docs/math.md` 6.3 keeps the bets out of the unguarded monitor's hypotheses; the selection brings them back.
* A sufficient condition that does not depend on which window is kept is (H*), the tolerance relative to every window that may be tried (section 1; Theorem 2, part 4). Under a constant conditional law from `σ_1` on (`docs/math.md`, Corollary 6.5; for example i.i.d. returns with any law), it holds for every window with b = 0, because `σ_k ≥ σ_1`. This covers the motivating case, a step in volatility inside the first window's τ part (design `step` of E24), for both the guarded and the unguarded monitor.
* The guarded and unguarded monitors test different nulls, and neither implies the other, but (H*) implies both. If volatility rises in the CS part of window 1 and stays, the tolerance fails relative to window 1 and holds relative to window 2. The guarded monitor is covered by Theorem 2 only on paths where window 1 is discarded (ρ_1 ≤ 1 + η): for P1 with η = 0.25, a rise of the standard deviation by a factor of 8 or more at the start of the CS part does that on every path, but a rise by any factor from 2 to 32 in the middle of the CS part leaves the median ρ_1 at 2.0 to 3.3, and window 1 is kept on every path [SYNTHETIC, round 10 review, `docs/review/round10_scripts/r10_tolerance_example.py`, 4000 paths per cell]. There both monitors are outside the null, and part 4 of Theorem 6.4 charges the guarded monitor `P(V_m) ≥ P(window 1 kept)`. If volatility falls during window 2 and comes back, the tolerance holds relative to window 1 but fails relative to window 2, and the guarded monitor may alarm, correctly relative to its own reference, where the unguarded would not.
* In part 4 of Theorem 6.4 the event `V_m` (the tolerance fails within m steps) is relative to the kept window, and the selection can change its probability; the bound `δ + P(V_m) + m / A` holds as stated.

**Remark 4 (decrease side).** The guard looks only at the increase side. Any other rule decided at `T_k`, for example "discard if the decrease side is off", is covered by the same Theorem 1.

**Remark 5 (missing returns, degenerate windows).** Both are handled inside a window as in `monitor.py` (a window is extended until usable; the τ part may restart before any sequence has started, which spends no δ). Each window still has exactly one sequence per side, started at a stopping time.

**Remark 6 (a fixed calendar day; not specific to the guard).** Corollary 1 (and part 3 of Theorem 2) bounds a given period of a given segment. For a fixed period `(b, b']` of the stream clock (for example a calendar day), with calibration at least m steps so that at most one segment monitors in it, the same argument gives

`P(an alarm in (b, b']) ≤ δ E[#{segments whose calibration starts before b'}] + (m + κ) / A` [PROVED].

*Proof.* Let J be the segment that monitors in the period. On `G_J`, at the first monitored step of the period its statistic is at most κ (restart at b, or a fresh start), and Ville's inequality from that stopping time gives `(m + κ) / A`. The event `G_Jᶜ` is contained in the union over segments j started before b' of `G_jᶜ`, and Theorem 1 at each start gives `δ` times the expected number of such segments. ∎

The first term grows with the number of earlier segments, but inside the null it grows slowly. Under the tolerance with h = ∞ in every segment, Theorem 2 part 2 gives `E[#{segments whose calibration starts before b'}] ≤ 1 + (δ + b' / A) / (1 - δ)` (a segment after the first starts after an alarm, and at most b' steps before b' are monitored), so for the d-th period of the stream clock `P(an alarm in it) ≤ δ (1 + (δ + d m / A) / (1 - δ)) + (m + κ) / A` [PROVED]; for P1, 5.05% on day 1, 6.02% on day 25 and 9.05% on day 100 [NUMERICAL]. A null cannot end good segments quickly, since each period of a good segment alarms with probability at most `(m + κ) / A`. Whether an adaptive null attains more than `δ + (m + κ) / A` on some day is open; we have not constructed one. So "δ + m / A for every day" is a statement about the periods of each segment, not about calendar days; the long-run statements for calendar time are Corollary 6.6 and Corollary 2.

**Corollary 2 (the share of alarmed periods; not specific to the guard) [PROVED].** Let `𝒫_M` be the set of periods (of all segments) that contain at least one of the first M monitored steps, and `N_M` the number of alarms among the first M monitored steps. Suppose the tolerance holds with h = ∞ in every segment, relative to its kept window, and the restart is at every period boundary with carry at most κ. Then

`E[N_M] ≤ (δ + ((m + κ) / A) E|𝒫_M|) / (1 - δ)`,

so the long-run share of alarmed periods, as a ratio of expectations `E[N_M] / E|𝒫_M|` with `E|𝒫_M| → ∞`, is at most `(m + κ) / (A (1 - δ))`: 4.04% for P1 (m = 1440, κ = 0, A = 36,000, δ = 0.01). With K = 1 this is the unguarded P1 of E22 and E23.

*Proof.* 1. Each alarm ends its segment, so each period holds at most one alarm and `N_M` is the number of periods in `𝒫_M` with an alarm among the first M monitored steps. 2. For period p of segment j, starting at `b_p` (the boundary, or `T_j` for the first period), the event `{p ∈ 𝒫_M}` is in `F_{b_p}` (the number of monitored steps before `b_p` is known at `b_p`), and so is `G_j`. Corollary 1 is stated given `F_{b_p}` (`P(G_j, alarm in p | F_{b_p}) ≤ 1{G_j} (m + κ) / A`; with the guard, through Theorem 2), so `P(G_j, p ∈ 𝒫_M, alarm in p | F_{b_p}) ≤ 1{G_j, p ∈ 𝒫_M} (m + κ) / A`. 3. Summing over all (j, p) and taking expectations bounds the alarms of good segments by `((m + κ) / A) E|𝒫_M|`. 4. Bad segments add at most one alarm each, and only segments that start before M monitored steps have passed (`C_j ∈ F_{S_j}`) can add one: by Theorem 1 at each start, at most `δ E[#{j : C_j}] ≤ δ (1 + E[N_M])`, as in Theorem 3 part 3. Then `E[N_M] ≤ ((m + κ) / A) E|𝒫_M| + δ (1 + E[N_M])`, and `E[N_M] ≤ M < ∞`. ∎

The per-period bound `δ + (m + κ) / A` of Corollary 1 (5% for P1) is for a given period of a given segment. The share of monitored periods with an alarm pools periods that exist only because their segment has not alarmed yet, so it is not that probability; Corollary 2 is the bound that applies to it, and it is smaller, because δ is charged once per segment, not once per period. With the rank fallback the same counting holds under the hypotheses of Theorem 3 part 3, with `m / A` on the rank branch's periods (its statistics restart at 0, and `{p ∈ 𝒫_M}` is in `H_{b_p}`).

## 4. Alternatives

### 4.1 A time-out

**Rule.** A segment that has monitored D full periods (D m monitored steps) without an alarm ends, and a new segment calibrates (each segment with budget δ).

**Proposition 4 [PROVED].** (a) Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 1 hold for each segment as stated (a time-out only shortens a segment). (b) If the tolerance holds with h = ∞ relative to every segment's window, `E[N_M] ≤ (δ (1 + ⌊M / (D m)⌋) + M / A) / (1 - δ)`. Per monitored period, the long-run bound becomes `(δ / D + m / A) / (1 - δ)` as M grows: the time-out costs δ / D alarms per period.

*Proof.* (a) The time-out is a stopping time, and Ville's inequality for the first m steps or for a period does not care whether the segment would have continued. (b) In the proof of Corollary 6.6 the bad-segment term is `δ E[#{k : C_k}]` with `C_k` = "segment k starts before M monitored steps have passed". A segment starts after an alarm or after a time-out, and a time-out among the first M monitored steps needs D m monitored steps of its own, so `#{k : C_k} ≤ 1 + N_M + ⌊M / (D m)⌋`. In the good-segment supermartingale W, a time-out drops the current statistic without banking it, which only lowers W. So `E[N_M] ≤ δ (1 + E[N_M] + ⌊M / (D m)⌋) + M / A`. ∎

With δ = 0.01 and D = 7 the extra term is 0.0014 per day against `m / A = 0.04`. The costs are elsewhere: a blind segment still lasts up to D periods, and each time-out spends one calibration window (for P1, one day in D + 1 if no alarm comes). Applied only to segments that are near-blind at T (a decision in `F_T`), the time-out is a delayed retry without spending, with the accounting of Remark 2.

### 4.2 A fallback to the awake rank monitor

**Rule.** If all K windows of a segment are near-blind, monitor the rest of the segment with E14's awake rank monitor (awake-B: smoothed conformal p-values of `|r_t|` among the segment's earlier returns, likelihood-ratio betting functions in rank space, payoffs divided by 1 + b, `docs/theory_running_tau.md` 4.1, 4.2 and Remark B.3), started with an empty reference set at `T_K`, with the same A and, with P1, restarted at the same period boundaries. After its alarm, the next segment calibrates again with window 1.

**Theorem 3 (the mixed guarantee) [PROVED].** Let `F = {all K windows are near-blind} ∈ F_{T_K}`. Suppose that on `Fᶜ` the tolerance holds relative to the kept window (horizon h), and that on F, conditionally on `F_{T_K}`, the returns observed after `T_K` are exchangeable (and the tie-breaking draws used after `T_K` are i.i.d. uniform, independent of `F_{T_K}` and of the returns; section 1 adds each draw to the filtration only at its time of use). Then:

1. `P(an alarm within the first m monitored steps of the segment) ≤ P(Fᶜ ∩ Gᶜ) + m / A ≤ Σ_k δ_k + m / A ≤ δ + m / A` (h = m).
2. With period restarts (carry at most κ): for every period `(b, b']` of the segment with `b' - b ≤ m`, `P(an alarm in it) ≤ δ + (m + κ) / A` (h up to b'); on F the bound `m / A` holds given `F_{T_K}` and the p-values before b (the rank statistics restart at 0 whatever the carry).
3. If the hypotheses hold in every segment (h = ∞; in a fallback segment, the returns `r_{T_K + 1}, r_{T_K + 2}, ...` exchangeable given `F_{T_K}`, or at least the first M of them), `E[N_M] ≤ (δ + M / A) / (1 - δ)`.

*Proof.* 1. Split on `Fᶜ ∩ Gᶜ`, `Fᶜ ∩ G` and F. The first has probability at most `Σ_k δ_k` by Theorem 1 (on `Fᶜ` some window k ≤ K is kept). On `Fᶜ ∩ G`, Step 3 of Theorem 6.4 with `E_s = Fᶜ ∩ G ∩ {T = s}` gives `m P(Fᶜ ∩ G) / A`. On F: given `F_{T_K}`, Proposition B.1 of `docs/theory_running_tau.md` applied to the exchangeable returns after `T_K` makes the p-values i.i.d. uniform and independent of `F_{T_K}`, so in `H_t = F_{T_K} ∨ σ(p-values up to t)` each payoff has conditional mean `1 / (1 + b) ≤ 1` and the two-sided Shiryaev-Roberts average N satisfies `E[N_t | H_{t-1}] ≤ N_{t-1} + 1`; Ville's inequality on `[T_K, T_K + m]` given `F_{T_K}` gives `m P(F) / A`. The three terms add to at most `Σ_k δ_k + m / A`. 2. The same split with the period's supermartingale (Corollary 1 on `Fᶜ`; on F a restart at b sets N to 0 and the argument runs in H given `H_b`). 3. Corollary 6.6's single supermartingale W mixes the filtrations F and H, so we use a per-segment argument instead. For segment j let `R_j` be the number of monitored steps still to come when it starts monitoring before the M-th one (known at its start of monitoring), and `ℓ_j ≤ R_j` its monitored steps among the first M; `Σ_j ℓ_j ≤ M`. On its good branch (`Fᶜ_j ∩ G_j`, or `F_j`), `1_E (N_t - (t - T_j))` is a supermartingale (in F, or in H given `F_{T_j}`; restarts only lower N), and optional stopping at `min(alarm, T_j + R_j)` gives `A P(E and segment j's alarm is among the first M monitored steps) ≤ E[1_E ℓ_j]`. On its bad branch, `P(Fᶜ_j ∩ Gᶜ_j | F_{S_j}) ≤ δ` by Theorem 1, and only segments that start before M monitored steps have passed (`C_j`, known at `S_j`) can contribute. So `E[N_M] ≤ δ E[#{j : C_j}] + M / A ≤ δ (1 + E[N_M]) + M / A`. ∎

The two hypotheses are about different things: a tolerance on conditional truncated variances for the cs branch, exchangeability of the returns after the fallback time for the rank branch. Neither needs the returns of the blind windows to be exchangeable, since the rank monitor's reference set starts empty at the fallback.

### 4.3 A grid of truncation levels (not implemented)

`docs/math.md` 6.4, fix 2: fix levels `τ_1 < ... < τ_J` when the CS part starts (for example multiples of the τ-part scale), run J sequences at level δ / J each, and choose τ at the end of calibration by any rule, for example the level with the largest headroom `τ_j² / m↑_j`. All J bounds hold together with probability at least 1 - δ, so the choice is free and no calibration time is lost. Proved there; the cost is `log(J / δ)` in place of `log(1 / δ)` in every segment (J = 5, δ = 0.01: 6.2 against 4.6), and it cannot help when the CS part is more volatile than the largest level allows.

## 5. What is proved, checked and assumed

* **Proved here:** Lemma 1, Propositions 1 and 2, Lemma 2, Theorem 1, Proposition 3, Theorem 2, Proposition 4, Theorem 3, the upper bounds of Remark 6, Corollary 2.
* **Open:** whether an adaptive null attains more than `δ + (m + κ) / A` on a fixed calendar day (Remark 6).
* **Corrected after the round 10 review:** Proposition 3 (a) (the law is inside the tolerance relative to the kept window only), the reading of Proposition 3 (the naive guard with three windows stays below δ on this law; it fails with probability 1 only without a cap), the selection-free null (H*) and the example of Remark 3, the sketch of Remark 6 (replaced by an explicit bound), "2A - 1" and the carry in Lemma 1 and Proposition 2, the filtration of the tie-breaking draws, and the carry in Theorem 3 part 2.
* **Checked numerically** (`tests/test_guard.py`, E24): the blind condition on constructed data; the retry and spending bookkeeping; identity with the unguarded monitor when K = 1, and when no window is blind with `δ_1` equal to the unguarded δ; Monte Carlo null checks of the first-m and per-period bounds on i.i.d. Gaussian and Student t3 data with a step in volatility; Proposition 3's coverage inflation (`results/e24_selection.csv`); false alarms, blind shares, detection and calibration cost in E24's synthetic designs.
* **Assumed, not proved:** the tolerance (or, for the rank fallback, exchangeability after the fallback) on real data. As in E22 and E23, it fails on most real days, so real-data alarm rates are not false alarm rates.

## 6. Recommended guard

The recommendation was fixed after E24's synthetic experiments and before any real-data rerun (iteration 2 of `docs/iterations/E24.md`); the evidence is in `docs/report_parts/E24.md` and `results/e24_synthetic.csv` [SYNTHETIC, 800 paths per design]. The synthetic experiments chose among the monitors G, GR and GE; η = 0.25 itself was set before the synthetic run, after iteration 1's scratch look at real BTC windows with ρ ≤ 1.2.

### 6.1 Recommendation

**The retry guard with K = 3 windows, halving budgets (δ/2, δ/4, δ/4), near-blind margin η = 0.25, and the fallback to E14's awake rank monitor** (`GuardedPeriodicVolatilityMonitor(..., guard_tries=3, guard_eta=0.25, guard_fallback="rank")`, arm GE in E24, G1 in the real reruns and the forward pre-registration). Reasons:

1. **It keeps every proved bound** with the same total δ and the same A (Theorems 2 and 3), so P1's bound δ + m/A = 5% for each period of a segment is unchanged. Under every null design with a constant law after the first τ part (steps in volatility of 2 to 8, Gaussian and t3, the tick design, the permuted calm BTC returns) no confidence-sequence monitor, guarded or not, raised an alarm in the first day or in any of the first four days of its first null segment: 0 of 800 paths for each, one-sided 95% upper limit 0.0037 against the bound 0.05 (the rank monitor: 0 alarms as well, on the 710 to 800 paths where its first segment starts after the step, and 4 in the tick design).
2. **η = 0 is not enough.** A near-blind window keeps the increase side formally on but nearly powerless (Proposition 2). With a step of 4 the windows have ρ ≈ 1.2 and the guard with η = 0 detects a later doubling of the standard deviation within a day in 0.1% of paths (the unguarded monitor 0.25%); with a step of 6 (ρ ≈ 1.03) in 16% (unguarded 0%). With η = 0.25: 86.5% and 100%. In the blind designs (step 8, Gaussian and t3; the tick design) η = 0 and η = 0.25 both detect in 98% to 100% of paths, against 0% for the unguarded monitor.
3. **The cost is calibration time, and only where the first window is near-blind:** one extra window (1440 minutes) in about 86% of paths with a step of 4 and in every path with a step of 6 or 8 or the tick design; none with a step of 2 or in the permuted calm returns (every first window kept). The spending widens the first window's bounds by a factor of about 1.06 (Remark 1).
4. **The rank fallback costs nothing when it is not used** (it was never used in the synthetic designs, because a second window after a step is calibrated under the new law) and is the only option that keeps the increase side alive when the near-blindness persists, as with tick-level price discreteness on quiet weekends (E24's real-data finding). The time-out (Proposition 4) was not chosen: it keeps a blind segment for up to D days, which is the failure E23 met.
5. **What it does not fix.** When the volatility drifts during the first window (design `drift8_gauss`), the first window is not near-blind (ρ ≈ 1.6) and is kept; every cs monitor, guarded or not, then alarms on the first monitored day, correctly relative to the theorem, whose tolerance fails relative to that window. And on whole calm BTC days in random order (design `calm_btc_days`), where the level changes at day boundaries, all monitors alarm early (first-day alarm share 0.76 to 0.78 for the cs monitors, 0.62 for the rank monitor); these are alarm rates, not false alarm rates. The rank monitor alone detects faster in eight of the nine designs (median 25 to 30 minutes after a doubling against 48 to 64 for GE in step6, step8, frozen and drift8; 25 against 144 in step2), and slower under Student t3 returns (127 against 115); it is never blind, but its bound needs exchangeability rather than the tolerance.
