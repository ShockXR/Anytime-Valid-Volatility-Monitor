## Verdict

**The mathematics is right, the code does what the pre-registration and Amendment 1 say, and every number I recomputed matches.** Lemma 1, Theorem 1 and the application of Ville's inequality to the mixture are correct; the filtration is well defined and no information from later strata enters the conditioning. An independent implementation of section 4 with Amendment 1 (written from the text, importing nothing from `experiments/`) reproduces every stratum of the planned calendar, of F24a's planning calendar with the 2028 placeholders and of both post hoc periods, under both rule sets, and every post hoc e-process (60 comparisons, 0 mismatches). The power numbers reproduce exactly with the stated seed, and an independent simulation agrees within Monte Carlo error. The status part, run end to end on known data at two looks, writes only complete strata and matches my independent computation.

**No critical finding.** One major finding: the status part checks only that nearby event rows are not `scheduled`, not that they exist, so a missing or late-added row changes a stratum that has already been reported (demonstrated on a synthetic calendar), and nothing freezes reported strata. Fourteen minor findings: three qualifications of the null's description (the drift direction is systematic, consecutive-day releases, holiday controls), two small overclaims, three wrong or loose numbers or times, one incomplete figure label, and five gaps in gates, traceability and provenance.

## Findings

### Critical

None.

### Major

**M1. The calendar can change under a stratum that has already been reported; rule 4 checks statuses, not completeness, and reported strata are not frozen.**
*Where.* `experiments/e25_sequential_events.py` lines 289 to 312 (`completion`), 968 to 1000 (`fresh_plan`, `status_refusals`), 1063 to 1123 (`part_status`); pre-registration line 21 (events "updated only under F24a's rule") and line 31 (rule 4).
*What.* Rule 4 makes a stratum complete when no event row within 30 days of its release says `scheduled`. A row that is missing (never added, or added late) passes this check, and its release day then neither excludes nearby candidates (rule 2) nor blocks completion. When the row is added, `assign_strata` reassigns the controls of strata that were already reported, so a later look reports a different M_j for the same j. F24a's rule, which the pre-registration adopts for this file, comes with refusals that E25's status part does not have (`E24.future_refusals`: a month without a CPI row, a missing scheduled FOMC row, a `released` row with an access date before the release). The pre-registration also says rows after 2027 are added by hand "before their month begins"; nothing enforces it. Two different paths for the same strata would let a reader choose between them, which Theorem 1 does not cover.
*Evidence* (`r11_calendar_integrity.py`, E25's own functions, synthetic calendar and alarms): with the CPI row of 10 November 2026 missing, the FOMC stratum of 28 October 2026 is complete at a look with data to 1 December 2026, with no refusal, and is reported with n = 4 (11 November kept, one day from the missing release), K = 1, M_1 = 1.9094; with the row present the same stratum has n = 3, K = 0, M_1 = 2.5408.
*Practical risk.* Low on the current calendar (every month to December 2027 has its CPI row; F24a's refusals will check the file for its own period, but only when F24a runs on 1 June 2027, after E25 may already have reported), and the test is meant to run past that date.
*Fix.* (i) In `status_refusals`, refuse a stratum (and so every later one) unless every calendar month that meets [d − 30, d + 30] has a CPI row and every date in `E24.FUTURE_FOMC_SCHEDULED` (extended when 2028 schedules are added) is present as `date` or `original_date`, and no `released` row has an access date before its date. (ii) Freeze reported strata: write each complete stratum's controls, n_i, Z_i, K_i and the data files' SHA-256 to a ledger at first report, and refuse a later look whose recomputation differs (a data revision by the mirror, or a reschedule into the 30-day zone after completion, would otherwise also change a reported stratum). Both are additional refusals and change nothing in the test; disclose them as a post hoc amendment made before the first stratum completes.

### Minor

**m1. "No shared minutes between strata" is not true in general.** `docs/report_parts/E25.md` line 118 ("Amendment 1 strengthens the case for H0 (no shared minutes between strata)") and `docs/iterations/E25.md` line 134 ("No two windows of different strata share a minute"). Amendment 1 keeps overlapping release windows: on 12 June 2024 the CPI and FOMC release windows share 30 minutes (`results/e25_posthoc_summary.csv`, `strata_with_overlapping_release_window` = 2; `r11_report_numbers.py` finds no such pair on the planned calendar or with the 2028 placeholders). It is harmless in practice, because the later FOMC stratum loses its Wednesday candidates to the CPI controls and enters with n = 0 (e = 1), as on 12 June 2024; by rules 2 and 3 this happens for every same-day pair away from the first 30 hours of the stream. *Fix:* "no control window shares a minute with a window of another stratum; two release windows can (12 June 2024), and the later stratum then has no controls".

**m2. The drift is not "in either direction" across strata: every one-sided stratum has its controls after the release.** Amendment 1 item 2 (pre-registration line 100), report line 56, and the integrated paper text (`paper/short_paper.md` line 1128). Because strata are filled in release order and earlier strata take their later candidates first, all 19 one-sided strata to January 2029 are after-only, and the planned strata keep 57 controls before releases against 115 after (31 against 64 to December 2027; post hoc 17 of 17 and 3 of 3 one-sided strata after-only) (`r11_report_numbers.py`). A drift common to all windows therefore moves the unbalanced strata in the same direction. The effect on the level is small for plausible drifts: with no release effect and every window's rate halving every 91 days, P(M ever ≥ 20 over the 41 planned strata) is 0.0258 against 0.0226 without drift (s.e. 0.0011; `r11_drift_sim.py`, [SYNTHETIC]). *Fix:* say that the imbalance is systematic (controls after the release), give the counts, and report as exploratory the control rate before against after the release and M on the balanced strata alone.

**m3. "No independence between strata" needs one qualification for releases on consecutive days.** Theory note line 13 and Remark 2 (line 48); pre-registration line 57. The statement is right given H0, but H0 conditions on the earlier strata, and the design keeps every control at least 2 days from every release while releases are not kept apart from each other: 4 pairs of releases at most 2 Eastern days apart to December 2027 (9 and 10 December 2026, 9 and 10 June 2027, 14 and 15 September 2027, 8 and 10 December 2027), 7 to January 2029 (`r11_report_numbers.py`). Any short-range dependence of the outcome process then distinguishes the later release window from its controls given the earlier stratum. On seen data S2's one-day dependence is weak and, where detectable, negative, which is the conservative direction (odds ratio of an up alarm in [08:30, 14:30) ET given one in [14:00, 20:00) ET the day before: BTC 0.75, ETH 0.62, SOL 0.63; [08:30, 14:30) then [14:00, 20:00) next day: BTC 0.16 (log OR −1.84 ± 1.65), ETH 1.63, SOL 0.82; 383 release-free day pairs per coin, `r11_lag_dependence.py`, [REAL, seen data, descriptive]). *Fix:* add one sentence to Remark 2 and the report: H0 needs no independence, but it does need that the earlier strata carry no information that separates the release window from its controls, which consecutive-day releases test; give the counts and the measured dependence.

**m4. Gate 5's range is over seven laws, not eight.** Report lines 10 and 118 ("2.5% to 4.4% over eight laws"; "in the simulated laws it is 2.5% to 4.4%"), also `ITERATION_LOG.md` line 633. The all-or-nothing law gives 0 (`results/e25_validity.csv`, row 4). The table (lines 36 to 43) and the paper ("at most 4.4%") are right. *Fix:* "at most 4.4% in every law (2.5% to 4.4% in the seven laws in which a stratum can carry evidence; 0 in the all-or-nothing law)".

**m5. A rounding slip.** Report line 109: the best expected log e-value under the original rules is 0.3046 (`results/e25_power_growth_original_rules.csv`, ψ = 8), which is 0.30, not 0.31 (the iteration notes give 0.305). *Fix:* 0.30.

## Checks that passed

1. **Mathematics.** Lemma 1: under conditional exchangeability given G_{i-1}, the arrangements given S_i are uniform, so P(Z_i = 1 | G_{i-1}, S_i) = p_i and the conditional mean of ψ^Z/(1 − p + ψp) is 1; the boundary cases S = 0, S = n + 1 and n = 0 give e = 1. The e-value is the likelihood ratio of Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric law with one release slot against the uniform law (P(Z = 1 | S) = ψp/(1 − p + ψp) checked by hand). Theorem 1: e_i(ψ) is G_i-measurable (n_i is a function of the calendar only for complete strata, see 2), the tower property gives E[e_i | G_{i-1}] = 1, each product and their convex combination are nonnegative martingales with M_0 = 1, and Ville's inequality bounds the supremum; the statement for an arbitrary random index τ follows because {M_τ ≥ 1/α} lies in {sup M_j ≥ 1/α}. The filtration is σ(outcomes of strata 1 to j), indexed by release order, not calendar time; interleaving of windows in calendar time does not enter the argument, and no later stratum's outcome is in G_{i-1}. H0 is coherent with the amended strata: no control window overlaps any window of another stratum, and the only overlap left (release with release, same day) gives the later stratum n = 0. Remark 3 (optional continuation) and Remark 4 (the per-stratum maximum log(ψ(n + 1)/(n + ψ)) and the log 4 cost of the mixture) are correct. Remark 2's figure (5.8% for seven FOMC statements sharing controls) is in `docs/report_parts/E23.md` line 37.
