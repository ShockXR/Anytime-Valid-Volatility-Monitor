# Pre-registration E18: landmark market shocks as case studies

*Written and committed before any return in the event windows below was loaded, plotted or summarised for this study, and before `experiments/e18_landmark_events.py` existed. The commit time on GitHub is the record of when the design was fixed. Tracker B85.*

**Question.** When one of the large market shocks that everyone remembers happens, how quickly do the e-detector and its published competitors (E13) raise an "up" alarm (a detected rise in volatility), were they watching at that moment, and how many alarms did they raise in the quiet week before?

**What this is, and what it is not.** Four events and up to six assets per event are **case studies, not evidence**. There will be no pooled test and no p-value. The evidence about the methods is in E6, E7, E12 and E13. The point of fixing everything in advance is that the outcome of each case is reported as it comes, including misses and alarms that come before the reference time.

Crypto trades all the time; for the two Monday sell-offs that started in Asia the reference is the open of the Tokyo Stock Exchange, 09:00 JST = 00:00 UTC, the first large stock market to open on that Monday.

## 2. Streams (what a user would have run)

* **Crypto.** Binance spot one-minute bars from the mirror used in E6 (`avmonitor.data.load_binance`), returns divided by the predictable intraday profile (`intraday_scale`, 20-day lookback, past days only) computed on data from 1 January 2024. Three half-year streams, each started at its first minute and run without interruption: **H2 2024** (1 July to 31 December 2024, for C1), **H1 2025** (1 January to 30 June 2025, for C2) and **H2 2025** (1 July to 31 December 2025, for C3). H1 2025 and H2 2025 are exactly the two crypto samples of E13 (built by `prepare_h1_2025` and `e06b_five_coins_monitor.prepare`); H2 2024 is new and built the same way.
* **Nifty 50.** Exactly E13's stream: `load_clean("2023-10-01", "2026-01-01")` and E7's session profile (`deseasonalise`), monitoring from 1 January 2024 to 31 December 2025 (N1, N2 and N3 are all in it).

## 3. Methods and settings (nothing tuned here)

* **e-detector**, frozen real-data settings of E6 and E7: `VolatilityMonitor` with A = 28,800 per 1440-minute day (crypto) or 7,500 per 375-bar session (Nifty), each side alarming at 2A, c = 3, calibration 1440 minutes (crypto) or 750 bars (Nifty, E7's pre-registered primary), null band 0.25, plug-in baseline, recalibration after each alarm. It runs once over each stream from its start, as a user would have run it.
* **Competitors**, exactly E13's real-data code (`experiments/e13_benchmarks.py`: `real_bocpd`, `trailing_robust_var`, `C.bjv_online_statistic`, `C.scan_alarms`), each with the E13 first-run window and the longer windows of review round 3:
  * **BOCPD** (Adams and MacKay 2007): hazard 1 / A, α₀ = 2, predictable prior variance from the robust variance of the previous day (1440 minutes or 375 bars). Short-run windows w = 250 (run-length cap 512, first run); crypto w = 720 and 1440, Nifty w = 375 (cap 2w, round 3).
  * **BOCPD on truncated returns** (our modification): the same on returns clipped at 3 times the square root of that prior variance; same windows.
  * **BJV-type rolling test** (our reconstruction of the idea of Bibinger, Jirak and Vetter 2017), truncated and Gaussian-normalised: k = 120 (first run, statistic from the monitored returns only, as in E13); crypto k = 480 and 1440, Nifty k = 375 (round 3, using returns before the monitored period so the statistic exists from the first possible alarm).
  * As in E13, every competitor can first alarm after the e-detector's first calibration window, and pauses for 1440 minutes (crypto) or 750 bars (Nifty) after each of its alarms.
  * Not included: the studentised BJV version, the Gaussian-matched and nominal thresholds, and the confidence-sequence e-detector (the request named the three competitor families above; fewer rows keep the tables readable).
* **Rate matching, computed without the event windows.** For each stream and each competitor configuration, the up and down thresholds are found with E13's bisection (four rounds of 50 steps per direction) so that the competitor raises as many up alarms and as many down alarms as the e-detector, **counting only alarms outside the exclusion windows** [T − 8 days, T + 2 days) of the events in that stream (one window per crypto stream, three in the Nifty stream). The alarm counts inside the windows, and so the outcomes below, never enter the thresholds. The statistics themselves are computed on the whole stream, so the run is the one a user would have seen; an alarm inside a window can still shift the pause pattern after it, and the thresholds use data from after the event (a user could not have set them this way in real time, which favours the competitors). Both points will be stated in the report.

## 4. Outcomes

For every event, asset and method, in the primary design (streaming, thresholds matched without the windows):

1. **State at T.** e-detector: monitoring, or calibrating (with the time of the alarm that started the calibration and the time monitoring resumed). Competitors: watching, or paused after their own alarm (same details).
2. **Delay to the first up alarm**: minutes from T to the first up alarm in [T, T + 24 h), measured as in E12 (the open time of the alarm's minute bar minus T, so an alarm on the first bar counts as 0). For Nifty, minutes from 09:15 IST (equal to bars, since the 24-hour window holds one session). "Missed" if there is none. For C3 also from 20:57 UTC.
3. **Alarms in the 7 days before T**, [T − 7 days, T): numbers of up and down alarms, the time of the last up alarm before T, and, for context, the number expected at the method's average rate outside the exclusion windows (7 times its alarms per day for crypto; its alarms per session times the sessions in the window for Nifty). Under the rate matching this expected number is about the same for every method. Alarms here are the false alarm check: they are not known to be false, they are alarms with no landmark event.
4. **Alarms in the 24 hours after T**, [T, T + 24 h): numbers of up and down alarms, and the direction of the first one.

Every alarm in [T − 7 days, T + 24 h) is listed with its time and direction in `results/e18_alarms.csv`.

**Secondary, labelled as such in the report:**

* **S1, fresh start.** An e-detector calibrated on the 1440 minutes (750 bars) just before T and monitoring from T (E12's fresh-calibration design); for the competitors, the first up crossing of their matched threshold at or after T, ignoring any pause in force at T. Delay within 24 hours.
* **S2, another start date.** Outcomes 1 to 4 for the e-detector streamed from 21 January 2024 to 31 December 2025 (crypto, E12's stream design) and over all Nifty sessions from April 2017 (E7's stream, `load_clean()` default range). This shows how much the state at T depends on when the user started.
* **S3, matching on the whole sample.** Outcomes 1 to 4 for the competitors with thresholds matched on all alarms of the stream, windows included (E13's own rule).

## 5. Reporting

* `experiments/e18_landmark_events.py` writes `results/e18_events.csv` (the table of section 1), `results/e18_outcomes.csv` (one row per event, asset, method and design), `results/e18_alarms.csv`, `results/e18_thresholds.csv` (both matchings, with achieved counts) and `results/figures/e18_events.png`: one panel per event and asset (18 panels), raw one-minute log returns from T − 7 days to T + 24 hours (Nifty: the sessions in that span on a trading-minute axis), a vertical line at T, and one row of marks per method (up and down alarms marked differently).
* `docs/report_parts/E18.md` gives one table per event (rows: asset and method; columns: outcomes 1 to 4), then the secondary designs, labelled **[REAL]** and "case study". Every pre-registered outcome is reported, including events a method misses and alarms before T. No pooled test, no ranking of methods by these cases.
* Nothing in sections 1 to 4 will be changed after the run. If a stream cannot be built or a run fails, that is reported instead of a replacement.

## 6. What was already known before this document

Stated so that the reader can judge how blind the design is. The data are not new: all of it was used in earlier experiments.

* E6 reports that all ten one-minute extremes of the five Binance coins in July to December 2025 fall between 21:15 and 21:45 UTC on 10 October 2025.
* ALT1 (a different, quarticity-based monitor) reports that its monitors on two coins alarmed at 15:28 to 15:31 UTC on 10 October 2025, followed by decrease alarms on 11 October.
* E7 (and the MAIN iteration notes) report that the e-detector, streamed over Nifty from 2017, raised an up alarm at 09:26 IST on 4 June 2024 (09:24 in an earlier version) and was then recalibrating for two sessions; that session's robust volatility was 6.3 times the median of the previous 20 sessions.
* ALT4 (correlation monitor) lists an up alarm on 5 August 2024 for the BTC and SOL pair and a BTC fall of 7.4% that day.
* The news sources above give the daily index moves (for example Nifty about 5.9% lower on 4 June 2024, Nikkei 12.4% lower on 5 August 2024, bitcoin about 10% lower on 10 October 2025).
* Alarm lists for the e-detector and E13's competitors on the H1 2025 and H2 2025 crypto streams and on the Nifty stream, and E12's crypto stream, already exist in `results/` (E6, E12, E13). They were not opened around these dates for this study. Because the primary design reruns E13's streams, some primary outcomes for C2, C3, N1, N2 and N3 are fixed by computations made before this document; only the matching rule and the H2 2024 stream are new.
