# Experiment report

Every experiment answers one research question. Scripts are in `experiments/` (and in `alternatives/<name>/` for the alternative prototypes), raw outputs in `results/`, figures in `results/figures/`. Seeds are fixed in the scripts. Each result is labelled:

* **[SYNTHETIC]** for our simulations.
* **[REAL]** for market data: Bitstamp BTC/USD one-minute bars (E5), Binance spot one-minute bars for BTC, ETH, XRP, SOL and DOGE (E6, E10, E12 and later), Nifty 50 one-minute bars and India VIX daily closes (E7). Sources and SHA-256 hashes are in `data/MANIFEST.csv`; raw files are not committed (`data/README.md`).
* **[PUBLISHED]** for numbers copied from Bapat (2026).

To rerun: `pip install -r requirements.txt && pip install -e .`, then `python scripts/download_data.py`, then `python experiments/<script>.py`. This report is assembled from `docs/report_parts/` by `python scripts/build_report.py`. What the results do not establish is collected in `docs/limitations.md`.

## Index

| ID | Question | Main finding |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Do the printed formulas reproduce the published simulation tables? | Yes for the plain kernel with one fitted volatility; the large-jump robust rows do not reproduce. After normalisation the robust kernel wins in most cells. |
| E2 | How large is the truncation bias? | 3 raw MADs keep 70.5% of the fourth moment, 3 scaled MADs 96.4%, at every n. |
| E3 | Can the truncation run online? | Yes: rolling or exponentially weighted MADs keep the jump protection. |
| E4 | False alarms and delay against parametric and classical detectors | The robust e-detector is the only valid one under jumps and fat tails, 10% to 70% slower on clean Gaussian data. The plug-in baseline roughly triples the false alarm rate with short calibration (E4e). |
| E5 | Real data, Bitstamp BTC | 81% and 90% of up and down alarms are followed by volatility in the flagged direction, but clustering explains much of the up figure. |
| E6 | The paper's five coins on Binance | Published tables imply unnormalised weights, the raw MAD and about 2,000 missing minutes; an ETH sign slip and a DOGE contradiction. Alarms beat EWMA and HAR forecasts, but a hindsight-tuned ratio rule is as precise. |
| E7 | Nifty 50 and India VIX | Out of sample, 78% of up alarms are followed by higher volatility (47% at random); every budget day watched from the open raised an alarm; the monitor answers "has volatility changed?", not "is it high?". India VIX moves with the alarms on the same day but the alarms do not lead it. |
| E8 | A guarantee with an estimated baseline | Proved: P(false alarm within m steps) ≤ δ + m / A with a confidence-sequence baseline; observed at most 0.27% against 5%. |
| E9 | How tight is the guarantee, and when does it fail? | About 4 times conservative and distribution free; persistent GARCH clustering breaks it. |
| E10 | Monitoring relative to a volatility forecast | Restores the guarantee under clustering; a frozen persistent forecast lets through at most (1 - β)/α of any break, and an information bound puts the delay near two days. |
| E11 | Detection delay and bet design (theory) | Proved delay bound; a refined grid is first-order asymptotically optimal (its delay divided by log A / KL_inf tends to 1; first-order optimality for bounded means is known from Ram and Ramdas 2026); the default grid's limit is 1.00 to 1.08 times that; the price of robustness is a ratio of information numbers that predicts E4's 14% to 73% penalties, mostly the cost of making no distributional assumption; truncation caps decrease detection at log(B / (B - 1)) per step (at least 73 steps at c = 3 for the default bets, and at least 58 for any valid bets); a separate decrease clip c_down = 2 cut simulated decrease delays by 13% to 51% for continuous laws but was 12% slower for a 20% fall at a tick of 0.5 sd; the false alarm bound is nearly attained by an adversarial null for a single bet. |
| E12 | Crypto alarms around FOMC and CPI releases | Streaming monitor (fixed in advance): up alarms within 6 hours of 8 to 9 of 16 FOMC statements (all at which it was monitoring) against 11% to 17% of weekday-matched controls; primary test passes for FOMC, not for CPI on BTC. Fresh calibration: 14 to 15 of 16 against 29% to 32%. The streaming monitor is blind about 70% of the time while recalibrating. |
| E13 | Benchmark against Bayesian online change points and a two-window variance-ratio scan inspired by Bibinger, Jirak and Vetter (2017) | Under jumps and fat tails BOCPD raises false alarms on 53% to 73% of days at a threshold giving 1% on Gaussian data; the e-detector stays at 1.2% to 1.3% (plug-in baseline, measured; 0.0% to 0.1% with the confidence-sequence baseline, whose 5% bound is proved). On clean Gaussian data BOCPD detects large decreases two to three times faster (1.0 to 1.9 times for sd x 0.8 or with ticks or price noise), and on clipped returns keeps that speed under jumps. The variance-ratio scan with windows of 50 and 125 returns (our construction, not the paper's procedure) finds a 25% change in 6% to 30% of offline windows against 100%; with windows of 500 returns it finds 99%, so the gap is window length, and online the long-window test needs 1.9 to 3.5 times the delay. On crypto, at equal alarm counts, the e-detector's alarms are followed by a day beyond the forecast more often than those of competitors with 2 to 4 hour windows (78.5% and 80.5% against 58.7% to 71.7%; held out 82.0% and 69.7% against 56.7% to 71.5%, where truncated BOCPD is not significantly different on down alarms, p = 0.47); with day-long windows truncated BOCPD and the variance-ratio scans match its down alarms (plain BOCPD stays lower) and the best competitor, picked after seeing the results, trails its up alarms by about 6 points (borderline), and on a four-hour outcome the short-window competitors do as well or better. On Nifty no first-run difference is significant. |
| E14 | A guarantee with a running truncation level, and no blind period | The plug-in has no bound of the form C m / A at a fixed calibration length (Proposition A.6 and Lemma A.7, proved for the four simulated laws; 41% to 43% false alarms over five days, where a known baseline would guarantee at most 25%). A band over all truncation levels allows any running τ with a proof; an exact rank detector needs no baseline (1.7% to 3.2% per day) and, restarted at once, catches 98% to 99.6% of changes against 56% to 66% for monitors that recalibrate for a day after each alarm (65% to 66% for the default plug-in). On E12's releases it watches 114 of 114 instead of 55. |
| E15 | Panel monitoring with false discovery control | Proved FDR control at every stopping time with e-BH on per-asset e-processes (a Shiryaev-Roberts statistic divided by t is not an e-value); FDR at most 0.96% in 48 designs; on five coins plus Nifty, discoveries are confirmed 78% to 97% of the time against about 50% at random, and e-BH's gain over Bonferroni is mostly earlier flags. |
| E16 | Adversarial search for null streams that push the false alarm rate up | Inside the class of Theorem B the bound always held: the worst adversary found (bold play, which watches the detector) reaches 2.62% per day and 18.1% over five days against 5% and 25% (Gaussian 0.97% and 12.4%); under that adversary the rest of the slack is wealth stranded in the decrease and small increase bets. A search gives lower bounds on the worst case only; the only proved limit is 0.98 of the bound at one day. Outside the class, each failure matches a broken condition and disappears when it is restored (for ticks, the same values drawn independently): GARCH from a mean conditional-mean drift of 3.9% at one day (1.6% over five days), a plug-in baseline (42% over five days; 32.5% in one day under a shape change), price ticks from 4 sd (five days; 6 sd for one day). |
| E17 | Can e-detectors at 1, 5 and 15 minutes be combined with one proved bound, and does that beat each frequency? | The usual proof does not cover the plain every-minute average (a coarse detector is not a fair bet between its block ends; a union bound proves three times the budget for balanced weights); a certified average has P(false alarm within m) ≤ Σ w_k ⌈m/k⌉ / A when each frequency's null holds (for example independent returns with any tails and the true baselines; no proof with the plug-in baselines used on real data). It never beats the best single frequency in independent-return designs but stays within 0% to 5% (equal weights) or 11% to 18% (balanced) of it on persistent changes, and catches 95% to 97% of paths (84% to 89% for Student t3) after a jump in the lag-one autocorrelation of returns from 0 to 0.3, which the one-minute detector almost never sees (3.8%). Under price noise only the 5 and 15 minute gap average is valid, and it helps only for persistent increases at the strongest noise level, which was added after seeing the first results. On five coins it behaves like the one-minute monitor, and at matched alarm counts (a check added after review) the 5 and 15 minute detectors alone are no more precise than it. |
| TH4 | How much can a small drift of the conditional mean inflate the false alarm probability? | Proved: P(false alarm within m) ≤ (m / A) I_m(δ) with a closed-form inflation factor, attained by a deterministic stream where it reaches 1 (below that the strongest stream found reaches 0.33 to 0.63 of it at one day); the one-day bound reaches 10% at a drift of 1.63% and 25% at 2.66% (downward drift only: 6.36% and 10.51%). Pathwise bounds cover a drift that varies (Theorem 2 for any predictable drift, Theorem 3 for an exogenous one); the mean drift alone cannot give a C m / A bound, and Theorem 3's formula is not a bound when the drift responds to the data. The plug-in baseline is bounded by averaging over calibration windows (41.8% at one day with 390 steps, within 1.3 of E16's worst stream). Against E16 no proved bound is below a measured rate; for GARCH the proved bound is below 1 in 22 of 64 cells at one day, all with a small drift. |
| E18 | How fast do the monitors flag landmark shocks (Indian election results 4 Jun 2024, sell-offs of 5 Aug 2024 and 7 Apr 2025, crypto cascade 10 Oct 2025)? Pre-registered case studies | Case studies, not evidence. On Nifty the streaming e-detector flagged all three shocks (11, 104 and 172 minutes after the open). On crypto it flagged none of the fifteen coin and event pairs within 24 hours of the reference time: it had raised up alarms 4.5 to 6.9 hours earlier, as each episode began, and was recalibrating. At matched alarm rates most competitors were paused for the same reason and most of their catches came the minute a pause ended, except the day-long two-window variance-ratio scan (inspired by Bibinger, Jirak and Vetter 2017), which flagged 10 Oct 2025 on all five coins within 17 to 73 minutes. With a fresh calibration just before the reference time the e-detector flagged all 18 cases in 15 to 207 minutes. Exploratory and post hoc: E14's monitors with no blind period, at the same null band and a similar alarm rate, flagged 15 and 17 of the 18 cases (fast on 5 Aug 2024 and 10 Oct 2025, slow on 7 Apr 2025); without the band the rank monitor is faster but alarms five to seven times as often. |
| E19 | Does the guarantee hold on real data (referee M1, m32)? | Pre-registered. On streams that permute real one-minute returns of calm days (real marginal law, exchangeable), the known-baseline, confidence-sequence and rank monitors stay below every bound in 252 cells (one day: 2.26%, 0.01% and 3.38% against 5%); the plug-in exceeds its nominal level over five days (41% against 25%). Calm days resampled in blocks break every null, at about the real series' alarm rate. With the confidence-sequence baseline as the primary configuration on five coins, about a quarter fewer alarms, up-alarm validation unchanged (78% against 80%), down-alarm validation higher (96% against 88%); in 5 of 7031 segments (all BTC) its increase side could not alarm at all (post hoc). GARCH-relative monitoring alarms near the nominal rate, every alarm validated. |
| E20 | Which real-data claims survive multiplicity, dependence between days and a strong forecast? | Re-analysis with methods fixed before its results. Only E12's FOMC and CPI family was a pre-specified primary test (FOMC on BTC holds, CPI does not). Every alarm-against-control comparison holds (Holm p ≤ 0.0016), but only 1 of 48 e-detector-against-ratio-rule comparisons does, and the 6-point margin over the best day-long competitor does not. Against HAR with the last hour's realised variance only the decrease alarms add information (Holm 0.046), no more than a 240-minute ratio rule, and there is no out-of-sample QLIKE gain. |
| E21 | Do the benchmarks favour the e-detector (referee M5)? Classical CUSUM and Shiryaev-Roberts rules on the same statistic with simulated or bootstrap thresholds, Student t BOCPD, CUSUM monitoring with a training sample | Partly. At equal Gaussian false alarm rate Ville's threshold costs little delay (same speed for increases; a calibrated CUSUM is 15% to 40% faster for decreases). Gaussian thresholds fail under fat tails (CUSUM 5.0% to 8.2% against 1.3%); a bootstrap repairs them. A Student t BOCPD fixes BOCPD's false alarms but finds 56% of 25% increases (e-detector 98%). On real data, at matched alarm counts, classical rules on the same statistic match the e-detector, while Student t BOCPD and the training-sample CUSUM rules do worse in most comparisons (unadjusted). |
| E22 | Does E12's pre-registered FOMC result hold with a monitor that carries a proved per-day false alarm bound (referee round 2, J1)? | Yes [REAL], pre-registered: the confidence-sequence baseline restarted every UTC day (5% per day of a segment under the ±25% tolerance) flags 8 of 16 BTC FOMC statements within 6 hours against 10.9% of matched controls, Holm p = 0.00033 (plug-in: 9 of 16, Holm 0.00025); it watches more releases than the plug-in (80 of 114 against 55). The tolerance fails on real days: the monitor alarms on about half of its monitored days, so E22 does not test the bound. |
| E23 | Does E22's FOMC and CPI contrast hold on fresh releases (January to October 2026), which no analysis had seen? | **No** [REAL], pre-registered: with the guaranteed monitor of E22 on BTC, 2 of 16 releases had an up alarm within 6 hours against 5.9% of matched controls, p = 0.25 (planned power 0.82). The monitor could not raise an increase alarm for 28% of its monitored time on BTC (one segment of 47 days). Secondary: the never-blind rank monitor flagged 4 of 6 FOMC statements on each coin (Holm 0.016 to 0.055); no CPI contrast under any monitor. |
| TH5 | How fast can any valid procedure detect a change, and when does the plug-in baseline keep its level? | Proved: a deterministic rule valid at levels n / A, even one that sees raw returns, can stop on a path only where a product of per-step likelihood bounds reaches A / n (a randomised rule only with probability at most n W_n / A). On a complete collapse no valid deterministic rule alarms before 45 steps (randomised: mean at least 43.4; Shiryaev-Roberts e-detectors 58, the default grid 73), and over 18 post-change laws the default grid's mean delay is at most 1.7 to 2.2 times that of the best valid deterministic rule. The plug-in baseline keeps m / A, up to a factor 1 + O(m / √n), when m is small compared with √n, and some stream with the right conditional mean breaks it by an unbounded factor when m is large compared with √n and log A; a margin z = Φ⁻¹(1 - δ / 2) restores m / A + δ. The growth-optimal decrease bet is at its upper limit only for light tails. |
| TH6 | Does Theorem B give 5% per day, and what gives a bound for every day? | No: it bounds the first day after each (re)start and, through Corollary C, the long-run count (0.05 false alarms per day); a constant null law makes the default detector alarm with certainty on day 40. Measured on later days: 4.7% on average over days 11 to 20 for i.i.d. returns, 5.25% for bold play, 28% on a day after nine calm days. With a known baseline, restarting the statistics at every day boundary gives a proved m / A for every day given the past (with the confidence-sequence baseline, δ + m / A per day of a segment, not given the past, while Theorem D's tolerance holds) (mean delay 1.06 to 1.44 times longer at 390-step days, 1.02 to 1.14 at 1440); the e-CUSUM gives 5.146% for any window of a day, but only for exchangeable nulls. Theorem D restated with a tolerance hypothesis that does not depend on the bets, plus a version with no assumption. |
| ALT1 | Quarticity stream; pre-averaging for noise | Price noise breaks the raw-return guarantee; non-overlapping pre-averaged blocks restore it. |
| ALT2 | Risk switch | Tails on par with daily volatility targeting, worse tracking, no Sharpe difference; the robust trigger beats the Gaussian one. |
| ALT3 | Robust local IQ estimator | Squaring a local variance is 95% efficient in simulation, against 86% for the kappa-corrected kernel with an estimated MAD (98% with an oracle cut-off) and 74% for untruncated RQ; one daily MAD stops converging when volatility moves within the day; on BTC and DOGE large returns come in bursts, and our version of the robust kernel is 0.33 to 0.42 times MedRQ. |
| ALT4 | Correlation monitor | Sign concordance gives a valid correlation monitor that volatility changes cannot fool. |
| ALT5 | Two-sample (rank) betting test | Ranks against a growing reference give an exactly valid detector for any return law and horizon (1.9% to 3.5% per day against 5%); ranks against a fixed reference fail like a plug-in baseline; 2.9 times faster for a change in tail shape, 4% to 19% slower for a scale change. |
| ALT6 | Sequential fixed-accuracy estimation of volatility (classical purely sequential rules against anytime-valid confidence sequences) | Truncated Chow and Robbins covers 0.94 to 0.96 at about the oracle sample for every law tested (on raw squares 0.72 to 0.78 for t3); a betting confidence sequence stopped at width 2d is valid for any law and costs 2 log(2/α) / z² = 1.92 times the oracle (1.55 with the running intersection); on BTC about 50 minutes for plus or minus 20% on volatility (140 with a guarantee); under clustering only the recent window's average is reachable, and the running intersection is invalid. |
