## E9. How tight is the guarantee, and when does it stop applying?

Script `e09_stress_guarantee.py`. All results **[SYNTHETIC]**, fixed seeds.

**Q9a and Q9b. Tightness and distribution-free validity.** Two-sided monitor with the *true* baseline (τ = 3 times the scaled MAD of the distribution, m₀ = E[min(r², τ²)] from 20 million draws), alarm level A = 7800 per side pair (each side at 2A), 4000 independent paths per row. Theorem B says P(false alarm within m steps) ≤ m / A.

| Horizon m (steps) | Bound m / A | Gaussian | Student t3 (infinite fourth moment) | Jumps (0.5% per step, 10 sd) | Price ticks (tick = 0.5 sd, 20% zero returns) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 98 (a quarter of a 390-step day) | 1.3% | 0.00% | 0.18% | 0.12% | 0.05% |
| 390 (one day) | 5.0% | 1.1% | 1.4% | 1.3% | 1.4% |
| 1950 (one week) | 25% | 12.8% | 14.3% | 13.2% | 13.7% |
| 3900 | 50% | 29.6% | 30.6% | 29.7% | 31.2% |
| 7800 (= A) | 100% | 55.9% | 55.9% | 56.4% | 56.9% |

95% Wilson intervals for the one-day row are about ±0.3 points (for example 0.8% to 1.5% for the Gaussian case).

**Reading.** The guarantee holds in every case, and the four distributions give almost the same false alarm rates, which is what "nonparametric" should mean: once returns are truncated, the bound depends only on the mean of the bounded statistic (the observed rates could in principle differ more than they do). The bound is conservative by a factor of about 4 at one day (1.1% to 1.4% against 5%) and by about 2 at the scale of A. Part of the slack is structural (the two-sided split at 2A each, and Ville's inequality ignores the overshoot), part is the betting grid. We expect (from the other horizons in the table; we did not run it) that a user could lower A by a factor of up to about 3 and still see about 5% per day *in this i.i.d. setting*, but that would no longer be backed by a proof, so the default keeps the proven level. The theory track (E11) studies where the slack comes from.

**Q9c. Volatility clustering, where the null is false.** GARCH(1,1) returns with unit unconditional variance, τ and m₀ set to their unconditional values, 2000 paths of one 1440-step day, A = 28,800 (5% budget per day). Here E[X_t] = m₀ on average, but the *conditional* mean E[X_t | past] moves above and below m₀, so Theorem B does not apply. Share of days with a false alarm:

| Null band b | α + β = 0.95 (half-life 14 steps) | 0.99 (69 steps) | 0.999 (693 steps) | 0.9999 (6931 steps) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 78% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| 0.10 | 41% | 97% | 99% | 98% |
| 0.25 | 16% | 77% | 93% | 92% |
| 0.50 | 3.6% | 34% | 60% | 69% |
| 1.00 (increase side only) | 0% | 12% | 16% | 20% |

**Reading.** Short-lived clustering (half-life 14 steps, the shortest case in the table) is absorbed by a band of 0.5. Persistent clustering is not absorbed by any reasonable static band: with a half-life of an hour or more, even b = 1 (alarm only if variance more than doubles) leaves 12% to 20% of days with an alarm. This is not a failure of the proof; it says what the monitor detects. Persistent GARCH clusters *are* changes in conditional volatility, and the monitor reports them. If the goal is to flag breaks *relative to a volatility model* rather than ordinary clustering, the null has to be stated relative to a forecast: monitor returns divided by a predictable volatility forecast. E10 tests that idea.

**Q9d. Runtime** (`e09d_profile.csv`; one shared 4-core machine, so timings are indicative). The streaming monitor processes about 100,000 returns per second for one series (10 µs per step), so a year of one-minute crypto bars takes about 5 seconds. The vectorised detector costs 0.04 µs per path-step at 10,000 paths, so a Monte Carlo study of 10,000 paths of one week takes about 1 second. The normalised robust kernel IQ costs about 0.15 µs per return. Nothing in the prototype needs compiled code at these sizes; numba was considered and not added.
