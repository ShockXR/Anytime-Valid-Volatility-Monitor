## E23. E22's release test on fresh releases: the FOMC statements and CPI releases of 2026

Script `experiments/e23_fresh_releases.py`; pre-registration `docs/preregistration_e23.md` (commit `b5c1573`, 06:35:40 UTC on 3 November 2026, committed alone with the event file `data/events/us_macro_events_2026.csv` before any Binance return of 2026 was downloaded or loaded); outputs `results/e23_*.csv` and `results/figures/e23_release_rates.png`; tests `tests/test_e23.py` [SYNTHETIC]; iteration notes `docs/iterations/E23.md`. Every market-data result is **[REAL]**; the power calculation is **[SYNTHETIC]** (a simulation whose inputs are E22's real rates).

**Question.** Every real-data result on FOMC statements and CPI releases so far (E12, E14 Q14g, E22) used the releases of 2024 and 2025, and E22 was designed after E12's answer was known. Does E22's pre-registered result hold on releases nobody in the project had looked at: the FOMC statements and CPI releases of January to October 2026?

**Summary.**
* **The pre-registered primary test fails.** [REAL] With P1 (the confidence-sequence baseline restarted at every UTC day boundary, E22's primary monitor), an up alarm came within 6 hours of 2 of the 16 FOMC statements and CPI releases of 2026 on BTC (12.5%, Wilson 95% interval 3.5% to 36.0%), against a mean matched control rate of 5.9%: a ratio of 2.1, one-sided stratified permutation p = 0.25 (Fisher cross-check 0.29; `e23_tests.csv`, family F23a). The planned power was 0.82 at E22's rates for the 17 releases expected, and 0.78 for the 16 that the data cover (`e23_power.csv`, `e23_power_actual.csv`). E12's and E22's release contrast is **not confirmed** on fresh releases.
* **No P1 secondary family passes.** [REAL] The pooled test on ETH (4 of 16, p = 0.067) and SOL (5 of 16, p = 0.039) gives Holm 0.079 for both (F23b); none of the six FOMC and CPI cells passes Holm (F23c; best SOL FOMC, 3 of 6, p = 0.079, Holm 0.48). The FOMC part on BTC was 1 of 6 statements (16.7%) against 10.3%; in 2024 and 2025 it was 8 of 16 against 10.9%.
* **The rank monitor, which is never blind, still sees the FOMC statements.** [REAL] S2 (E14's awake rank monitor) flagged 4 of the 6 FOMC statements within 6 hours on each coin, Holm p = 0.016 (BTC), 0.033 (ETH), 0.055 (SOL) in its pre-registered secondary family (F23e); its CPI cells fail on every coin. The plug-in passes one cell (ETH FOMC, 4 of 6, Holm 0.040). These are secondary results and cannot rescue the primary test.
* **On BTC, P1 was blind or recalibrating at 5 of the 16 releases, and alarmed after only 2 of the 11 it watched.** [REAL] Two BTC segments could not raise an increase alarm (τ² ≤ m_up): 10 to 15 August and 17 August to 3 October 2026 (47 days), 28% of P1's monitored time on BTC; they held the CPI releases of 12 August and 11 September and the FOMC statement of 16 September. In 2024 and 2025 one BTC release in 38 fell in a blind segment and 13 of the 26 watched BTC releases were followed by an up alarm.
* **The per-day bound is far from the observed alarm rates, as in E22.** [REAL] P1 alarmed in 37.8%, 48.0% and 46.0% of its monitored periods (a segment within one UTC day; BTC, ETH, SOL) against the 5% bound that holds under the ±25% tolerance; the tolerance fails on most real days, so these are alarm rates, not false alarm rates.

### Design

Everything is E22's and E12's code: each (coin, arm) run is `E22.job` itself, after E23 sets E12's period and event file and adds the inclusion rule. A gate run first (`e23_gate.csv`): with E12's own period and events, the switch reproduced E22's committed P1 BTC rows exactly (30 test rows of 14 columns, 38 window rows of 27 columns, the alarm and period counts; 0 mismatches), and the pooled function, restricted to one release type, reproduced E12's per-type test to the last digit for every arm, coin, type and both outcomes (`e23_pooled_check.csv`, 48 rows, 0 mismatches).

### Reading

* **The confirmatory test did not confirm.** The reading fixed in advance applies: the release contrast is not confirmed on fresh releases. With power 0.78 at E22's rates for the 16 releases available, a failure would happen with probability about 0.22 if those rates were true and about 0.63 if the true excess were half of E22's; the result says that in 2026 the contrast under P1 on BTC was smaller than in 2024 and 2025 or absent, not that it is absent. E12's and E22's FOMC result for the monitor with a per-day guarantee rests on data that were known when E22 was designed, and the README and the paper must say that the one confirmatory test did not confirm it.
* **Why, descriptively (no test attached).** Both of the mechanisms named in the pre-registration contributed on BTC: P1 was blind or recalibrating at 5 of 16 releases (in 2024 and 2025: 6 of 22 CPI and 6 of 16 FOMC releases were not watched, but only one was blind), and it alarmed after 2 of the 11 releases it watched (13 of 26 in 2024 and 2025). The blind segment of 17 August to 3 October is the failure mode of E19 on a fresh stream: a segment whose truncation level τ² falls below the increase side's threshold m_up cannot alarm upward until a down alarm ends it, and on BTC that took 47 days.
* **What still holds.** The awake rank monitor, which carries a 5% bound on the first day after each restart under exchangeability and is never blind, flagged 4 of 6 FOMC statements on every coin, with Holm p at most 0.055 in its pre-registered secondary family. This agrees with the FOMC contrast of 2024 and 2025 (E14 Q14g: 13 of 16 on BTC) but is secondary: it cannot replace the primary test, and its alarms are no more than an alarm rate of 0.70 to 0.82 per day allows. No arm shows a CPI contrast on any coin in 2026.

### Caveats

* Sixteen releases on one coin: the Wilson interval of the primary rate runs from 3.5% to 36.0%. ETH and SOL move with BTC and are not independent replications.
* The data stop on 24 October 2026, a week short of the planned end, so the FOMC statement of 28 October is out by the pre-registered inclusion rule; the power at the actual count is reported above.
* The bound of Corollary 1 is not tested by E23: on these days its hypothesis (the ±25% tolerance) fails most of the time.
* The power calculation assumes independent release and control outcomes; real alarms cluster in time, so the true power may differ.
