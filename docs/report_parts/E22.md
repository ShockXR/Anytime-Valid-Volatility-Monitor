## E22. E12's FOMC and CPI test with monitors that carry a proved per-day bound

Script `experiments/e22_guaranteed_events.py`; pre-registration `docs/preregistration_e22.md` (commit `949c1d5`, made before any part of E22 was run on real data); outputs `results/e22_*.csv` and `results/figures/e22_release_rates.png`; tests `tests/test_e22.py` [SYNTHETIC]; iteration notes `docs/iterations/E22.md`. Every market-data result is **[REAL]**.

**Question.** The referee's second report (finding J1) notes that E12's test of up alarms after FOMC statements and CPI releases, the only pre-specified primary test of the real-data work (E20), used the plug-in baseline, which has no finite-sample guarantee, while the abstract says that the FOMC result shows what the guarantee buys. Does the result hold when the monitor is one that carries a proved per-day false alarm bound?

**Summary.**
* **The pre-registered primary test passes.** [REAL] With the confidence-sequence baseline restarted at every UTC day boundary (P1), an up alarm came within 6 hours of 8 of the 16 FOMC statements on BTC (50%, Wilson 95% interval 28% to 72%), against a matched control rate of 10.9%: a difference of 39.1 points, a ratio of 4.6, one-sided stratified permutation p = 0.00017, Holm p = 0.00033 in the primary family (`e22_tests.csv`, family F22a). E12's plug-in had 9 of 16 against 13.9%, Holm p = 0.00025.
* **CPI on BTC, the other member of the primary family, also passes under P1** [REAL]: 5 of 22 against 5.9%, p = 0.0097, Holm 0.0097. It is a secondary result, and a fragile one: the release count is the plug-in's (5 of 22); what changed is the matched control rate (11.7% under the plug-in). Without the daily restart (S1) the same cell gives 3 of 22, p = 0.12.
* **The confidence-sequence arms are not blind at most releases; they are watching at more releases than the plug-in.** [REAL] P1 was monitoring with an increase side able to alarm at 80 of the 114 coin-release pairs (70%), S1 at 77 (68%), the rank monitor S2 at all 114, the plug-in at 55 (48%). One BTC FOMC statement (29 January 2025) fell in a segment whose increase side could not alarm (τ² / m_up = 0.95), in both P1 and S1; the plug-in flagged it after 15 minutes.
* **The guarantee is a property of the monitor, not of these data.** [REAL] P1 raised an alarm in 48.1% to 53.5% of its monitored periods (a segment within one UTC day; bound 5% per period under the tolerance), 0.92 to 1.15 alarms per monitored day against a long-run bound of 0.0404. On real one-minute returns the ±25% tolerance of Theorem 6.4 does not hold on most days, so these alarms are not false alarms in the sense of the theorem, and E22 does not test the bound.
* **Reproduction.** [REAL] The plug-in arm rerun through the E22 script reproduces E12's published numbers exactly (60 test rows, 114 release windows and the monitor summaries; every compared value equal, `e22_reproduction.csv`), and S2 reproduces E14 Q14g's awake-B rows exactly.

### Design

Everything except the monitor is E12's, imported from `experiments/e12_crypto_events.py`: the 16 FOMC statements and 22 CPI releases of 2024 and 2025, Binance one-minute BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT and SOLUSDT returns over the predictable intraday profile (711 monitored days from 21 January 2024), the streaming design, the outcome (an up alarm within 6 hours of the release), the matched control windows (same weekday and US Eastern clock time, at least 2 days from any event day) and the exact one-sided stratified permutation test, with E12's stratified Fisher test as a cross-check.

| Arm | Monitor | Bound proved, and its hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| P0 (reference) | E12's plug-in, `VolatilityMonitor(A = 28,800, c = 3, calibration 1440, band 0.25)` | none (nominal m / A = 5% for the first day of a segment if the baseline were the true one) |
| **P1 (primary)** | `PeriodicVolatilityMonitor(baseline="cs", delta=0.01, A = 36,000, period=1440)`, restart at 00:00 UTC (offset 0, checked), recalibration of 1440 minutes after each alarm | δ + m / A = **5% for every day of a segment**, marginally (not given the past), if the tolerance (H↑), (H↓) of `docs/math.md` Theorem 6.4 with b = 0.25 holds **to the end of that day** (Corollary 1 of `docs/theory_per_period.md`). Long run: (δ + M / A) / (1 - δ) alarms in M monitored minutes, 0.0404 per monitored day (Corollary 6.6; it applies to P1 because a restart only lowers the supermartingale of its proof) |
| S1 | the same without the daily restart, `VolatilityMonitor(baseline="cs", delta=0.01, A = 36,000)` | 5% for the **first** monitored day of each segment under the tolerance for that day (Theorem 6.4, part 1; part 4 adds P(tolerance fails) with no assumption); later days of a segment only δ + d m / A; long run as P1 |
| S2 | E14's awake rank monitor (awake-B: ranks of \|u\| among the segment's earlier values, A = 28,800, payoffs divided by 1.25, no blind period) | m / A = 5% for the first day after each restart if the segment's returns are exchangeable given the past (Theorem B.2, Remark B.3 of `docs/theory_running_tau.md`); long run 0.05 per day |

P1 runs E12's design as it stands; no substitute configuration was needed. Because recalibration takes 1440 minutes, the monitored minutes of each UTC day belong to one segment, so each UTC day is one period of Corollary 1 and P1 raises at most one alarm per day. P1 differs from P0 in three ways at once (baseline, A and the restart); S1 removes only the restart.

Every arm alarms about 10 to 47 times more often than its bound allows under its null. The monitors are not at fault: the nulls exclude volatility that moves by more than 25% (or is not exchangeable) within a day, which is the normal state of one-minute crypto returns (TH6, E19). The numbers say how far real days are from the null, not how often the monitors raise false alarms.

### Reading

* **E12's FOMC result does not depend on the plug-in baseline.** With a monitor whose false alarm probability is proved to be at most 5% in each day of a segment (under a ±25% tolerance on the conditional truncated variance up to the end of that day), the pre-registered test gives the same answer as E12: 8 of 16 FOMC statements on BTC were followed by an up alarm within six hours, against 10.9% of matched control times (Holm p = 0.00033). The FOMC contrast also holds on ETH and SOL, without the daily restart, and with the rank monitor, in every cell after Holm.
* **What it does not show.** It does not show that the bound holds on these data: the same monitor alarmed in about half of its monitored days, ten times its budget, so the tolerance fails on most real days and the bound is not in force there. It does not show that the alarms after FOMC statements mark lasting changes. And the abstract's phrase "what the guarantee buys" should be read as "the result holds for a monitor that carries the guarantee", not as evidence that the guarantee was exercised.
* **CPI.** On BTC the CPI cell passes under P1 (Holm 0.0097) where it failed under the plug-in (0.11), but the release count is unchanged at 5 of 22; the matched control rate halved. Without the daily restart it fails again (3 of 22, p = 0.12), and on ETH and SOL under P1 it does not survive Holm. CPI evidence therefore depends on the configuration; only the FOMC evidence is robust to it.
* **Blindness.** The concern that the confidence-sequence baseline is blind at most releases (E19's blind segments) does not materialise here: one release in 114 fell in a blind segment, and the confidence-sequence arms watched 68% to 70% of releases against 48% for the plug-in.

### Caveats

* The data and E12's results were known when E22 was designed; S2's result was published in E14 Q14g; S1's alarm list over 21 January 2024 to 30 June 2025 is identical to E19's committed confidence-sequence list at band 0.25 for the three coins (285, 290 and 260 alarms; checked post hoc, `results/e19_cs_events.csv`). The pre-registration fixes the configuration and the test, not the novelty of the data.
* Sixteen FOMC statements: the Wilson interval of P1's BTC rate runs from 28% to 72%. The three coins move together and are not independent replications.
* P1 changes the baseline, A and the restart together; S1 isolates the restart. The restart at 00:00 UTC falls 5 hours into the 6-hour window of FOMC statements made in US Eastern standard time (19:00 UTC); matched controls in daylight saving months start at 18:00 UTC and do not cross it. The restart only lowers the statistic.
* The bound of Corollary 1 is marginal and per segment: given the observed past (for example, no alarm yet) it is P(Gᶜ | past) + m / A, which can exceed 5%. Its hypothesis is a tolerance that real days violate; part 4 of Theorem 6.4, which charges the probability of violation, is proved for the first m steps of a segment (S1), not stated for the restarted monitor.
* The deseasonalising profile uses past days only, so it is known before each minute and the hypotheses are statements about the deseasonalised return; its accuracy affects whether the tolerance holds, not the validity of the theorem.
