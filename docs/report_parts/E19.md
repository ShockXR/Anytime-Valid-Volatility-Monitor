## E19. The guarantee on real data: null streams with the real marginal law, the confidence-sequence baseline on five coins, and the forecast-relative null

Script `experiments/e19_real_null.py`; outputs `results/e19_*.csv` and `results/figures/e19_*.png`; tests `tests/test_e19_real_null.py`; iteration notes `docs/iterations/E19.md`. Design pre-registered in `docs/preregistration_e19.md` (commit 8fbd573, before any real-data part was run); everything not in that document is marked **post hoc**. Labels: Part 1 **[REAL marginal, constructed streams]**, Parts 2 and 3 **[REAL]**, Part 4 **[SYNTHETIC]**.

**Question.** The referee report (`docs/review/referee_report_paper.md`, M1) says that the real-data evidence never exercises the anytime-valid guarantee: the main real-data runs use the plug-in baseline, which has no proof (E14, Proposition A.6), and on real days the null "volatility equals its calibration level" is false most of the time, so real alarm rates are not false alarm rates. M1 asks for (a) the confidence-sequence (cs) baseline of Theorem D as the primary real-data configuration, (b) null streams that keep the real marginal law of one-minute returns, built by permuting returns from calm periods, with block-bootstrapped streams to show what clustering does, and (c) the forecast-relative null on real data with its cost. Minor comment m32 asks for the Gaussian row of Table S2 (split clip, E11f) with the cs baseline.

**Set-up.** Data: deseasonalised one-minute returns of the five Binance coins of E6 (BTC, ETH, XRP, SOL, DOGE against USDT; `prepare` of `e06b_five_coins_monitor.py`), July to December 2025, and of the Nifty 50 (E7's `load_clean` and `deseasonalise`), sessions from January 2022 to October 2026. Detector everywhere: the default two-sided robust e-detector (X = min(r², τ²), τ = 3 times the scaled MAD, six increase and five decrease bets, Shiryaev-Roberts statistics, each side alarming at 2A). Four baselines or detectors in Part 1: (i) known baseline (τ and m0 of the pool, A = 28,800 for crypto and 7,500 for Nifty, statistics restarted at 0 after an alarm); (ii) plug-in (1440 or 750 calibration returns, same A, recalibration after an alarm); (iii) cs (`baseline="cs"`, δ = 0.01, τ from the first 25% of the window, A = 36,000 and 9,375, so that δ + one day / A = 5%); (iv) the rank detector of E14 (route B, likelihood-ratio bet family, reference set emptied after an alarm). Parts 2 and 3 run `VolatilityMonitor` itself on the real series; Part 1 runs vectorised copies that the tests check alarm for alarm against `VolatilityMonitor` and `ConformalMonitor`.

**Summary.**
* **On exchangeable streams with the real marginal law, every proved monitor stays below every bound (M1(b)) [REAL marginal, constructed streams].** Pre-registered primary endpoint (five coins pooled, 20,000 streams that permute the returns of calm days, band 0): P(false alarm within the first day) is 2.26% (95% interval 2.06% to 2.48%) for the known baseline, 0.01% (0.00% to 0.04%) for cs and 3.38% (3.14% to 3.64%) for the rank detector, against a bound of 5%; the plug-in gives 5.50% (5.19% to 5.82%), above its nominal 5%. Over five days and per monitored day the three proved monitors stay below their bounds as well (known baseline 15.8% against 25% and 0.040 false alarms per monitored day against 0.05; rank 16.7% and 0.037; cs 0.13% against 21% and 0.0002 against 0.041), for each coin, for Nifty 50, and on i.i.d. draws from the pools, where the null holds exactly. For the rank detector the permuted streams are an exact test (a short lemma in Q19a). The plug-in, which has no proof, exceeds its nominal bound wherever it is measured over more than a day: 41% within five days and 0.103 false alarms per monitored day on every coin, as E14 found on synthetic data.
* **Real dependence inside calm days breaks every null.** On stationary-bootstrap streams of the calm days (mean blocks of 30, 390 and 1440 minutes; five coins pooled) every monitor alarms within the first day on 86% to 100% of the streams at band 0 and raises 2.0 to 7.6 alarms per monitored day against 0.05; even the exact rank detector does (99%). Post hoc, these rates match the alarm rates of the real series (plug-in band 0: 4.5 to 5.5 per monitored day on the bootstrap streams, 4.9 to 5.2 on the real series). Dividing each calm day by its own volatility level (post hoc) lowers these rates by 3% to 42% for the coins but leaves them far above every bound (96% to 99% within a day, 3.1 to 4.9 alarms per monitored day; cs 68% to 86% and 1.1 to 1.8): most of the alarms come from volatility changes within calm days.
* **cs as the primary configuration on the five coins (M1(a)) [REAL]: fewer alarms, the same or better validation, at most a short delay on increases.** Pre-registered primary quantity (five coins pooled, July to December 2025, band 0.25): cs raises 0.51 to 0.53 alarms per day per coin against 0.66 to 0.70 for E6's plug-in; 78.2% of its up alarms are followed by higher volatility over the next day (day-block bootstrap 95% interval 71.3% to 84.8%) against 79.6% (73.3% to 85.3%), and 96.3% of its down alarms by lower volatility (92.9% to 98.8%) against 88.3% (82.1% to 93.0%). Differences cs minus plug-in: -1.3 points for up (-5.8 to 3.2) and +8.0 points for down (2.7 to 13.7; Bonferroni 97.5% interval for the two directions, post hoc, 2.1 to 14.5). The out-of-sample period (January 2024 to June 2025) agrees (up 80.8% against 77.9%, down 90.9% against 84.5%). When both raise an up alarm within 6 hours of each other, cs comes a median 4 to 10 minutes later (per coin and period, band 0.25); down alarms come 40.5 to 142 minutes later.
* **What the proof costs in monitoring time.** cs alarms less often, so it monitors more of the time (band 0.25, both periods together: 43% to 50% of the minutes against 29% to 34% for the plug-in; 46% to 49% against 30% to 33% in July to December 2025), and its first alarm after calibration comes later (median 686 to 1061.5 minutes against 390 to 503). **Post hoc finding:** in 1 of the 33 cs runs (BTC, July to December 2025, band 0.5) the monitor was blind to increases for the last 4.5 months, through 10 October 2025 (the day with the largest sum of squared returns in the period) and 21 November (the highest robust volatility, 3.4 times the median day): τ is fixed from the first quarter of the calibration window, and when that quarter is much quieter than the rest, τ² can fall below the band-inflated upper bound U (1 + b), after which no increase bet can win. Over all 53 cs runs of Parts 2 and 3 this happened in 5 segments of 7031 (five runs, all on BTC series), lasting 2.6 to 136 days, and never with the plug-in (0 of 9184 segments, `e19_blind.csv`). It costs power, not protection against false alarms.
* **Forecast-relative null on real data (M1(c)) [REAL]: with a GARCH(1,1) forecast the alarm rate falls to about the nominal level; with an EWMA forecast it does not.** Monitors on u / σ̂ with σ̂ from a GARCH(1,1) fitted on January to June 2025 and frozen: 0.033 to 0.054 alarms per day per coin (plug-in, band 0.25; 39 alarms in total, 0.89 of the nominal 0.05 per monitored day), and every one of the 39 was followed by a move in the flagged direction (pooled Q6d share 100% for the 21 up and the 18 down alarms; Wilson lower ends 84.5% and 82.4%). Band 0 still alarms at 2.5 (cs) to 7.4 (plug-in) times the nominal rate. With an EWMA forecast (half-life 360 minutes) the monitors alarm 17 to 89 times more often than the nominal rate and are validated less often (62% to 82%) than on the raw returns, so the EWMA forecast does not make the null true. The cost of the GARCH-relative null is that the monitor answers a different question: it flags one or two departures from the forecast per coin per month, against about 20 alarms per month on the raw returns.
* **Table S2, Gaussian row, with the cs baseline (m32) [SYNTHETIC].** The plug-in rows reproduce Table S2. With cs (A = 36,000) the one-day null rate is at most 0.05% (bound 5%) for c_down = 3, 2 and 1.5, against 5.65% to 6.10% for the plug-in. Median decrease delays rise by 24% to 142% (for example 446.5 against 247 steps at a 20% fall with c_down = 3). The split clip still helps: c_down = 2 is faster than c_down = 3 in all three cells (mean gains 52 to 93 steps); c_down = 1.5 is the fastest for falls of 33% and 50% but slower than c_down = 3 for a 20% fall (+45 steps, SE 10), where with the plug-in it was faster.

The calm days are not all at the same level: their robust volatility varies by a factor of 1.55 (SOL) to 2.12 (BTC) between the 10% and 90% quantiles, because the calm rule compares a day with its own recent past (computed from `e19_calm_days.csv`; post hoc).

**Why the permuted streams test the guarantee exactly for the rank detector.** Let Y_1, ..., Y_N be a uniformly random ordering of the pool (a multiset of N values). Given Y_1, ..., Y_t, the rest of the ordering is a uniformly random ordering of the values not yet drawn: every arrangement of them has the same conditional probability. The same holds at a stopping time T, because the event {T = t} is fixed by Y_1, ..., Y_t. So after every alarm the rest of a permuted stream is again exchangeable, given everything before it. That is the condition of Remark B.3 (`docs/theory_running_tau.md`) for the rank detector restarted at once with its reference set emptied, so its bounds hold exactly on these streams, as on i.i.d. streams; its smoothed rank p-values are then i.i.d. uniform whatever the pool, so its alarm law does not depend on the marginal law at all. For the known and cs baselines the null is only approximate: E[X_{t+1} | Y_1, ..., Y_t] is the mean of the values not yet drawn, a martingale that starts at m0 and wanders. Its largest relative departure from m0 along a stream (over the first calibration plus 20 days) has median 0.24% to 0.31% for the coins and 0.07% for Nifty, 99% quantile at most 0.77%, and maximum 1.19% (`e19_perm_drift.csv`). The i.i.d. streams (draws with replacement) are the exact check for these two baselines.

**Reading.** Keeping the real order inside blocks of calm days is enough to make every monitor alarm within a day on almost every stream, including the two whose guarantee is exact under their null (the known baseline and the rank detector). This is not a failure of any theorem: within a block E[X_t | past] moves with the real volatility, so the null is false, and the detectors do what they are built to do. Post hoc, two comparisons say where these alarms come from. First, the alarm rates per monitored day on the bootstrap streams are those of the real series (Part 2): plug-in band 0 4.5 to 5.5 on the bootstrap streams against 4.9 to 5.2 on the real series (July to December 2025), band 0.25 1.4 to 2.3 against 2.0 to 2.35; cs band 0 1.95 to 2.70 against 2.2 to 3.0, band 0.25 0.57 to 1.08 against 1.03 to 1.15 (computed from `e19_null_pooled.csv` and `e19_cs_cost.csv`). So calm days alone, resampled in blocks, produce the real-data alarm rate. Second, dividing each calm day's returns by that day's robust volatility (post hoc; 500 streams per data set and stream type, `e19_null_std.csv`, pools in `e19_pools_std.csv`) removes the differences in level between calm days and keeps the dependence within each day. For the coins this lowers the alarm rates per monitored day by 3% to 42% at band 0 (21% to 48% at band 0.25) but leaves every one far above its bound: at band 0, 96% to 99% of the streams alarm within the first day (cs 68% to 86%), with 3.1 to 4.9 alarms per monitored day (cs 1.1 to 1.8); at band 0.25, 46% to 75% within a day (cs 30% to 48%) and 0.56 to 1.69 alarms per monitored day (cs 0.33 to 0.59). For Nifty the drop is larger (20% to 83% at band 0, 39% to 87% at band 0.25): at band 0, 30% to 48% within the first session (cs 9% to 10%) and 0.46 to 0.75 alarms per monitored session; at band 0.25, 8% to 12% (cs 1.4% to 4.4%). The permuted day-standardised streams behave as before (five coins pooled, band 0, one day: known baseline 2.0%, rank 3.3%, cs 0.1%, plug-in 5.2%). So on the coins most of the bootstrap alarms come from volatility changes within calm days, over minutes to hours, not from putting days of different level side by side; on Nifty the differences in level between sessions account for a larger part, but the rates stay far above the bounds.

### Post hoc: segments in which the cs monitor cannot raise an increase alarm [REAL]

Found while checking why BTC, July to December 2025, cs, band 0.5 raised only 18 alarms (55 to 66 on the other coins): after 17 August 2025 it raised none. With `baseline="cs"`, τ is fixed from the first 25% of the calibration window and the increase side bets against m_up = U (1 + b), with U the upper confidence bound computed from the other 75%. Since X = min(r², τ²) ≤ τ², if τ² ≤ m_up then every increase factor 1 + λ (X / m_up - 1) is at most 1, so each bet's Shiryaev-Roberts statistic satisfies S_n = f_n (S_{n-1} + 1) ≤ S_{n-1} + 1 ≤ n, and no increase alarm can come within 2A = 72,000 monitored minutes (50 days), whatever the data do. In the BTC segment τ² = 5.37e-9 (the first 360 minutes were very quiet) and m_up = 1.5 U = 5.50e-9; the decrease side needed E[X] below 0.5 L = 1.58e-9, while X averaged 4.47e-9 afterwards. With the plug-in, τ and m0 come from the same window and τ² / m_up = B / (1 + b), which reaches 1 only if B ≤ 1 + b.

### What this does not show

* The permuted and i.i.d. streams make the null true by construction. They show that the bounds hold with the real marginal law of one-minute returns (zeros, ticks, heavy tails); they do not show that the null holds on real data, and the bootstrap streams show that it does not, even on calm days.
* For the known and cs baselines the permuted streams carry a small finite-population drift of the conditional mean (at most 1.2% of m0); the i.i.d. streams have none and give the same picture. No bound with drift (TH4, Proposition 2b) was computed for them.
* The calm-day rule was fixed in advance and not varied. The pools mix days whose level differs by a factor of about 1.5 to 2.1 (10% to 90% quantiles); the post hoc day-standardised run separates this from the dependence within days.
* Part 2 and Part 3 rates are real alarm rates. On real days the null is not known to hold, so these numbers are not false alarm rates, and the validated shares say only that alarms are followed by moves in the flagged direction more often than random times; as in E6e, a rate-matched ratio rule does about as well.
* Part 3 uses one forecast family fitted once (GARCH(1,1), January to June 2025) and one EWMA half-life; a better forecast would change the cost.
