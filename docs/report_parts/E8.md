## E8. A baseline that keeps the guarantee: confidence sequences

Script `experiments/e08_cs_baseline.py`; outputs `results/e08*.csv` and `results/figures/e08_*.png`. Theory in `docs/math.md`, section 6. Code: `src/avmonitor/confseq.py`, `VolatilityMonitor(baseline="cs")`. Seeds are fixed in the script; all settings were fixed before the runs.

**Question.** The false alarm guarantee of the e-detector (P(false alarm within m steps) ≤ m / A) assumes the *true* pre-change mean of the truncated squared return. The monitor plugs in an estimate from a calibration window. Can we keep a proven guarantee with an estimated baseline, and what does it cost in detection speed?

**Method.** Instead of the plug-in mean, the increase detector bets against an *upper confidence bound* U and the decrease detector against a *lower confidence bound* L. The bounds come from a *confidence sequence*: a sequence of intervals that contain the target at every sample size at once with probability at least 1 - δ. We use an empirical Bernstein sequence (Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2024) on X = min(r², τ²), with τ = 3 × scaled MAD of the first 25% of the window (a *sample split*, so that τ is fixed before the sequence starts). `docs/math.md` Theorem 6.4 proves: with no change, P(any false alarm within m monitored steps) ≤ δ + m / A. We compare three baselines:

* **plugin**: the current default, mean of X over the whole window, A = 7800 (bound 390 / A = 5% per 390-step day, proved only for the true mean);
* **margin_z = 1**: plug-in moved by one i.i.d. standard error in the conservative direction, A = 7800 (no proof);
* **cs**: confidence bounds with δ = 0.01 per side, A = 390 / (0.05 - 0.01) = 9750, so δ + m / A = 5% (proved).

[SYNTHETIC] Returns with unit volatility: Gaussian, jumps (probability 0.005 per step, size 8 sd), Student t5 (unit variance). Calibration windows of 390, 1440 and 5000 steps. One-sided rates use one statistic at level A. "Either side" uses the monitor's rule (each side at 2A; for cs, δ = 0.005 per side, so the total budget is again 5%). 10,000 paths per null cell (Monte Carlo standard error at most 0.22 points), 2000 per change cell.

**Reading.**
* The cs baseline keeps its guarantee with a wide margin: at most 0.27% false alarms per day against a proved 5%, and the confidence bound itself failed on at most 0.08% of paths against an allowed 1%. Figure: `e08_false_alarms.png`.
* With 10,000 paths, the plug-in at 390 steps is at 4.5% (Gaussian and jumps) and 4.9% (t5) on the increase side, and 5.07% (t5) for either side, so it sits at the bound without exceeding it beyond Monte Carlo error. E4d's 5.4% (2000 paths, standard error about 0.5 points) is about 1.6 combined standard errors above our Gaussian value, so the "break" it reported was not confirmed. Over the 500 pre-change steps of Q8b (bound 500 / 7800 = 6.4%), the plug-in increase side at 390 steps under t5 alarmed on 7.0% to 7.5% of 2000 paths in three rows that share their calibration windows (`e08b_change.csv`), 1 to 2 standard errors above the bound. So the plug-in is close to its bound at short calibration, with no proof either way.
* margin_z = 1 brings every rate to 1.5% or less, but it is on the wrong side of the truth on 17% to 19% of paths: it is a heuristic safety margin, not a guarantee.

**Reading.** The adaptive rule delivers what it promises: the bound ends up 16% (w = 0.3) or 26% (w = 0.5) above the truth in every scenario, the guarantee holds (at most 0.12% false alarms), and the window lengthens by 8% to 13% for fat-tailed t5 returns relative to Gaussian ones. The spread of lengths is narrow (the 10% and 90% quantiles lie within 13% below and 17% above the median), so in these i.i.d. settings the rule behaves almost like a fixed length chosen for the target width. Its value is that the user states the precision they want instead of a length, and the proof covers the data-dependent stop (`docs/math.md`, 6.6).

### Q8d. Is the width the bound's fault? [SYNTHETIC]

Upper and lower bound divided by the true mean of X, at the end of a fixed window, δ = 0.01: a normal-approximation interval (no finite-sample guarantee), the best bound any constant bet can give for the true law ("best constant bet", a benchmark that knows the distribution, not a strict limit for every betting bound), and our empirical Bernstein bound (median of 500 paths).

| Returns | Calibration (sequence length) | Upper: normal approx. | best constant bet | ours | Lower: normal approx. | best constant bet | ours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian | 390 (292) | 1.189 | 1.290 | 1.368 | 0.811 | 0.785 | 0.741 |
| Gaussian | 1440 (1080) | 1.098 | 1.139 | 1.171 | 0.902 | 0.881 | 0.869 |
| Gaussian | 5000 (3750) | 1.053 | 1.072 | 1.078 | 0.947 | 0.934 | 0.931 |
| Jumps | 390 (292) | 1.194 | 1.301 | 1.377 | 0.806 | 0.780 | 0.731 |
| Student t5 | 390 (292) | 1.211 | 1.320 | 1.426 | 0.789 | 0.761 | 0.704 |
| Student t5 | 5000 (3750) | 1.059 | 1.080 | 1.082 | 0.941 | 0.926 | 0.920 |

**Reading.** At 1440 steps and above our upper bound is within 3.5 points of the best constant bet; at 390 steps the upper bound gives away 7.6 to 10.6 points. Most of the width is intrinsic: the truncated squared return has a standard deviation 1.4 to 1.6 times its mean and a range of 4.7 to 6.5 standard deviations, and even the best constant bet needs a bound 29% to 32% above the mean from 292 observations. A tighter construction (the "hedged capital" betting sequence) could recover roughly the gap to that benchmark.

### Q8f. Volatility clustering [SYNTHETIC]

GARCH(1,1) with α = 0.05, β = 0.94 (persistence 0.99, unit unconditional variance), 1440-step windows, 4000 paths. Inside one window the conditional truncated variance varies by a median factor of 2.6 between its 10% and 90% quantiles. Each bound is checked against its own target (`docs/math.md`, Theorem 6.2): the cs bounds against the bet-weighted average of the conditional means, the i.i.d. standard-error intervals against their plain average.

| Interval | Nominal miss | Upper bound misses | Lower bound misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| cs, δ = 0.01 (checked at every step) | 1% | 0.05% | 0.18% |
| cs, δ = 0.05 (checked at every step) | 5% | 0.85% | 1.50% |
| plug-in + 1.00 i.i.d. SE | 15.9% | 18.9% | 10.9% |
| plug-in + 1.64 i.i.d. SE | 5% | 6.7% | 2.1% |
| plug-in + 2.33 i.i.d. SE | 1% | 1.6% | 0.2% |

**Reading.** The confidence sequence stays valid under clustering, as the theorem says. The i.i.d. standard error is too small for the upper bound (1.6% misses at a nominal 1%), which is the side that protects the increase detector. The effect is moderate here, but it has no bound. The bigger issue is elsewhere: with conditional variance moving by a factor of 2.6 inside a window, the monitoring null "never above the calibration average" is simply false under clustering, whatever the baseline (E9 Q9c). The confidence sequence protects against estimation error, not against this.

### Q8e. Real data: Bitstamp BTC/USD, July to December 2025 [REAL]

The validation is E5c's: robust volatility in the 1440 minutes after each alarm divided by the robust volatility of its calibration window. **Reading.**
* The cs baseline cuts alarms by 16% to 20% at every band (156 to 131, 126 to 101, 92 to 76). Almost all of the cut is in decrease alarms (86 to 56, 53 to 35, 33 to 22); increase alarms change little. The control row shows that the larger A alone changes almost nothing (126 alarms at both A values; the split moves from 73/53 to 74/52 and the increase hit rate from 81% to 77%), so the cut comes from the baseline.
* Hit rates of decrease alarms rise (67% to 82% with no band, 90% to 97% with band 0.25, 97% to 100% with band 0.5), and the remaining decrease alarms are followed by larger drops (median 0.45 against 0.65 at band 0.25). Hit rates of increase alarms stay at 77% to 83%. All variants beat random times (about 50%, E5c control). A rate-matched ratio rule does about as well (E6e), so this check shows that the alarms are not noise, not that they add precision.
* On this data the cs baseline acts like an extra, data-driven band: at band 0 its null values sit 16% above and 18% below the plug-in, close to a fixed band of 0.16 to 0.18, and its alarm count and decrease hit rate fall between those of the plug-in with band 0 and with band 0.25. Its value is the proof, not better alarms.
* A side effect worth knowing: with recalibration after every alarm, the monitor spends most of the time calibrating (it monitors only 15% of the minutes with the plug-in and no band). Fewer alarms mean more monitored time (29% for cs). The adaptive rule needed longer windows here (median 2455 minutes for w = 0.3, against 1233 steps for synthetic Gaussian data with a slightly different split), so it monitors only 25% of the time. We did not check why; a likely reason is that the truncated squared deseasonalised returns are more dispersed relative to their mean than in the synthetic laws, or drift within the window.

**Caveats.**
* The synthetic studies are i.i.d. within each regime, where the theorem's null holds exactly. Under clustering the null itself is false (Q8f, E9), and no baseline fixes that.
* The guarantee is conservative: about 0.3% or less against 5%. Part of that is Ville's inequality (with the true baseline the plug-in is already at about 1% to 2%, E9 Q9a), part is the width of the bound. The proved budget cannot be spent more aggressively without losing the proof.
* One asset, one venue, six months for the real run; "followed by higher volatility" is a sanity check, not ground truth (see E5c). The adaptive rule's target w = 0.3 was fixed from the synthetic design before the real run, not tuned on it.
* The betting (hedged capital) confidence sequence, a union bound over a grid of truncation levels, and a running τ (`docs/math.md`, 6.4) were not implemented.
