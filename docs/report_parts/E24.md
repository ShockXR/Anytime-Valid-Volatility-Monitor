## E24. A proved guard against blind segments of the confidence-sequence baseline, and a forward pre-registration

Theory `docs/theory_blind_guard.md`; code `src/avmonitor/guard.py`; script `experiments/e24_blind_guard.py` (parts `theory`, `selection`, `synthetic`, `real`, `e19blind`, `power`, `future`, `figs`); outputs `results/e24_*.csv` and `results/figures/e24_*.png`; tests `tests/test_guard.py` [SYNTHETIC]; forward pre-registration `docs/preregistration_e24_future.md` with `data/events/us_macro_events_e24_future.csv`; iteration notes `docs/iterations/E24.md`. Labels: [PROVED], [NUMERICAL], [SYNTHETIC], [REAL]; every real-data result here is **post hoc and exploratory**, because the data were seen by E19, E22 and E23 before the guard was designed.

**Question.** In E23 the primary monitor P1 could not raise an increase alarm for 28% of its monitored time on BTC, and 3 of the 16 releases of 2026 fell in such blind segments. E19 had proposed a guard (discard a blind calibration window and calibrate again) and left it unproved, because the kept windows are selected by their bounds. Can the guard be proved, what does it cost, does it help, and what test on releases nobody has seen would settle the release question?

**Summary.**
* **The guard is proved, and the naive version is not.** [PROVED] A blind segment (τ² ≤ m↑) is decided when calibration ends, so a guard can act on it. Discarding blind windows and calibrating again breaks the per-window coverage of the kept window's bounds (Proposition 3: under a law inside the tolerance relative to the kept window, a window before the K-th is kept exactly when its upper bound fails), but spending the error budget over the windows tried, δ_1 + ... + δ_K ≤ δ, gives P(the kept window's bounds fail) ≤ δ (Theorem 1), and with it Theorem 6.4, Corollary 6.6 and the per-day Corollary 1 unchanged, with the tolerance taken relative to the kept window (Theorem 2). That null depends on the guard's tuning through the selection; the tolerance relative to each of the K windows, (H*), is a selection-free sufficient condition that implies both the guarded and the unguarded hypotheses. A fallback to the rank monitor after K blind windows keeps δ + m/A under a mixed hypothesis (Theorem 3). [NUMERICAL] For P1's window the construction triples the failure probability with three windows and no spending (7.6 × 10⁻⁴ against 2.5 × 10⁻⁴; `e24_selection.csv`), which is still below δ = 0.01; the naive guard exceeds δ on this law only from about 40 windows, and with no cap on the number of windows the kept window's bound fails with probability 1.
* **Blind is not the only failure: near-blind windows are just as deaf.** [PROVED, SYNTHETIC] With ρ = τ²/m↑ ≤ 1.0105 P1's increase side cannot alarm within a day whatever the data; with a step in volatility of 4 or 6 after the τ part (ρ ≈ 1.2 and 1.03) the unguarded P1 and the guard with E19's criterion (η = 0) detected a later doubling within a day in at most 16% of paths, the guard with η = 0.25 in 86.5% and 100% (`e24_synthetic.csv`).
* **Recommended guard: K = 3, budgets δ/2, δ/4, δ/4, η = 0.25, rank fallback** (chosen among the guarded monitors from the synthetic results, before any real-data rerun; η = 0.25 and K = 3 were set before the synthetic run, after a scratch look at P1's real BTC windows; `docs/theory_blind_guard.md` 6.1). [SYNTHETIC] No alarm in the first day or any of the first four days of a null segment in 800 paths of any constant-law design, for any monitor (upper limit 0.0037; bound 0.05); detection within a day 86.5% to 100% where the unguarded P1 had 0% to 0.25%; cost: one extra calibration day only where the first window is near-blind.
* **On real data the guard removes every blind segment at a small cost.** [REAL, post hoc] It acted only on BTC (3 windows in 711 days of 2024 to 2025, 5 in 297 days of 2026; no fallback needed); no monitored minute of the guarded P1 was blind, against 28% for P1 on BTC in 2026. All 8 discarded windows began on a weekend: the τ part was a quiet weekend stretch, in three 2026 windows with a median absolute return of exactly one price tick, and the sequence part was 5 to 1010 times more volatile. In E19's diagnostic (no daily restart) the guard turned BTC's 83% blind time (band 0.5, one segment of 136 days) into 0%, with one discarded window; the unguarded rows reproduce E19's exactly (`e24_e19_blind.csv`).
* **The guard does not rescue E23's primary test, and nothing here is confirmatory.** [REAL, post hoc, exploratory] On BTC in 2026 the guarded P1 had 4 of 16 releases with an up alarm within 6 hours against a matched control rate of 8.9% (raw p = 0.056; FOMC 3 of 6), P1 2 of 16 (p = 0.25), the rank monitor S2 6 of 16 (p = 0.007). These data were seen and the guard was designed after E23 failed.
* **A forward test is pre-registered** (`docs/preregistration_e24_future.md`, commit `22f1988`, 08:46 UTC, 3 November 2026, before any return after 24 October 2026 was loaded). By a rule committed before the power part ran (a scratch power check of S2 alone, 2,000 replications, preceded it; `docs/iterations/E24.md`, iteration 4), the primary test is **S2, BTC FOMC statements and CPI releases pooled, 25 October 2026 to 1 June 2027 (11 planned releases), power 0.82 at the pooled 2024 to 2026 rates** [SYNTHETIC; planning inputs REAL]; 0.60 at the 2026 rates, 0.37 at half the pooled excess. The script refuses to run before 1 June 2027, on data that end earlier, or while any release is unverified; after the round 10 review it also refuses when a scheduled FOMC row has been deleted, when a release is marked verified before it happened, or when the pipeline no longer reproduces E23's committed rows (a disclosed amendment that only adds refusals).


### Theory [PROVED] (`docs/theory_blind_guard.md`)

| Statement | Content |
|---|---|
| Lemma 1, Proposition 1 | A segment is blind when τ² ≤ m↑ = (1 + b) Ū_T: every increase factor is at most 1, so the increase side cannot alarm within 2A - 1 monitored steps of a (re)start, and with P1's daily restart (carry 0) never. The event is in F_T (T, the end of calibration, is a stopping time), so the guard can act on it. |
| Proposition 2 | Near-blind, ρ = τ²/m↑ ≤ 1 + η: the exact floor d_min(ρ) on the steps to an increase alarm after a (re)start, over all data, attained by X ≡ τ², and a bound log(1 + λ_max η) on the expected log growth per step under any law. For P1 no increase alarm is possible within a day when ρ ≤ 1.0105; d_min = 1503 steps at ρ = 1.01, 371 at 1.05, 91 at 1.25 [NUMERICAL, `e24_near_blind.csv`, figure `e24_near_blind.png`]. |
| Theorem 1 | Retry guard with error spending: windows k = 1, ..., K with budgets δ_k, Σδ_k ≤ δ; discard a near-blind window and calibrate again; then P(the kept window's bounds fail given the past) ≤ Σ_k δ_k P(window k is tried) ≤ δ, for any discard rule decided at the end of each window. |
| Proposition 3 | The selection is real: under a law inside the tolerance relative to the kept window (not relative to the discarded ones), a window before the K-th is kept exactly when its upper bound fails. With no cap on the number of windows (E19's proposal) the kept window's bound fails with probability 1; with a cap K and the same δ for every window it fails with probability 1 - Π_k(1 - p(δ)), about K times one window's, which for P1's window exceeds δ only from about 40 windows (p(δ) is about 1/20 of δ/2). Spending makes the bound hold for every law and every K. |
| Theorem 2 | Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.6 of `docs/math.md` and Corollary 1 of `docs/theory_per_period.md` hold unchanged with δ the total budget, T the end of the kept window and the tolerance relative to the kept window's sequence part. The kept window depends on δ_k, the bets and η, so this null depends on the guard's tuning, and the guarded and unguarded monitors test different nulls; the tolerance relative to each of the K windows, (H*), is a selection-free sufficient condition that implies both (part 4), and it holds under a constant conditional law from the end of the first τ part (covers a step in volatility inside it). |
| Proposition 4 | Time-out after D periods: per-segment bounds unchanged; long-run bound grows by δ/D alarms per period. |
| Theorem 3 | Fallback to E14's awake rank monitor after K near-blind windows: P(alarm within m monitored steps of a segment) ≤ Σδ_k + m/A, where the cs branch needs the tolerance relative to the kept window and the rank branch needs exchangeability of the returns after the fallback given the past; the per-period and long-run bounds carry over (the latter by a per-segment optional-stopping argument). |
| Remark 6 | Not specific to the guard: Corollary 1 bounds a period of a segment; for a fixed calendar day the bound is δ E[segments started by then] + (m + κ)/A, which under the tolerance in every segment is at most δ(1 + (δ + d m/A)/(1 - δ)) + (m + κ)/A on the d-th day (P1: 5.05% on day 1, 6.02% on day 25, 9.05% on day 100) [PROVED]; whether a null attains more than δ + (m + κ)/A on some day is open. |
| Corollary 2 | Not specific to the guard: under the tolerance in every segment, E[N_M] ≤ (δ + ((m + κ)/A) E\|𝒫_M\|)/(1 - δ), with 𝒫_M the periods holding one of the first M monitored steps; so the long-run share of alarmed periods (a ratio of expectations) is at most (m + κ)/(A(1 - δ)) = 4.04% for P1. This, not the 5% of Corollary 1, is the bound for the per-day shares of E22, E23 and this experiment. |

### Synthetic experiments [SYNTHETIC] (`e24_synthetic.csv`, `e24_synthetic_paths.csv.gz`, figure `e24_synthetic.png`)

Eleven-day streams of one-minute returns; the standard deviation doubles at a uniform minute of day 9; 800 paths per design; five monitors on the same paths: U, P1 unguarded (`PeriodicVolatilityMonitor(baseline="cs", delta=0.01, A = 36,000, period=1440)`, band 0.25); G, guarded, K = 3, η = 0, keep; GR, as G with the rank fallback; GE, K = 3, η = 0.25, rank fallback (the recommendation); R, E22's rank monitor S2 (A = 28,800). Null endpoints use each monitor's first segment that starts after the pre-change law has settled (after the first 360 minutes for the steps and `frozen`, after the first day for `drift8_gauss`). * **Null rates.** In the seven designs whose law is constant after the first τ part (the five steps, `frozen`, `calm_btc_perm`) no monitor raised an alarm in the first day, or in any of the first four days, of its first null segment: 0 of 800 paths (one-sided 95% upper limit 0.0037) against the bound δ + m/A = 0.05 (R: 0 of 710 to 800 paths with a null segment; in `frozen` only 4, because its first segment starts before the frozen share falls). Expected alarms per path before the change are bounded by 0.27 to 0.31 (cs) and 0.37 to 0.42 (R); observed 0. The bounds hold with a wide margin, as in E19 and E22; the designs cannot rank the monitors by false alarms.
* **Where the hypotheses fail.** In `drift8_gauss` the first window is not near-blind (ρ ≈ 1.6) and is kept; every cs monitor alarms on the first monitored day of every path, which the theorem allows because its tolerance fails relative to that window. In `calm_btc_days` the level changes at day boundaries and every monitor alarms early (first-day share 0.78 U, 0.76 guarded, 0.62 R; 4.4 to 8.1 alarms per path): alarm rates, not false alarm rates.
* **What the guard buys.** Blindness disappears (blind share 0 for every guarded monitor in every design), and with η = 0.25 so does near-blindness. The rank monitor detects faster in eight of the nine designs (median 25 to 30 minutes against 48 to 64 for GE in step6, step8, frozen and drift8; 25 against 144 in step2), and slower under Student t3 returns (127 against 115); it is never blind. The guarded P1 keeps the tolerance-based per-day bound.

### Proposition 3 in numbers [NUMERICAL] (`e24_selection.csv`)

Under the construction of Proposition 3 (b = 0.25, η = 0, so a window is kept exactly when U_T < 0.8):

| Configuration | One window | 3 windows, δ each | 3 windows, halving (Theorem 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1's window (n_cs = 1080, δ = 0.01; 400,000 replications per level) | 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ | 7.6 × 10⁻⁴ | 1.9 × 10⁻⁴ |
| illustration (n_cs = 150, δ = 0.3 per window; 200,000) | 0.0139 | 0.041 | 0.0063 |
| the same through `GuardedVolatilityMonitor` (4000 paths) | | 0.043 (s.e. 0.0032; formula 0.041; 0.0426 with that run's own p = 0.0144) | |

The selection multiplies the failure probability by the number of windows tried. On this law the naive guard with three windows still stays below δ (7.6 × 10⁻⁴ against 0.01; 0.041 against 0.3), because one window's sequence fails far less often than its budget δ/2; it exceeds δ from about 40 windows (26 in the illustration), and with no cap the kept window's bound fails with probability 1. Spending keeps the failure probability below δ for every law and every K. The round 10 review recomputed p(δ) with 1,000,000 replications per level (2.59 × 10⁻⁴ for P1's window, so K = 39; δ/2 is 19, 23 and 32 times p(δ) at δ = 0.01, 0.005 and 0.0025; `docs/review/round10_scripts/outputs/r10_selection.txt`).

### Reading

The retry guard with error spending is a proved fix for blind segments of the confidence-sequence baseline: it keeps every bound of the unguarded monitor, under a hypothesis stated relative to the window it keeps, and on real BTC data it removes all blind time at the cost of about one calibration day per four months. The selection that E19 worried about is real (Proposition 3: with no cap it makes the kept window's bound fail with probability 1, and with a cap it multiplies the failure probability by up to the number of windows) and is paid for by splitting δ, not argued away. The hypothesis is relative to the window kept, which depends on the guard's tuning; the tolerance relative to every window tried is a selection-free condition under which both the guarded and the unguarded bounds hold. The guard does not, on the data already seen, restore the 2024 to 2025 release rates in 2026, and the rank monitor remains the stronger release detector; whether either result holds on new releases is now a pre-registered question with a fixed date.

### Caveats and what failed

* All real-data comparisons here are post hoc on seen data; the guard was designed after E23's failure, partly from E23's BTC windows.
* The synthetic null checks cannot separate the monitors (no alarm at all); the bounds are far from tight in these designs.
* The near-blind margin η = 0.25 and K = 3 were fixed before the synthetic run (commit `4c294e4`), after a 3-path smoke run and after iteration 1's scratch look at P1's 2026 BTC windows with ρ ≤ 1.2; other values were not explored. The design `frozen` and the null-segment filter were committed with the results.
* The first version of the per-period summary counted an empty first period (segments starting on a day boundary); fixed in the summary before any number was reported (iteration 2).
* Iteration 1's scratch look at P1's blind BTC windows attributed them all to tick-level τ parts; the committed account on the guarded monitor's windows (8 windows, two mechanisms) is in `e24_real_guard_windows.csv`, and the synthetic design `frozen` was chosen after that first look.
* The forward test's power rests on planning rates that include 2024 and 2025, when release rates were higher than in 2026; its power at the 2026 rates is 0.60. The official schedule pages could not be fetched; the 2027 dates rest on search results that index them.
* Whether an adaptive null exceeds δ + (m + κ)/A on a fixed calendar day (Remark 6) is open; the earlier sketch of such a null was withdrawn after the round 10 review, because a null cannot end good segments quickly.
* The round 10 review (`docs/review/independent_review_round10_e24.md`) found Proposition 3 (a) false as stated and its reading overstated, the example of Remark 3 false, and eleven smaller points; all were corrected in the theory note, the code's docstrings, the tests and this part (iteration 5 of `docs/iterations/E24.md`). No experiment was rerun and no number in the tables changed.
