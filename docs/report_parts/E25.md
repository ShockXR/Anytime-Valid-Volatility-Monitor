## E25. An anytime-valid version of the forward release test: an e-process over releases

Pre-registration `docs/preregistration_e25_sequential.md` (commit `30804bb`, 3 November 2026, before any Binance return after 24 October 2026 23:59 UTC was loaded and before any code for this test was written); proof `docs/theory_sequential_events.md`; script `experiments/e25_sequential_events.py` (parts `theory`, `strata`, `gates`, `power`, `posthoc`, `status`, `figs`); outputs `results/e25_*.csv` and `results/figures/e25_*.png`; tests `tests/test_e25.py` [SYNTHETIC]; iteration notes `docs/iterations/E25.md`. Labels: [PROVED], [SYNTHETIC], [REAL]. Every real-data result here is either a gate on known data or **post hoc and exploratory**: no fresh stratum is complete yet, and the fresh test has not been run.

**Question.** E24's forward test F24a is a fixed-sample test: it may be run once, on 1 June 2027, nobody may act on the data before then, and its sample cannot be extended after a failure without spending more than its 5%. Can the same question (do the rank monitor's up alarms come more often within six hours of FOMC statements and CPI releases than at matched control times?) be asked with a test that may be checked after every release and continued without an end date, at level 5% for every look; what does that cost in power; and what does it do on releases already seen?

**Design** (fixed in the pre-registration; implemented without change). Each release i, in order of release time, gets a stratum: its own window [t, t + 6 h) and up to eight control windows at the same US Eastern weekday and clock time 1 to 4 weeks before and after it, chosen from the calendar only (rule 1); a candidate is dropped if its day is less than 2 days from a release day, if it is earlier than the stream start plus 1440 + 360 minutes, or if its window is not inside the data (rule 2), or if an earlier release already kept it (rule 3); a stratum enters the test when the data reach t + 28 days + 6 hours and no event-file row within 30 days still says "scheduled" (rule 4), and strata enter in order. With Z_i the release window's outcome (an up alarm of S2, E14's awake rank monitor as in F24a), n_i controls kept, S_i windows with the outcome and p_i = S_i/(n_i + 1), the e-value is e_i(ψ) = ψ^{Z_i}/(1 - p_i + ψ p_i) and the e-process is M_j = (1/4) Σ_{ψ ∈ {2,4,8,16}} Π_{i≤j} e_i(ψ). Under H0 (the n_i + 1 outcomes of each stratum are exchangeable given the earlier strata), M is a nonnegative martingale with mean 1, so P(M_j ≥ 20 for some j) ≤ 5% whenever anyone looks and whatever stopping rule is used (Theorem 1 of the theory note, by Ville's inequality). Primary E25a: BTC, FOMC and CPI pooled, reject at the first complete stratum with M ≥ 20. Secondary E25b: ETH and SOL, M ≥ 40 each. F24a runs on 1 June 2027 unchanged; if either test is used to call the contrast confirmed, the report must say that two pre-registered 5% tests were run on the same releases (at most 10% together).

**Summary.**
* **Every gate passes.** [PROVED, checked exactly] The conditional mean of e(ψ) under H0 is exactly 1 in all 216 cases (n = 0 to 8, every S, every ψ of the grid; rational arithmetic). [REAL, known data] S2's alarms before each of three cut-offs are identical whether or not later data exist; E25's code reproduces the committed S2 alarms and the release outcomes of E22 and E23 exactly (33 checks, 0 mismatches). [SYNTHETIC] Under H0 the chance that M ever reaches 20 within 200 releases is 2.8% to 4.6% over eight laws (limit 5.46%).
* **The fresh test cannot be computed yet.** Its first stratum, the FOMC statement of 28 October 2026 with 3 controls (4, 18 and 25 November 2026), completes on 26 November 2026 at 01:00 UTC; the status part refuses before any download until then, and was not run.
* **Power: less than F24a on 1 June 2027, more after it.** [SYNTHETIC; planning inputs REAL] At the pooled 2024 to 2026 planning rates M reaches 20 by 1 June 2027 with probability 0.54 (F24a: 0.82), by 1 January 2028 with 0.89, by 1 January 2029 with 0.99; at the 2026 rates 0.28, 0.63, 0.88 (F24a: 0.60); at half the pooled excess 0.16, 0.36, 0.58 (F24a: 0.37); under the null 0.011, 0.017, 0.021. Estimating each stratum's control rate from at most 8 controls lowers the crossing probability on 1 June 2027 by 29% to 40% relative to an oracle that knows it (0.75, 0.41, 0.27).
* **Post hoc, on seen data** [REAL, exploratory]: on 2024 to 2025, M crosses 20 on BTC at the 9th of 36 strata (12 June 2024) and ends at 4.3 × 10⁵; on 2026, BTC's M ends at 6.7 after 15 strata and never reaches 20 (ETH 13.1, SOL 15.9).
* **Two findings about the fixed design** (not changed; reported): 30 pairs of control windows of different strata overlap by 30 minutes, and rule 3 leaves 19 of the 23 planned strata with controls unevenly placed around the release.

Gate 5 in detail (shares of 20,000 sequences of 200 strata on which M ever reaches the level):

| Law of the strata under H0 | ≥ 10 (bound 0.10) | ≥ 20 (bound 0.05) | ≥ 100 (bound 0.01) |
|---|---|---|---|
| n_i + 1 i.i.d. Bernoulli(5%) windows, release one of them at random | 0.059 | 0.028 | 0.005 |
| the same at 10% | 0.062 | 0.030 | 0.006 |
| the same at 20% | 0.065 | 0.032 | 0.007 |
| the same at 40% | 0.074 | 0.036 | 0.008 |
| all or nothing (S = 0 or n + 1): every e-value is 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| exactly one window with the outcome, at a uniform position | 0.058 | 0.032 | 0.007 |
| S uniform on 0..n + 1, release position uniform | 0.070 | 0.034 | 0.007 |
| S chosen from the earlier strata (1 after a release without the outcome, n after one with it) | 0.094 | 0.046 | 0.009 |

The bound is not attained by these laws, because M overshoots the threshold at the stratum where it crosses; the last law, in which S depends on the past (allowed by H0, which needs no independence between strata), comes closest. The mean of M after 1 and after 10 strata is 1 within two Monte Carlo standard errors in every law; after 200 strata the sample mean is below 1 in most laws, as expected for a test martingale whose mean is carried by rare very large values.

### The strata of the planned calendar (`e25_strata_planned.csv`)

The forward event file's released and scheduled rows (scheduled treated as released, for planning only) without the statement of 27 January 2027, stream start 25 October 2026 00:00 UTC, no data end: 23 strata to December 2027 (8 FOMC, 15 CPI), 3 to 8 controls each, 125 of 184 candidates kept. Rule 2 drops 6 candidates before the stream start plus 1800 minutes and 37 within 2 days of a release; rule 3 drops 16 (two each from eight strata). Eastern day arithmetic follows the clock across daylight saving changes: the controls of the CPI release of 10 March 2027 are at 13:30 UTC before 14 March and 12:30 UTC after. The first stratum (FOMC, 28 October 2026, 18:00 UTC) keeps 4, 18 and 25 November 2026 at 14:00 EST (its earlier candidates precede the stream; 11 November is one day from the CPI release of 10 November) and completes when the data reach 25 November 2026 20:00 EST (four weeks after the release, plus 6 hours), that is **26 November 2026, 01:00 UTC**. With the 2028 placeholders of F24a's plan, 41 strata enter by early January 2029 (`e25_power_calendar.csv`).

### Power (pre-registration, section 9) [SYNTHETIC; planning inputs REAL] (`e25_power.csv`, `e25_power_growth.csv`, figure `e25_power.png`)

F24a's planning model: the release window has the outcome with S2's BTC rate of its type, each control with the matched control rate of its type, all independently (pooled 2024 to 2026: FOMC 77.3% against 16.5%, CPI 37.5% against 12.5%; `results/e24_future_planning_inputs.csv`). Strata from E25's rules on F24a's planning calendar; a stratum enters when complete, assuming each event-file row is updated when its release happens. 10 strata enter by 1 June 2027 (F24a counts 11 releases: the CPI release of 12 May 2027 is in F24a's sample, but its stratum completes on 10 June), 21 by 1 January 2028, 39 by 1 January 2029. Seed 25,000; 20,000 replications per scenario (Monte Carlo s.e. at most 0.0035).

(F24a's values are those of `results/e24_future_power.csv`, S2 pooled; the script checks them.) The expected log e-value per release, exact under the model, is largest at ψ = 8 under the pooled rates (0.31 per release; 0.62 for an FOMC statement, 0.13 for a CPI release) and at ψ = 4 under the 2026 rates (0.17; a CPI release contributes −0.007) and at half the excess (0.085). If only the best ψ contributed, M ≥ 20 would need that ψ's product to reach 80 (the average over four ψ pays a factor 4), a log growth of 4.4, that is about 14 releases at the mean growth of the pooled rates; the simulated median, 10, is earlier because the other products also contribute and the path fluctuates.

So at F24a's date E25 has about two thirds of F24a's power at the planning rates and less than half at the smaller effects, as the pre-registration expected; its crossing probability passes F24a's power on 1 June 2027 on 14 October 2027 at the planning rates (0.835), on 25 November 2027 at the 2026 rates (0.610) and on 6 January 2028 at half the excess (0.376) (`e25_power_curve.csv`), and it is the only one of the two that can continue.

### What E25 shows and what it does not

* It shows [PROVED; checked exactly and by simulation] that the pre-registered e-process has the guarantee claimed: under H0 the chance that it ever reaches 20 is at most 5%, for any number of looks and releases; in the simulated laws it is 2.8% to 4.6% within 200 releases.
* It shows [REAL, known data] that the pipeline that will compute it is causal and reproduces E22's and E23's committed outcomes exactly.
* It shows [SYNTHETIC] that the price of looking at any time is power at a fixed date (0.54 against F24a's 0.82 on 1 June 2027 at the planning rates) and that the gain is the ability to continue (0.89 by 2028, 0.99 by 2029).
* It does **not** yet test anything on fresh data: the first fresh stratum completes on 26 November 2026. The post hoc paths are illustrations on seen data.
* A crossing would say that release windows differ from their controls under this monitor; it would not say which release type carries the effect, how large it is (M is not an effect size), or that the monitor's own 5% bound holds on these data (it does not; E22, E23).

### Findings about the fixed design (reported to the coordinator; the design is implemented as committed)

1. **Overlapping windows.** Rule 3 makes every window belong to one stratum, as the pre-registration says, but windows of different strata can overlap in time: a CPI control at 08:30 ET and an FOMC control at 14:00 ET on the same Wednesday share the half hour 14:00 to 14:30 ET. The planned calendar has 30 such pairs (`window_overlaps`, printed by the `strata` part), and in the post hoc period two release windows overlap (CPI and FOMC on 12 June 2024). Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 hold under H0 as stated; the overlap weakens the case for H0 in the sense of Remark 1 of the theory note, because an earlier stratum's outcome then carries partial information about a later stratum's control. A fix would extend rule 3 to drop a candidate whose window overlaps any window of an earlier stratum.
2. **Unbalanced controls.** Section 6 says that controls on both sides of the release remove a linear drift to first order. After rules 2 and 3 the controls of 19 of the 23 planned strata are not placed symmetrically (mean offset of the controls from the release not zero; rule 3 gives shared candidates to the earlier release, so later strata lean towards controls after the release), and 2 strata have controls on one side only (the FOMC statement of 28 October 2026 and the CPI release of 11 August 2027). A linear drift is removed to first order only in the 4 balanced strata.
3. **Wording.** Section 9 says "with n_i from rule 4's assignment"; the assignment is rules 1 to 3 of section 4 (rule 4 is completion). The code uses rules 1 to 3.

### Caveats and what failed

* The planning entry times assume that the event file is updated at each release; a later update delays strata, never changes them.
* The mean of M after 200 simulated strata was first reported as a validity check and replaced by the means after 1 and 10 strata (a test martingale's sample mean over long horizons is dominated by rare values).
* A test of the status refusal assumed that complete data make a stratum complete; the code was right (a scheduled row within 30 days also blocks), the test was corrected. An empty release list broke a pandas mask; fixed (iteration 1).
* E12's `alarm_frame` prints division-by-zero warnings for S2 alarms with a zero baseline scale, in a diagnostic column that E25 does not use.
* The official schedule hosts cannot be fetched from this machine; the forward calendar rests on E24's event file, verified through search results that index the official pages.
